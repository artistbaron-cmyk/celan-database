# Celan Root Relationship Laboratory

**Status:** Exploratory working document — nothing in this file is canon  
**As of:** 2026-09-16  
**Input inventory:** inherited, promoted, and the 86 user-approved created roots  
**Canon boundary:** a form enters the dictionary or Expressions index only through separate explicit approval

## Purpose

Celan now has enough internally meaningful roots for unexpected combinations to develop from the language itself. The purpose of this laboratory is to investigate those combinations without pretending that every mathematically possible pairing is a real word.

The central question is not:

> What English word can these two roots be made to translate?

It is:

> What would an Ohnoshan speaker literally hear, where would that relationship recur in Ohnoshan life, and how might repeated use turn it into terminology, metaphor, humor, slang, or idiom?

The [Created Celan Roots Register](16_created_roots_register.md) defines the approved semantic centers. This laboratory may combine those roots with the entire active Celan inventory, but it may not silently broaden or rewrite them.

## Status ladder

| Stage | Label | Meaning |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | Mechanical possibility | The forms can be placed together, but no useful reading has been established. |
| 1 | Recoverable literal construction | Speakers can infer the root relationship in a specific context. This does not yet justify a headword. |
| 2 | Recurring Ohnoshan referent | The construction names an object, condition, practice, or event likely to recur in the world. |
| 3 | Conventional metaphor | Repeated literal use plausibly supports a stable figurative reading. |
| 4 | Register-bearing expression | The form functions as slang, jargon, insult, humor, idiom, or nationally characteristic speech. |
| 5 | Approved canon | The user has explicitly approved the form, meaning, pronunciation, examples, register, and data-layer placement. |

Everything in this document remains below Stage 5.

## Relationship test

Every candidate should answer these questions:

1. **Literal parse:** What does each root contribute?
2. **Order:** Why is this root first and the other second? Would reversing them change the reading?
3. **Lived bridge:** What recurring Ohnoshan object, event, institution, hazard, joke, or habit connects the roots?
4. **Semantic pathway:** How does the literal construction acquire any figurative meaning?
5. **Register:** Is it ordinary vocabulary, technical jargon, slang, humor, insult, ritual speech, or a national preference?
6. **Collision:** Does an approved word already express the concept?
7. **Distortion:** Does the combination misuse either root or encode a false theory?
8. **Frequency:** Would speakers repeat it often enough to lexicalize it?
9. **Sound:** Is the unsmoothed form speakable? What optional euphonic form might emerge?
10. **Placement:** If approved, should it become a headword, expression, transparent phrase, specialist label, or merely an illustrative possibility?

## Worked model: Narngar

### Form

**Narngar** /nahr-n-gahr/  
Possible euphonic form: **Narangar** /nah-rah-n-gahr/

### Literal parse

- `Nar`: nausea and the associated emetic event
- `Ngar`: weapon or purpose-built harmful implement
- Literal reading: **nausea weapon** or **weapon designed to induce nausea or vomiting**

The order matters. `Ngar` is the concrete category being specified: it is a weapon, and nausea identifies its effect. A reversed experimental form, `Ngarnar`, would more naturally foreground nausea caused by a weapon or a weapon-sickness state; it should not automatically be treated as a synonym.

### Lived Ohnoshan bridge

A `Narngar` could be a chemical, botanical, magical, acoustic, sensory, or technomantic device used to incapacitate without immediately destroying the target. Such devices are plausible in perimeter defense, riot control, caravan protection, creature deterrence, sabotage, or nonlethal capture.

The literal object must become culturally familiar before the slang leap feels earned.

### Semantic pathway

```text
Nar + Ngar
    ↓
nausea-producing weapon
    ↓
something overwhelmingly revolting or punishing to experience
    ↓
slang evaluation: “violently disgusting”
```

Possible slang scene:

> “That drink is a narngar.”  
> Meaning: “That drink is violently disgusting.”

### Candidate classification

- Literal technical noun: Stage 2 candidate
- “Violently disgusting” slang: Stage 3–4 candidate
- Current canon status: **Unapproved**

### Questions before approval

- Does Ohnosha actually use nausea-inducing devices often enough to establish the literal bridge?
- Is the slang universal, military, adolescent, Mechuman, Jasaran, or another voice?
- Does the compound refer only to purpose-built devices, or can a natural substance be called a `Narngar`?
- Is `Narangar` merely optional euphony, or has one register lexicalized it?

### Working distinctions produced by this pass

- **General compulsion:** canonical `Tharbel`; **care weaponized into control:** experimental `Savangar`.
- **Institutional/social corruption:** canonical `Ruvan`; **corruption centered on usable supplies or false reserves:** experimental `Ruvansul`.
- **Lineage or kin-thread:** canonical `Zhir`; **biological or measured lineage information carried in blood:** experimental `Ndemdava`.
- **General intelligence or wisdom:** not `Raku`; **exceptional analytical processing:** possible slang reading of `Kheluraku` or `Rakukhelu`.
- **Any advanced settlement:** not automatically `Rakutash`; **city functioning as an integrated computational organ:** possible `Rakutash`.
- **General code-switching:** not automatically `Miraxar`; **protective code-switching used to manage exposure or embarrassment:** possible `Miraxar` use.
- **Dry humor:** needs a deadpan performance bridge; **humor beside death:** the more immediate literal pathway of `Dumahav`.

## Second intentional pass — ancestry without imprisonment

This pass changes the standard of judgment. A developed Celan word does not need to remain a permanent arithmetic sum of its roots. The roots supply ancestry and an initial field of association. Repeated Ohnoshan use may then narrow, redirect, moralize, joke with, or partially obscure that ancestry.

The required discipline is therefore not permanent literalness. It is a recoverable history:

```text
root ancestry
    -> recurring Ohnoshan scene
    -> repeated comparison or social use
    -> conventional living meaning
    -> possible slang, technical, regional, or ironic residue
```

At the end of that history, an ordinary speaker may know the current meaning without consciously analyzing the roots. The relationship only needs to remain culturally believable when its history is examined.

### What this pass changes

The candidates no longer divide simply into “literal enough” and “too figurative.” They divide according to whether their semantic history is already recoverable:

- **Strong living pathways:** `Fekkel`, `Miraxar`, `Savangar`, `Kesngar`, `Rethgora`, `Ndemdava`, `Rakutash`, `Ruvansul`, and `Kheluraku`.
- **Stronger with a different destination:** `Dumahav` as death-facing or gallows humor rather than dry humor.
- **Valuable opacity awaiting history:** `Plantar` as possible slick-talker slang.

This is not an approval list. It is a map of where each word’s cultural work would have to happen. The most successful forms preserve a residue of their roots without requiring speakers to perform an etymology every time they talk.

## Fresh generative pass — let life get hold of the roots

This pass begins from a different creative posture. It does not ask whether a compound can already defend itself as a literal dictionary definition. It asks what might happen after generations of Ohnoshans joke with it, misuse it, professionalize it, shorten its original context, and hand it to children who no longer know why it began.

These are semantic openings, not verdicts. Some may become headwords, some expressions, some specialist terms, and some nothing at all. Their purpose is to reveal cultural territory that a mechanically literal pass would never reach.

| **Morlkel** /mohrl-kehl/ | `Morl` stone, endurance, and unchanging weight + `Kel` speech | **Stone-delivered speech: flat, immovable, and giving away nothing through tone.** | It can be the voice used under interrogation, the blunt speech of someone finished negotiating, or the delivery that makes a joke land several breaths late. ### Shape of the pass

This pass deliberately permits several kinds of growth at once:

- **Metonymy:** `Remakel` moves from face-speech to public voice; `Gorakel` moves from joint-speech to body language.
- **Social invention:** `Miratal` names a culturally recognizable act of taking embarrassment away from someone else.
- **Moral reversal:** `Lianxar`, `Tlangar`, and `Vaarngar` turn valued things—truth, intimacy, and peace—into possible instruments of concealment or control.
- **Embodied cognition:** `Davelkes`, `Khelumura`, `Teshdava`, and `Nardava` let the body describe kinds of knowing rather than merely anatomy.
- **Historical residue:** `Dumadava` and `Xilohm` could exist specifically because Ohnosha contains records and relationships severed by fractured reality.
- **Living-language drift:** `Dovzhir`, `Mbukel`, and `Darkel` are understandable from their roots, but their proposed meanings are social conventions rather than literal objects.

The pass is successful even if only some forms survive. Its purpose is to expose the semantic neighborhoods Celan can inhabit once roots are allowed to acquire memory, attitude, and history.

## Order experiments

Root order is not cosmetic. A pair should be tested in both directions before either form is recommended.

| Pair | First possible reading | Reversed possible reading | Reason to keep separate |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Narngar` / `Ngarnar` | A weapon specified by nausea-producing effect | A nausea state specified as weapon-caused | Object versus outcome/state |
| `Sulngar` / `Ngarsul` | A weapon made from or enacted through resources | A resource specifically intended for weapons | Coercive tactic versus military supply |
| `Kesngar` / `Ngarkes` | A weapon aimed at attention or perception | Detection, recognition, or awareness of weapons | Weapon type versus perceptual act/system |

These readings are hypotheses. Actual Celan compound order must be checked against the relevant established families and spoken examples before approval.

## Productive contradiction

Some of the richest slang comes from roots whose literal meanings strain against each other.

### Ruvansul

`Sul` is usable supply. `Ruvan` is uncontrolled organic decay. A neutral dictionary definition such as “rotten usable resource” is internally unstable. That instability may be precisely what makes the compound valuable as civic slang:

- reserves recorded in a ledger but spoiled in storage;
- power technically allocated but unavailable;
- medicine that has degraded beyond safe use;
- a public promise backed by unusable material.

The contradiction can motivate a figurative meaning—**a resource that exists officially but has already failed materially**—without changing either root's approved center.

### Savangar

`Sava` ordinarily carries care. `Ngar` carries purpose-built harm. Their collision could name care systems deliberately weaponized against the person they claim to treat. The moral force is powerful, but approval would require tight boundaries so that painful, unwanted, or unsuccessful care is not automatically called a weapon.

## Historical and phonological relationships

Not every relationship must be a transparent compound.

`Sura` animal fur and `Ruma` human hair already establish one approved model: related sound can preserve a plausible historical relationship while the modern forms remain separate roots. Future investigations may ask whether other pairs show:

- consonant shift across early contact communities;
- vowel change marking a bodily or material distinction;
- fossilized shortening in high-frequency speech;
- taboo replacement;
- professional reshaping;
- national preference later absorbed into universal Celan;
- sound-symbolic development;
- semantic narrowing from a once-broader ancestor.

Such histories require explicit creator decisions. Similar sound alone is not proof of common origin.

## Rejection and parking log

Candidates belong here even when they fail. Record why:

- roots do not actually support the gloss;
- compound duplicates an existing headword;
- metaphor depends only on an English idiom;
- root order is incoherent;
- form encodes false anatomy, science, or history;
- useful only once in lore;
- transparent phrase does not need lexical status;
- attractive sound has no semantic bridge;
- slang lacks a literal or cultural pathway;
- candidate would erase an established distinction.

Rejected or parked compounds remain non-canonical and must never be imported into the dictionary merely because they appear in this laboratory.

## Promotion workflow

1. Select one relationship or a tightly connected cluster.
2. Audit current headwords, expressions, examples, and root meanings.
3. Establish the literal Ohnoshan bridge before proposing figurative use.
4. Test root order and cadence in actual speech.
5. Decide whether the result is a headword, expression, jargon, phrase, or rejection.
6. Define register, scope, variants, and at least two natural scenes.
7. Present a bounded proposal to the user.
8. Integrate only after explicit approval.

The laboratory exists to encourage bold play while keeping canon precise.
