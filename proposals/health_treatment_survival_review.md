## Executive finding

Domestic and Sensory Life 1 already gives Celan an effective vocabulary for experiencing bodily trouble: illness, pain, wounds, fatigue, hunger, thirst, itching, shivering, swallowing, washing, and resting. National Expansion 1 adds the herbal remedy and restorative healer. Organized Conflict adds poison. Post-Fracture Mechanics adds timeline sickness and specialized stabilization.

The remaining gap is not basic bodily sensation. It is the organized language of **medical reasoning, medical intervention, physiological crisis, rescue, and survival procedure**.

This revised proposal therefore contains two passes and three working registers:

- **Pass 1 — Health, Diagnosis, and Treatment:** 23 proposed headwords.
- **Pass 2 — Exposure, Rescue, and Survival Procedure:** 21 proposed headwords.
- **Common register:** short forms for frequent household and conversational use.
- **Technical register:** longer compounds whose extra structure carries real clinical or institutional precision.
- **Transparent phrasing:** meanings expressed through ordinary syntax when a separate long derivative would add no useful distinction.

The approved and integrated dictionary effect is:

- **44 additional headwords**
- **44 additional displayed senses**
- **2 new primitive roots:** `Ndem`, blood; `Sava`, ordinary health care
- **0 added senses to existing headwords**
- **0 new dictionary categories**

These figures were verified after rebuilding the Canon Dictionary.

## Register and length decision

Celan can support an everyday word and a technical term in the same semantic area, provided they are not empty synonyms. The shorter form names what ordinary speakers commonly need; the longer compound exposes distinctions that matter to healers, institutions, records, and emergencies.

| Layer | Preferred size | Function in this proposal | Example |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Common | Usually 1–3 spoken syllables before productive inflection | Frequent household, travel, and conversational health language | `Sava` care; `Mavbal` infection; `Vaarshan` rescue |
| Technical | Often 3–6 spoken syllables when each component adds precision | Diagnosis, transmissibility, exposure pathology, evacuation, quarantine, and triage | `Keshenath` diagnosis; `Mavbalshaneth` infectious; `Shentalzhael` triage |
| Phrase | No new headword unless repeated use proves lexicalization necessary | Predictable actions and states already clear from known words | “give a warning”; “put under quarantine”; “move toward recovery” |

This is a frequency-and-precision tendency, not a new grammar rule or absolute syllable limit. A long everyday form should be shortened or phrased. A long technical form may remain when its internal structure helps speakers identify the exact condition or procedure.

## Pass 1 semantic separations

- `Mavkes` is evidence of a condition; `Keshen` is the examination process; `Keshenath` is the concluded diagnosis.
- `Sava` is the ordinary umbrella for care or treatment. `Talzhael` is narrower: a defined intervention or planned therapeutic course. Speakers can use `Sava` without knowing or emphasizing the clinical method.
- `Savaen` is ordinary caring for or treating. `Talzhaelaen` means administering or carrying out the defined intervention.
- `Savin` and `Savor` belong to the short everyday family because patient and clinic are frequent conversational roles and places; the earlier long candidates `Talzhaelin` and `Talzhaelor` are withdrawn.
- `Talzhael` is intervention applied by a caregiver; `Morzhael` is recovery occurring in the patient.
- `Zhaelphel` is generic medicine. `Fennilphel` remains specifically herbal remedy.
- `Zhaelshen` is one measured dose. A dosage schedule or regimen should be expressed transparently as an ordered `Belwek` or `Shara` of `Zhaelshen` doses unless later scene testing proves that phrase inadequate.
- `Mavkrez` is internally generated illness heat. It is not environmental heat, a heatwave, anger, or the surface burning sensation `Rekaivaen`.
- `Mavbal` is biological infection. It is not poison `Mavmor`, organic rot `Ruvan`, or illness in general `Mav`.
- `Mavbaleth` means presently infected. `Mavbalshaneth` means capable of transmission; an infection need not always be transmissible between people.
- No separate long verb or adjective is proposed for recovery. Ordinary speakers may say that someone “moves toward recovery,” “is in recovery,” or has “completed recovery” with `Morzhael` and existing grammar. The earlier candidates `Morzhaelaen` and `Morzhaeleth` are withdrawn.
- `Jorzhael` is immediate limited first aid, not a complete course of treatment.
- `Mavmorzhael` counters poison specifically and is not a synonym for all medicine.

## Pass 1 primitive-root review

`Ndem` is proposed for blood. Blood is a fundamental bodily substance required in injury, birth, medicine, ritual, kinship metaphor, and ordinary description. Existing `Dren` and `Dro` mean water or liquid but would reduce blood to generic water-like flow. `Mor` means bodily or material substance but cannot identify blood. `Shal` carries life, but “life-liquid” would over-spiritualize an ordinary anatomical substance. A compact independent primitive is therefore justified.

`Sava` is proposed for ordinary health care or treatment. Existing `Zhael` supplies restoration and renewal, while `Tal` supplies directed giving or application; together they form the precise technical `Talzhael`. That compound is honest but unnecessarily formal as the word a parent, traveler, herder, or neighbor would repeat in daily speech. `Phel` means soothing relief and would exclude care that is painful, preventive, diagnostic, or merely supportive. `Nku` means hidden nurturing but would blur medical care with comfort and mentorship. A compact two-syllable primitive is therefore justified as a high-frequency common-register center.

Approval would authorize only `Ndem`, `Ndemaen`, `Sava`, `Savaen`, `Savin`, and `Savor`, not automatic blood or care families.

## Pass 2 semantic separations

- `Rekrathlor` is an immediate crisis requiring prompt action. `Rathlor` remains danger generally.
- `Velmav` is dangerous systemic cold illness. `Velaivkor` is localized frozen-tissue injury. `Riv` remains the symptom of shivering.
- `Rekmav` is severe illness caused by environmental heat. `Mavkrez` is fever generated by illness.
- `Kadromav` is a bodily water deficit. `Drorav` remains thirst, `Kadreneth` remains ordinary dryness, and `Vindrenaen` remains deliberate drying of food or material.
- `Kesrin` is individual wakeful consciousness. It does not replace `Sel` mental clarity, `Kes` noticing, `Vethor` sleep, `Vethrin` dream, `Rin` essential self, or `Ohmvok` collective consciousness.
- `Kakesrin` is involuntary unconsciousness while alive. It is not sleep, confusion, sedation, spiritual absence, or death.
- `Vaarshan` centers the endangered being and the achieved movement into safety. `Thalwelvar` centers organized removal from a dangerous place, including preventive evacuation before anyone is trapped.
- `Mavbalxar` is preventive health separation. It is not imprisonment, privacy, military siege, or generic isolation.
- `Shalkav` does not imply victory, success, comfort, or absence of injury. It means that life or essential function continues.
- `Kavaen` is the short ordinary verb “to endure, last, or survive.” Unlike `Shalkav`, it can also describe an object, system, or institution that remains functional through damage. Context supplies the living-survival reading.
- `Vaarbelwek` is an executable sequence for safety. It is narrower than public policy and more operational than a general path or instruction.
- `Shentalzhael` prioritizes limited treatment; it is not diagnosis, allocation generally, or a judgment of anyone's social worth.

The earlier long verb `Rathlorkelaen` is withdrawn. Ordinary Celan can say “give/speak a `Rathlorkel`” without lexicalizing a predictable derivative. The earlier `Mavbalxaraen` is also withdrawn; speakers can “put [someone or something] under `Mavbalxar`.” These phrases preserve the technical nouns while keeping common clauses lighter.

## Cadence review

The candidates were tested in short diagnostic sequences rather than only in isolation:

```text
keshenaen zhaelral savin; aen zhaelral keshenath mavbal.
The healer examines the patient; the healer states a diagnosis of infection.

savaen zhaelral savin; talzhaelaen zhaelral mavbal ser zhaelphel.
The healer cares for the patient; the healer treats the infection with medicine.

rinaen savin an savor; var savin an morzhael.
The patient is in the clinic; the patient moves toward recovery.

kes kesvar savin kakesrin ser velaivkor.
The patrol finds the patient unconscious with frostbite.

thalwelvaraen lorbel welrin; dor zhaelral savin an mavbalxar.
The government evacuates the citizens; the healer puts the patient under quarantine.

kavaen Ilin ser vaarbelwek.
We survive by following the safety protocol.
```

These are proposal-stage cadence tests, not canonical example sentences. They deliberately contrast common `Sava` language with technical `Talzhael` language and use transparent phrasing for recovery and quarantine. The sequence keeps short anchors such as `Kes`, `Ser`, `Mav`, `Vel`, `Vaar`, `Sava`, and `Kavaen` around longer clinical compounds.

## National applicability without national ownership

All proposed meanings are universal. The national scenarios expose different concentrations of use:

- Jasara and Zarithan foreground dehydration, heat illness, dosage under scarcity, evacuation, and water-sensitive recovery.
- Nivveil foregrounds hypothermia, frostbite, rescue, unconscious travelers, and casualty transport.
- Valkeldor and Marakor foreground trauma care, bleeding, first aid, triage, and organized emergency response.
- Verdalris foregrounds herbal remedies, salves, infection, biological treatment, and recovery.
- Pelagae foregrounds pressure injury, water rescue, quarantine at ports, and evacuation from submerged structures.
- Mechuma foregrounds clinical measurement, manufactured medicine, emergency protocols, medical facilities, and mass coordination.

No proposed entry therefore receives a national origin or national-usage field.

## Decisions requiring explicit review

1. **Approve or reject `Ndem` as the primitive for blood.** Approval would authorize `Ndem` and `Ndemaen` only.
2. **Approve or revise the `Mavkes → Keshen → Keshenath` distinction** among symptom, clinical examination, and diagnosis.
3. **Approve or reject `Sava` as the common-register primitive for health care.** Approval would authorize only `Sava`, `Savaen`, `Savin`, and `Savor`.
4. **Approve the register distinction between `Sava` and `Talzhael`:** `Sava` is ordinary care or treatment; `Talzhael` is a defined therapeutic intervention or course.
5. **Approve the treatment/recovery distinction:** care or treatment is applied; `Morzhael` is recovery occurring in the body. The predictable long recovery verb and adjective remain phrases rather than headwords.
6. **Confirm that generic medicine should be `Zhaelphel`, while `Fennilphel` remains herbal remedy.**
7. **Confirm that dosage schedule remains a transparent construction rather than a separate headword.**
8. **Approve `Mavbal` as infection through harmful living growth, without redefining illness, poison, or rot.**
9. **Approve the fever/heat-illness contrast:** `Mavkrez` is fever; `Rekmav` is severe environmental heat illness.
10. **Approve the rescue/evacuation distinction:** `Vaarshan` centers a being brought to safety; `Thalwelvar` centers organized removal from a place.
11. **Approve `Kesrin` as individual wakeful consciousness before approving its absence and return forms.**
12. **Confirm that quarantine may apply to people, animals, objects, or areas when the purpose is infection control, while its ordinary verb remains a phrase.**
