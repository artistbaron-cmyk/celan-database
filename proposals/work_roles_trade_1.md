# Work Roles and Trade — Proposal 1

**Status:** Approved and integrated  
**Scope:** Universal Celan vocabulary  
**Target layer:** Stewardship, apprenticeship, skilled occupations, commercial acquisition and transfer, cross-border trade, and ownership  
**Integration state:** Integrated as `Work Roles and Trade 1` under `LX-WRT1-0001` through `LX-WRT1-0014`.

## Design commitments for this batch

1. Existing occupational and commercial words remain primary. New terms fill distinctions rather than renaming established specialists.
2. These meanings are universal overall, so national origin and national usage should remain blank if approved.
3. All entries use existing dictionary categories. No new category is proposed.
4. No new primitive root is proposed. The batch reuses the complete inherited and approved root inventory.
5. `Ral` is used only where the whole form names a stable entrusted or practiced role. Approval would not make it an unrestricted English-style “person who” suffix.
6. The unsmoothed form remains the headword. Any listed alternate is only the optional euphonic form.
7. Approval would authorize exactly the listed headwords and meanings, not their automatic derivative families.

## Meaning boundaries and contrast tests

| Pair | Required distinction |
|---|---|
| `Ral` / `Droral` | A `Ral` can steward any shared responsibility. A `Droral` specifically stewards water or water infrastructure. |
| `Ral` / `Lorbelral` | A steward manages something entrusted to their care. A public official holds civic or administrative office. A person may be both, but neither title implies the other. |
| `Pralilan` / `Ilan` | Every apprentice is a learner, but an `Ilan` may be an ordinary student or follower without supervised occupational practice. |
| `Pralral` / `Mekral` | An artisan makes skilled useful or artistic objects across materials. A `Mekral` specializes in constructed mechanisms, repair, and technical systems. |
| `Krenral` / `Pralral` | A smith is defined by work with metal. An artisan is the broader skilled-maker category and may work in fiber, wood, clay, stone, metal, glass, or mixed materials. |
| `Mormekral` / `Mekral` | A mason builds and repairs with structural units, especially prepared stone. A mechanic or technician works with mechanisms and connected technical systems. |
| `Lormekral` / `Mekral` | An engineer designs, analyzes, and directs a complex system. A mechanic or technician constructs, operates, diagnoses, or repairs it. The same person may perform both roles. |
| `Shenral` / `Kavral` | A scholar produces or interprets knowledge through systematic study. An archivist preserves records or objects whether or not they conduct original research. |
| `Kelvekral` / `Lorvekral` | A courier is responsible for the entrusted message or small delivery. A driver is responsible for controlling the vehicle. A courier may travel without operating any vehicle. |
| `Sharalorin` / `Lormahr` | A navigator is a person and can work on land, sea, air, underground, or in space. `Lormahr` is the skill of seamanship or ocean navigation. |
| `Anvanesh` / `Tal` | Buying specifically acquires through payment. `Tal` remains the broader act of giving, taking, offering, receiving, or transferring. |
| `Thalvanesh` / `Vanesh` | Selling is one directed commercial action. `Vanesh` remains commerce or trade generally, without choosing the buyer's or seller's perspective. |
| `Lorzhiranshan` / `Anvanesh` | Importing moves traded value into a jurisdiction across its border. Buying changes who acquires it. A government may import goods it did not purchase, and a citizen may buy locally without importing. |
| `Lorzhirthalshan` / `Thalvanesh` | Exporting moves traded value out of a jurisdiction. Selling changes who transfers it for payment. Goods can be exported before final sale or sold without crossing a border. |
| `Ianath` / direct possessive grammar | `Ianath` names the abstract established condition or right of ownership. `Ian`, `Anen`, `Eshen`, and `Velian` continue to mark whose particular object is being discussed. |
| `Ianath` / relational possession | Ownership concerns controllable property. It must not be extended to partners, kin, communities, sacred bonds, or other inalienable relationships merely because English permits possessive phrasing. |

## Root-reuse review

No new primitive is justified.

- **Steward:** established `Ral` already supplies caretaker, watcher, and entrusted vigilance. Promoting it as a standalone noun is more direct than adding `Bel`; the governed object or context identifies what the steward manages. `Wel` was rejected because a steward may manage a private household or bounded institution, not only a communal resource.
- **Apprentice:** `Ilan` already supplies student/follower, while `Pral` constrains the word to supervised practical craft or occupational learning. A new learner root would duplicate established `Ilan`.
- **Artisan, smith, and mason:** `Pral` supplies skilled craft; `Kren` supplies metal; approved `Mormek` supplies prepared masonry material. `Morlpral` was rejected for mason because it is already the headword for floor and would create a misleading analysis.
- **Engineer:** `Lor` contributes design and directed control, `Mek` constructed systems, and `Ral` an entrusted practiced role. `Mekral` alone was rejected because it already means mechanic or systems technician and does not consistently imply design responsibility.
- **Scholar:** `Shen` contributes disciplined measurement and evaluation. `Shenral` is already used with the translation “scholar” in three approved Built Spaces examples, making this an attested latent headword rather than an arbitrary new formation.
- **Courier:** `Kel` contributes message or ordered language, `Vek` carrying, and `Ral` entrusted responsibility. `Lorvekral` was rejected because operating a vehicle is not what defines a courier.
- **Navigator:** `Shara` supplies route and `Lorin` a person who guides. `Lormahr` remains the narrower ocean-navigation skill; `Eshlorral` remains the pilot who controls a vehicle.
- **Buy and sell:** `Vanesh` already supplies formal trade. `An` marks acquisition inward and `Thal` marks transfer outward. `Van`, the market-place root, was rejected because buying and selling can occur away from a shop or physical market.
- **Import and export:** `Lorzhir` supplies the jurisdictional border; `An` and `Thal` distinguish inward and outward direction; `Shan` supplies passage or transfer. Adding `Vanesh` was rejected because the resulting everyday administrative verbs would be unnecessarily long and the approved lexical definitions already constrain them to trade.
- **Ownership:** direct-possession grammar supplies `Ian`, while established `-ath` names the possession as an established or enduring state. `Bel` was rejected after review because ownership does not require a binding or institutional system and the root is already highly productive. `Vok` was rejected because ownership is not necessarily unity and its established verbal family carries bond-breaking associations.

## Cadence and lineage review

- The generic role stays maximally compact as `Ral`; `Pralral`, `Krenral`, and `Shenral` retain two strong beats for their occupational distinctions.
- The role family is audible without becoming mechanically identical: `Pralilan` uses the established learner, `Sharalorin` uses the established guide, and only genuinely entrusted/practiced roles use `Ral`.
- `Krenral mormekral shenral` gives metal, masonry, and scholarship distinct consonant attacks while preserving a recognizable occupational rhythm.
- `Anvanesh` and `Thalvanesh` form a short, learnable inward/outward commercial pair.
- `Lorzhiranshan` and `Lorzhirthalshan` are longer because they encode jurisdiction, direction, and transfer, but each remains four audible rhythmic units rather than a six-syllable trade compound.
- No African-influenced onset is added cosmetically. Existing lineages remain active in forms such as `Nkural`, `Mbalan`, and other approved vocabulary; future extensions should arise where their meanings genuinely support the work.
- Every letter remains audible. In `Lorzhirthalshan`, the root boundaries preserve both the final `r` of `Zhir` and the `th` sequence of `Thal` according to the speaker's accepted Celan realization.

## Collision and family audit

- Exact headword scanning found no collision for any of the fourteen primary spellings or six optional euphonic forms. `Ral` is an established active morpheme but not currently an app headword; the proposal promotes rather than reinvents it.
- `Shenral` has three approved example attestations but no current dictionary headword. Admission would give those examples the standalone scholar/researcher entry they already presuppose.
- `Mormekral` is built from established `Mormek` worked or masonry stone and does not reinterpret `Morlpral` floor.
- `Anvanesh` and `Thalvanesh` do not change the displayed noun/root meaning of `Vanesh`; they are separate directed verbs.
- `Ianath` does not alter possessive grammar or turn relational possession into ownership.
- Optional euphonic forms are variants only and would not increase the headword count.

## Risks requiring explicit review

1. **Seven of the nine occupations use `Ral`, including standalone `Ral`.** Each use is semantically tied to an entrusted or established practiced role, but approval should not be interpreted as making `Ral` universally productive for any activity or person. The standalone noun formalizes its established caretaker sense.
2. **`Shenral` formalizes a word already present in approved examples.** This is a promotion into the dictionary, not a replacement or exposure of a hidden root description.
3. **`Anvanesh` and `Thalvanesh` depend on directional perspective.** The first is acquisition into possession; the second is commercial transfer outward. Neither should collapse into generic trade.
4. **The import/export pair omits `Vanesh` for speakability.** Their definitions must remain explicitly trade-bound so they do not become generic verbs for crossing a border.
5. **`Ianath` must preserve Celan's relation-not-ownership principle.** It applies to property rights and controllable resources, not people, kinship, sacred relation, or belonging. `-ath` marks ownership as established; it does not make every possessive phrase permanent.
6. **`Sharalorin` covers the route-determining person, not every guide, driver, or pilot.** Vehicle control and route determination may be performed by the same person but remain distinct functions.

## Approval boundary

Approval of this proposal would authorize exactly **14 universal headwords** and **six optional euphonic variants**. It would not authorize:

- canonical integration before explicit approval;
- any national origin or national usage field;
- a new dictionary category;
- an unrestricted occupation suffix;
- automatic words for master, guild, worker, employer, employee, wage, profit, tax, debt, rent, loan, inventory, or shopkeeper;
- a generic border-crossing meaning for the import/export pair;
- an ownership interpretation for relational or inalienable possession;
- or replacement of any existing displayed definition.
