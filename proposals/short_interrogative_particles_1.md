# Celan Short Interrogative Particles — Proposal 1

**Status:** Approved and integrated  
**Date:** 2026-09-17  
**Scope:** Six short content-question words for the Question Frame

## Recommendation

Celan can create six short question words without turning six ordinary roots into additional dictionary meanings:

| English cue | Approved Celan | Pronunciation | Historical relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| what? | **mu** | /moo/ | A worn-down relative of `Mor`, matter or physical substance |
| when? | **nu** | /noo/ | A worn-down relative of `Nor`, time or temporal flow |
| why? | **zhe** | /zheh/ | A worn-down relative of `Zhir`, thread, lineage, or continuity through time |
| how? | **sha** | /shah/ | A worn-down relative of `Shara`, path, route, or way of proceeding |
| where? | **lo** | /loh/ | A locative form historically associated with place-bearing `-or` and possibly `Lor`, directional focus |
| who? | **ri** | /ree/ | A worn-down relative of `Rin`, essence, identity, or true self |

These are a **closed grammatical family**, not six new productive roots. They do not form compounds, accept lexical suffixes, or acquire ordinary noun meanings merely because they appear in the dictionary.

## The Question Frame

### Yes-or-no question

```text
ra + complete clause?
```

`Ra` alone continues to ask whether a proposition is true.

### Content question with an optional explicit tail

```text
ra + clause with missing information + [short question word]?
```

The opening `ra` announces a question. When used, the optional closing word tells the listener what kind of answer is requested. Omitting it remains grammatically correct when context supplies the missing answer category.

| Intended question | Proposed Celan frame |
|---|---|
| What do you notice? | `ra kes Ya mu?` |
| When does the caravan leave? | `ra thalshanaen sharavok nu?` |
| Why does the keeper preserve the record? | `ra kav kavral kavdava zhe?` |
| How does the mechanic repair the machine? | `ra zhaelpralaen mekral kalmek sha?` |
| Where does the friend go? | `ra var ser lo?` |
| Who goes to the city? | `ra var an Varthas ri?` |

The current source example `Ra var ser?` remains a grammatical compact model when context supplies place. Teaching material should place it beside the more explicit `ra var ser lo?` so learners understand that `lo` is available but not compulsory.

## Philosophy of the frame: opening a gap and naming its anchor

The frame has a coherent Ohnoshan philosophy:

```text
ra + what is known + what is missing?
```

`Ra` opens an unresolved space. The clause then lays down the event, participants, and conditions the speaker already knows. The final short particle names the kind of information that would anchor or complete the thought.

The listener therefore experiences a content question in four movements:

1. **`Ra` opens the gap.** The listener knows immediately that the statement is intentionally incomplete.
2. **The clause establishes the structure.** The speaker gives the known action and circumstances rather than hiding them behind the unknown.
3. **The final particle exposes the missing support.** `Mu`, `nu`, `zhe`, `sha`, `lo`, or `ri` identifies what the speaker needs.
4. **The answer closes the frame.** The respondent supplies the missing matter, time, cause, method, place, or identity.

This is not ceremonial mysticism. It is compatible with ordinary Ohnoshan habits of diagnosis, navigation, repair, testimony, and collective coordination: establish the condition, identify the missing information, then restore a workable whole.

In loose English, the final particle can be called the **reason for asking**, provided “reason” means the information the question seeks. It is not always a causal reason. Only `zhe` asks why something happened. A more exact description is **the missing anchor of the question**.

## Why these are related forms rather than clipped roots

This proposal does not claim that speakers consciously assemble `mu` from `Mor` or `ri` from `Rin` each time they speak. The relationship is historical and phonological:

- the older semantic family explains why a sound became associated with an answer type;
- constant everyday use shortened and altered the form;
- the altered form became grammatical rather than lexical;
- the living particle no longer carries the full meaning of its older relative.

This gives Celan memory without requiring transparent literalism. `Ri` can retain an echo of identity without meaning “essence.” `Sha` can retain an echo of path without making every question about a literal road.

`Mu` and `nu` deliberately do not reduce to the more mechanically obvious `mo` and `no`. A chain such as `mo / no / lo` would place three vital question words one consonant apart with the same vowel, making them needlessly vulnerable in rapid speech, damaged communications, and field conditions. The proposed vowel raising keeps their ancestry audible but their living forms distinct.

The history of `lo` is the least settled member of the set. Its approved grammatical function is historically related to bound locative `-or`, but the exact route of that development remains open. Its present function is not derived by a productive modern rule and does not add a new sense to `Lor`.

## Collision test

The particles remain distinct when their semantic relatives occur normally inside the clause.

| Stress condition | Proposed frame | Intended reading |
|---|---|---|
| Asking what while naming matter | `ra kes Ya mor mu?` | What matter or material do you notice? |
| Asking when while naming a time | `ra var sharavok an Varthas nor Lunwek nu?` | When does the caravan go to the city during the lunar month? |
| Asking why while naming lineage | `ra kav kavral zhir an liankavor zhe?` | Why does the keeper preserve the lineage in the archive? |
| Asking how while naming a route | `ra doraen sharalorin sharashen an shara sha?` | How does the navigator place the map on the route? |
| Asking for identity while naming essence | `ra kes arural rin ri?` | Who does the practitioner perceive as the essence or spirit? |

Unlike bare `mor`, `nor`, `zhir`, `shara`, and `rin`, the final particles cannot be mistaken for the ordinary lexical item immediately before them.

## `Ri` does not divide “who” from “whom”

Celan does not need to reproduce the English case distinction between **who** and **whom**. `Ri` asks for the identity of a missing person, being, or agentive participant. Its relationship to the verb, stated participants, and surrounding context determines that participant's role.

This is one grammatical particle, not two English-shaped entries. Formal Celan should not acquire a second form merely to preserve a distinction that its own question system does not require.

## Restrictions

The approved set follows these limits:

1. `Ra` remains at the beginning of standard questions.
2. An optional short content particle may close the question to make the requested answer category explicit.
3. Yes-or-no questions use `ra` without a closing content particle.
4. The six particles are grammatical words, not productive roots.
5. They take no ordinary derivational suffixes and do not form compounds.
6. Their resemblance to older roots records history; it does not add new meanings to those roots.
7. Omission of the closing content particle is ordinary and grammatical; casual omission of opening `ra`, regional variants, and embedded-question behavior remain separate future decisions.

## Critical judgment

This is stronger than both earlier extremes:

- It avoids six completely unrelated arbitrary sounds.
- It avoids making ordinary `rin`, `mor`, `nor`, `zhir`, and `shara` perform two jobs in the same sentence.
- It preserves the opening-and-closing rhythm of the Question Frame.
- It creates only six high-frequency grammatical particles, not six expandable root families.

The set is approved after spoken-cadence and collision review. The exact historical route from locative `-or` to `lo` may remain a diachronic question without weakening the living grammar. Celan does not need a separate who/whom distinction.
