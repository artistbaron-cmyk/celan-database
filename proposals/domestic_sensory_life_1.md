# Domestic and Sensory Life 1

Status: **Approved — integrated into canon**

Current size: **45 approved entries** after the pre-approval scene audit, withdrawal of `Mekmor`, and deliberate lexicalization of six primitive roots as nouns.

Purpose: extend Volume 4 with the small household objects, ordinary materials, bodily sensations, and physical actions needed to make domestic, craft, meal, care, and injury scenes feel lived-in.

Audit baseline: 641 app headwords and 710 displayed senses. Existing coverage already includes `Peleth` knife/blade, `Drenvor` bowl/cup, `Krezor` hearth/fireplace/forge, `Pralor` table/flat surface, `Nethor` chair/seat, `Nethlor` bed/pallet, `Belrin` woven fabric, and `Mormek` worked stone.

All candidates below are intended as universal Celan. National origin and national usage should remain blank unless a later review identifies a genuinely nation-specific term. Categories remain the existing `Noun`, `Verb`, and `Adjective`; primitive-root status is derivational metadata, not a new category.

## Admission and collision review

1. `Shem` covers the shared functional core of a spoon and ladle rather than copying English utensil sizes. A larger serving ladle can be described with size or abundance; a tiny spoon with `Fel`. It does not replace `Drenvor`, which is the vessel receiving food or liquid.
2. `Krezvek` is a deep cooking vessel. A water-boiling kettle can be expressed transparently as `Dren Krezvek` unless repeated scenes later justify a separate lexicalized compound. It does not replace `Krezor`, the hearth or forge in which heating occurs.
3. `Krezpral` is deliberately shallow or flat, preserving a useful pot/pan contrast with `Krezvek`. The optional form is the euphonic `Krezapral`; the unsmoothed form remains the headword.
4. `Emilpral` covers plates and serving trays by function. It does not replace `Pralor`, a table or general flat work surface.
5. `Kavvek` is a portable storage jar rather than any vessel. `Drenvor` remains the ordinary bowl/cup, while `Xarvek` is larger enclosed household storage.
6. `Eshpral` is a raised object-supporting surface. It does not mean every high or elevated place.
7. `Xarvek` unifies chest, cupboard, and cabinet through enclosed storage. Doors and architectural closures remain `Vorkor` and `Xaraen`.
8. `Rask` distinguishes animal skin/raw hide from `Mekrask`, the intentionally prepared material leather. Approval of `Rask` would authorize only this root and `Mekrask`, not an automatic anatomy or clothing family.
9. `Lem` and `Lemath` preserve the material/process distinction between shapeable clay and fired ceramic without stretching `Dren` or `Mor`. The completed/enduring force of `-ath` marks clay that has been permanently transformed. They do not replace ordinary soil, `Mor`, or worked stone, `Mormek`.
10. `Selmek` names the material glass, not a drinking vessel. A glass used for drinking remains a kind of `Drenvor` unless its material matters.
11. `Mekmor` has been withdrawn during review. Generic metal is instead represented by the new primitive candidate `Kren` at entry 28; specific copper and bronze terms remain distinct.
12. `Tir` treats copper as an irreducible material rather than reverse-engineering it from color. `Tirkal` lexicalizes bronze as strengthened copper, but must not become a generic word for every copper alloy.
13. `Aiv` supplies the currently missing general pain noun. Existing `Aie! / Aia!` remains the reaction “Ouch!” rather than becoming the name of pain.
14. `Aivaen`, `Pelaivaen`, and `Rekaivaen` distinguish continuing ache, sharp localized sting, and hot/searing pain. `Rekaivaen` does not replace literal fire or `Krezaen` burning with anger.
15. `Teshaen` and `Rivaen` require new roots because existing roots do not encode itching or involuntary trembling without strained metaphors. Approval would authorize only the listed meanings and derivatives.
16. `Jelaen` is the active sensory verb. Existing `Jel` and `Soril` continue to name flavor qualities rather than the act of tasting.
17. `Kelvorthamaen` is intentionally compositional despite its length. It keeps chewing distinct from general crushing or mashing, `Tham`.
18. `Feldrenaen` describes small separated liquid movement. Rain remains `Drenalor`, and dropping a carried object remains `Aloraen`.
19. `Pelvokaen` generalizes bond-breaking into a physical sharp break. This metaphor needs explicit approval because bare `Vokaen` is strongly associated with betrayal or breaking a social bond.
20. `Selpralaen` and `Pelvaraen` form a useful craft contrast: surface finishing versus abrasive removal. Neither replaces general making, `Pralaen`, or tool use, `Karethaen`.
21. `Zem` names wood after it is treated as material. A living tree remains a tree or a named species such as `Velmarin`; `Marin` continues to refer to a branch or outward-growing limb.
22. `Kren` replaces the withdrawn `Mekmor` proposal. It names the general material class without claiming that all metal is forged or merely a kind of earth. `Tir` and `Tirkal` remain the more specific copper and bronze terms.
23. `Drentalaen` is controlled liquid transfer. It covers pouring water, broth, oil, or another pourable liquid, but does not automatically mean spilling or leaking.
24. `Jekthamaen` combines repeated circular movement with the working of soft or liquid material. It does not replace `Jekaen`, ordinary turning or changing facing, or `Tham`, mashing and structural breakdown.
25. `Drenselaen` covers washing and rinsing with liquid. It is physical cleaning and does not extend `Sel` into spiritual or moral purification.
26. `Numaen` is narrower than eating and drinking: it names the inward throat action that completes either process. Chewing remains `Kelvorthamaen`.
27. `Nolaen` names perceiving a scent. It does not mean emitting an odor, which remains unresolved.
28. `Tovaen` names contact or contact-based perception. It remains distinct from `Korinaen`, which requires holding or maintaining a grip.
29. `Emilrav` and `Drorav` form a bodily-need pair: pull toward food and pull toward physical water. Neither is a general emotional longing, which remains in the `Rav` and `Thera` families.
30. `Felkal` is temporary fatigue or reduced bodily strength, not permanent weakness, low authority, or small physical size.
31. `Maveth` covers general bodily sickness or illness without diagnosing a cause. It does not replace pain, fatigue, a wound, or a specific symptom.
32. `Aivkor` names the injured bodily site rather than the sensation of pain. A wound may remain `Aivkor` after pain fades, and pain may exist without a wound.
33. `Tesh`, `Riv`, `Num`, `Nol`, `Tov`, and `Mav` are deliberately lexicalized as nouns rather than being exposed under a new `Root` category. Their noun senses remain distinct from the approved verbs `Teshaen`, `Rivaen`, `Numaen`, `Nolaen`, and `Tovaen`, and the adjective `Maveth`.

Exact collision scanning found no existing app headword or stored variant matching candidates 27–39 or their proposed euphonic alternatives.

## Primitive-root decision

This proposal introduces thirteen possible primitives:

- `Shem`: handled scooping implement
- `Rask`: animal skin/raw hide
- `Lem`: workable natural clay
- `Tir`: copper
- `Aiv`: bodily pain
- `Tesh`: itching irritation
- `Riv`: involuntary trembling
- `Zem`: workable wood material
- `Kren`: generic metal material
- `Num`: swallowing or inward throat passage
- `Nol`: olfactory perception
- `Tov`: physical contact perception
- `Mav`: bodily illness

These are proposed because the concepts are frequent, concrete, and not honestly recoverable from the current roots. Approval would authorize only their stated meanings and the explicitly listed derivatives in this batch.

## Scene stress tests

- Meal preparation: “Take the jar from the shelf. Stir the stew with the spoon, scrape the pan, and put the plate on the table.” Covered by `Kavvek`, `Eshpral`, `Shem`, `Krezpral`, `Jekthamaen`, `Pelvaraen`, `Emilpral`, and existing `Pralor`.
- Material craft: “The worker shapes clay, fires the ceramic, polishes the bronze fitting, and fastens it to the leather strap.” Covered by `Lem`, `Lemath`, `Selpralaen`, `Tirkal`, `Mekrask`, and existing binding/tool verbs.
- Injury and discomfort: “My hand stings, the scrape burns, my shoulder aches, and the healing skin itches.” Covered by `Pelaivaen`, `Rekaivaen`, `Aivaen`, and `Teshaen`.
- Cold and illness: “The child shivers while the water drips from the blanket.” Covered by `Rivaen`, `Feldrenaen`, and existing body/household vocabulary.
- Eating and breakage: “Taste the stew, chew slowly, and stop—the glass jar snapped.” Covered by `Jelaen`, `Kelvorthamaen`, `Pelvokaen`, `Selmek`, and `Kavvek`.
- Completed meal sequence: “Pour the water, stir the stew, wash the bowl, and swallow the medicine.” Covered by `Drentalaen`, `Jekthamaen`, `Drenselaen`, and `Numaen`.
- Bodily report: “I am hungry, thirsty, tired, and sick, and the wound on my hand still aches.” Covered by `Emilrav`, `Drorav`, `Felkal`, `Maveth`, `Aivkor`, `Aivaen`, and established `Nethnor`.
- Sensory inspection: “Smell the food and touch the clay before you shape it.” Covered by `Nolaen`, `Tovaen`, `Lem`, and established making vocabulary.

## Recommended boundary for this batch

The thirteen high-frequency concepts found by the pre-approval audit are represented by candidates 27–39. Six of their primitive roots were later approved for independent noun use, bringing the batch to **45 approved entries** without adding new concepts or an invented word category. This remains a natural boundary: it closes recurring failures in meal, cleaning, perception, care, and basic bodily-state scenes without absorbing entire future batches on architecture, textile repair, medicine, or specialized craft.

Useful concepts intentionally deferred unless later scene testing elevates them include lid, basket, pillow, bite, wipe, sweep, numbness, fever, cough, nausea, dizziness, needle, sew/mend, wool, bone, spill, tear, melt, and cast.

## Approved review decisions

1. `Shem` covers both spoon and ladle through their shared handled-scooping function.
2. The thirteen listed primitive roots are approved. No unrestricted derivative families are authorized.
3. `Lemath` expresses fired clay or ceramic as clay in a completed, enduring state.
4. `Kren` is the approved primitive for generic metal. `Mekmor` remains withdrawn and does not enter canon.
5. `Tirkal` lexicalizes bronze specifically as strengthened copper without becoming a term for every copper alloy.
6. `Pelvokaen` extends `Vok` bond-breaking into physical snapping without weakening social `Vokaen`.
7. `Kelvorthamaen` is accepted as the transparent everyday verb for chewing.
8. `Jekthamaen` is accepted for stirring or mixing through repeated circular working of a soft or liquid substance.
9. `Emilrav` and `Drorav` express hunger and thirst as bodily pulls toward food and physical water.
10. `Aivkor` names a wound or injured place even after pain fades; `Aiv` itself remains bodily pain.
11. `Tesh`, `Riv`, `Num`, `Nol`, `Tov`, and `Mav` are approved as standalone nouns with the definitions listed above. Their primitive-root status remains derivational metadata, not a user-facing category.
