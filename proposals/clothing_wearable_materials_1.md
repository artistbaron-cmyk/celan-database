# Clothing, Wearable Protection, and Ordinary Materials 1

Status: **Approved and integrated — 2026-09-12**

Scope: first of two category passes. This pass establishes general clothing, climate-specific footwear and outerwear, worn protection, fasteners, textile materials, oilskin, and three material-performance adjectives. Pass 2 will address stone, metals, bone, crystal, coral, shell, wax, resin, pigment, tallow, and alloys.

All candidates are universal Celan. National origin and national usage remain blank. Proposed euphonic alternatives are optional searchable variants; every primary form is the unsmoothed construction.

## Proposed new primitives

Approval of this batch would authorize only the two listed roots and the explicitly listed derivatives, not automatic word families. `Olanbelrin` reuses the approved `Olan` root from Food, Agriculture, and Preservation 2.

### `Rema` — face

Closest roots considered: `Lorinor` head, `Lorinshal` eye, `Kelvor` mouth, and `Xar` covering.

Why they were not sufficient: a face covering can cover the mouth and nose, the eyes, or the whole visible front of the head. `Lorinor` is too broad and the individual feature terms are too narrow. A reusable face root is also needed beyond clothing for expression, injury, recognition, grooming, and social interaction. `Rema` supplies that ordinary anatomical center without changing any established body term.

### `Sura` — fur or animal hair as material

Closest roots considered: `Rask` raw hide, `Mekrask` leather, `Felzhir` fiber, and `Prak` softness.

Why they were not sufficient: fur is hair attached to or taken from an animal, not the skin beneath it, a prepared hide, generic fiber, or softness itself. `Sura` preserves that distinction and allows wool to be transparently defined as animal hair prepared for textile use.

## Root-reuse and collision review

1. `Belxar` supplies the missing general category of clothing. It does not replace `Belkor` tunic or shirt, `Xaresh` cloak or mantle, `Sharbel` pants or leggings, `Trakor` shoes or boots, or `Arthaxar` armor. Each remains a more specific garment or protective object.
2. `Belxaraen` names the ongoing state of wearing a garment. It is narrower than `Dor` “put or place”: a person can put clothing on a table without wearing it.
3. `Velbelxar` is a substantial sleeved cold-weather outer garment. `Xaresh` remains a cloak or mantle and may itself be warm, but does not become the generic word for a coat.
4. `Eshtrakor`, `Velpraltrakor`, and `Pelveltrakor` preserve three footwear contrasts that `Trakor` intentionally leaves broad: open footwear, weight-spreading snow travel, and bladed ice gliding.
5. `Lorinorxar`, `Remaxar`, `Korinxar`, `Lorinshalxar`, and `Remashalxar` form a transparent body-part-plus-cover system. Their full definitions remain lexical: approval would not make every possible body-part-plus-`Xar` compound automatic.
6. `Zhirbel` is a flexible securing band. `Zhirin` remains rope or cord, and `Zhirvok` remains a belt worn around the body. `Welzhirbel` requires a coordinated strap system rather than one strap.
7. `Belmek` is a reusable mechanical fastener. It does not replace the general verb `Bel` “bind, connect, fasten,” and it does not include knots, sewing, glue, or one-time seals. `Belmekaen` and `Kabelmekaen` name engaging and releasing that mechanism.
8. `Rinbelxar` requires standardized identity or duty marking. Matching clothes, fashionable styles, ceremonial garments, and armor are not automatically uniforms.
9. `Felzhir` distinguishes a fine constituent strand from `Zhir`, which remains the broader thread, line, or cord concept, and from `Belrin`, which is completed woven fabric.
10. `Sura` does not overwrite `Rask` hide or `Mekrask` leather. `Belsura` is textile-ready wool; not every animal's fur is wool, and not every wool object is still called fur.
11. `Verdselzhir`, `Selzhir`, and `Kalbelrin` are material classes with different defining properties: plant-derived breathability, fine smooth filament, and heavy durable weave. They do not replace the broad fabric term `Belrin` or authorize species-specific silk and plant-fiber names.
12. `Olan` is a broad material class, while `Olanbelrin` is specifically oil-treated cloth. Approval would not decide edible-oil subtypes, fuels, or ritual oils.
13. `Drenartheth` is resistance to water penetration, not merely the absence of current wetness. `Dreneth` remains wet or damp.
14. `Shanartheth` describes blocked unwanted transfer and may apply to thermal, acoustic, electrical, or magical insulation. `Zhelshaneth` describes the opposite behavior only for electrical or magical charge; it does not mean that every substance can transmit everything.
15. Exact scanning found no current headword or stored variant matching any proposed primary or euphonic form.

## Risks requiring explicit review

1. `Belxar` establishes the productive metaphor “woven/bound covering = garment.” It is broad enough for ordinary clothing but explicitly excludes objects merely carried on the body and protective equipment that is not clothing.
2. `Velbelxar` lexicalizes “cold-endurance garment” as a sleeved heavy coat. If speakers would treat every warm outer layer as a coat, the definition should be broadened before approval; otherwise the sleeve-and-structure distinction should stay.
3. `Rema` and `Sura` are new primitives. Approval would authorize only their listed meanings and the derivatives in this document, not automatic families.
4. `Lorinshalxar` and `Remashalxar` intentionally distinguish close-fitting eye protection from a broader eye-or-face shield. They should be tested together so goggles and visor do not collapse into one term.
5. `Shanartheth` generalizes the established passing/transfer root `Shan` into material insulation. This is semantically useful across heat, sound, electricity, and magic, but should be approved only if “protection against passage” feels like a natural Celan technical metaphor.
6. `Olanbelrin` reuses the universal `Olan` oil root approved in Food, Agriculture, and Preservation 2. `Olan` remains one shared headword and is not counted again in this clothing batch.
