# Information, Computation, Automation, and Electrical Systems

## Combined Lexical Review Report — Passes 1 and 2

**Status:** Approved and integrated  
**Scope:** Universal Celan vocabulary; no national origins proposed  
**Canon effect:** 57 new headwords and 57 displayed senses  
**Current dictionary baseline:** 1,185 app headwords and 1,263 displayed senses  
**Dictionary after integration:** 1,242 app headwords and 1,320 displayed senses  
**Review method:** Whole Canon Dictionary reconciliation, active-root reuse, exact-headword collision check, hidden-root policy review, Ohnoshan Metaphor Test, Ohnosha world-fit review, spoken cadence testing, register control, and headword-admission review

## Executive finding

Celan already has more of this domain than the English checklist suggests. It has machines, components, engines, sensors, gauges, calibration, conduits, wires, generators, batteries, a power grid, a communications network, a data-center building, archives, identifying records, writing tablets, control rooms, and general words for repair, command, warning, access, and measurement.

What it lacks is the semantic layer connecting those objects into information systems:

- information distinct from speech, text, truth, wisdom, and memory;
- data distinct from one record or a written text;
- bounded files, searchable databases, input, output, displays, and interfaces;
- computation, algorithms, code, programs, and automation;
- ordinary information operations such as copying, saving, searching, transmitting, uploading, downloading, synchronizing, protecting, and recovering access;
- distinct technical failures such as a defect, crash, corrupted data, power fault, overload, outage, and short circuit;
- electrical current, circuits, potential, conductors, insulators, switches, contacts, capacitors, protection, grounding, diagnostics, and control units.

The category divides naturally into two passes:

- **Pass 1 — Information, Computation, and Automation:** 35 proposed headwords.
- **Pass 2 — Electrical Systems, Diagnostics, and Control:** 22 proposed headwords.
- **Proposed new primitives:** `Dava`, information, and `Raku`, rule-governed computation.
- **No proposed additive senses to existing headwords.**
- **No new dictionary categories.**

These words are universal overall. Mechuma reveals the densest computational need, Valkeldor reveals storm-energy and weapons applications, Pelagae reveals pressure-safe signaling and aquatic networks, and every nation uses records, messages, measurements, stored power, controls, and failures. Characteristic applications do not justify assigning national ownership or origin.

## Ohnosha-fit boundary

This proposal describes functions rather than importing one era of Earth products.

- `Davaxar`, file, can be held in a digital, crystalline, mechanical, magical, biological, or hybrid system.
- `Rakumek`, computing device, does not require a keyboard, silicon chip, or Earth-style personal computer.
- `Belraku`, program, is executable instruction structure regardless of whether the substrate is electronic, crystalline, technomantic, or another engineered medium.
- `Varokraku`, automation, does not imply artificial intelligence or humanlike agency.
- `Zhel` remains the shared energy or charge root because Ohnoshan electrical and magical engineering genuinely overlap. The new compounds narrow the physical or technical relation without redefining all magic as electricity.

The proposal deliberately defers artificial intelligence, cybernetic personhood, implants, prosthetics, holography, and detailed surveillance law. Those concepts require more world evidence or belong in later social and magical reviews. This is not a claim that Ohnosha lacks them.

## Pass 1 semantic separations

- `Dava` is meaningful content. `Kel` is language, word, name, speech, or a connected text. `Lian` is truth. `Vel` is wisdom or deep memory. `Shendava` is data arranged for examination or processing. These concepts may overlap in an event without becoming synonyms.
- `Kavdava` is any preserved informational account. `Rinshen` remains an official identifying record, and `Liankavor` remains an archive or repository.
- `Davaxar` is bounded information: the information has been placed into a retrievable unit. `Xardava` reverses the semantic direction: concealment or protection has been applied to information. The root order distinguishes file from encryption.
- `Beldava` is the organized information body. `Belzhir` is the communications network carrying information, and `Belzhirkavor` is the physical data-center facility maintaining systems at scale.
- `Davapral` displays changing information. `Kelpral` supports writing, drawing, calculation, and display more generally; it may function as a `Davapral` in context but does not cease to be a tablet.
- `Zheldava` must carry information. A spark, pulse, magical surge, or electrical charge without encoded or interpretable content remains `Zhel`.
- `Ohmshan` is an exchange boundary. `Belzhir` is the wider connected network. A screen or control panel may contain an interface, but `Davapral` and `Ohmshan` name different functions.
- `Anmek` and `Thalmek` apply across information, material, and energy systems. This allows engineers to discuss a mill, waterworks, power grid, or computer with the same input/output distinction.
- `Raku` is not intelligence, thought, wisdom, judgment, or ordinary problem-solving. It requires defined operations over representations or measurable states.
- `Rakushara` is the operational path. `Rakukel` is the symbolic expression. `Belraku` is the executable organized program. One program may use several algorithms and contain many lines or units of code.
- `Varokraku` names automation as transferred execution. `Belwek` remains any managed recurring process and may be performed manually, biologically, mechanically, magically, or automatically.
- `Andavaen` and `Thaldavaen` are defined from the speaker's or active system's transfer perspective. `Davashanaen` is direction-neutral transmission.
- `Mekreth` is the current malfunction, `Felmekreth` is a bounded underlying defect, and `Rekmekreth` is an abrupt severe loss of operation.
- `Rethdava` concerns damaged informational integrity. It does not mean a lie, disagreement, uncertain claim, social corruption, or organic rot.
- `Xardavaen` protects content; `Kaxardavaen` restores readable use. Neither grants authority. Authorization remains a civic or system permission expressed through `Belrinpel` and access context.

## Pass 2 semantic separations

- `Zhel` remains the ordinary broad word for energy, charge, spark, or power-flash. Context may identify electricity. The pass adds relational distinctions rather than a competing generic electricity word.
- `Zhelwel` is sustained charge movement. `Ravzhel` is the potential difference capable of driving that movement. Neither is the amount of stored energy in a `Zhelkav`.
- `Zhelshara` is one circuit path. `Zhelbel` is the connected distribution system or power grid containing many circuits.
- `Zhelshan` is a conductor as an object or functional material. `Zhelshaneth` says that something is conductive. `Krenzhir` remains a metal wire or cable, which may serve as one kind of conductor.
- `Zhelxar` is an insulating material, object, or barrier. `Shanartheth` describes an insulated object or layer.
- `Jorzhelkav` is optimized for rapid storage and release. `Zhelkav` is the broader battery or power-cell category used for sustained reserve power.
- `Jorzhel` is brief. `Worzhel` concerns capacity being exceeded and may persist. `Zhelreth` is any abnormal electrical fault, while `Rethzhelshara` identifies the specific unintended path called a short circuit.
- `Kazhel` is a loss of supplied power. An individual machine can stop for many other reasons described by `Mekreth`.
- `Zhelaen` supplies operating energy. `Kavzhelaen` specifically stores energy for later. `Thalzhelaen` releases stored charge outward.
- `Arthzhelshara` is deliberately broader than one Earth hardware design: fuse, breaker, magical cutoff, and comparable protective interruption devices share the same function.
- `Morzhelshan` may connect to physical earth or to an engineered common reference. It does not invoke sacred `Terra` or define a spiritual bond.
- `Mekkes` and `Mekkesaen` concern constructed systems. `Keshen` remains a clinical examination of a patient, while `Kesmek` remains a sensor device.
- `Varokmek` is the controlling component. `Varokraku` is the wider practice or condition of automated execution.
- `Rethzheldava` disrupts a signal. `Rethdava` describes information already made incoherent or damaged; interference can cause corruption, but they are not identical.

## Primitive-root review

### `Dava` — information

The active inventory has words for speech, text, truth, wisdom, memory, records of identity, archives, measurement, and communication networks. None provides the neutral concept of meaningful content that may be spoken, sensed, encoded, stored, copied, or transmitted without claiming that it is true, written, wise, measured, or remembered.

- `Kel` was rejected because its approved text sense explicitly excludes information or data generally.
- `Lian` was rejected because false information remains information.
- `Vel` and `Velrathor` were rejected because information need not be wisdom or memory.
- `Shen` was rejected because information need not begin as measurement or assigned value.
- `Bel` was rejected because a single fact or message need not be a system.

`Dava` therefore earns a two-syllable primitive with a stable semantic center and several independently useful compounds. Approval would authorize only `Dava` and the explicitly listed `Dava` forms in this report, not an automatic family.

### `Raku` — computation

Celan can count, measure, subtract, decide, reason, and use machines, but it lacks a root for applying defined operations to representations or states. Treating all computation as `Shen`, measurement, would exclude symbolic and logical operations. Treating it as `Varok`, command, would describe control rather than transformation. Treating it as `Belwek`, recurring process, would exclude non-recurring algorithms and manual computation.

`Raku` names the operation family rather than a particular device or substrate. It supports clear distinctions among computation, computing machine, processor, algorithm, code, program, and automation without forcing `Bel`, `Mek`, or `Kel` to become generic technical filler.

Approval would authorize only `Raku` and the explicitly listed `Raku` forms in this report, not an automatic family.

## Collision and integrity review

- No proposed spelling is intended to replace an existing headword.
- No established displayed sense is changed, hidden, narrowed, or removed.
- No hidden root-analysis sense is exposed as a dictionary definition.
- The proposal does not add meanings to `Kel`, `Belwek`, `Dreshal`, `Selshan`, `Rinshen`, or `Zhel`.
- Optional Euphony forms would resolve to their primary entries and would not increase the headword count.
- Every candidate uses an existing dictionary category.
- Exact collision checking must be rerun immediately before any approved integration because the dictionary may grow between proposal and approval.
