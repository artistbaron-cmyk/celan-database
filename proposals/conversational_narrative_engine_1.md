# Conversational and Narrative Engine 1

Status: **Approved for canon and integrated on 2026-09-11**

Purpose: supply the ordinary physical verbs, mental verbs, aspect words, causal nouns, discourse repairs, and spatial/temporal relations needed to tell a scene, explain what happened, and respond naturally in conversation.

Audit baseline: 602 app headwords, 670 displayed senses, and 40 approved expansion headwords before this proposal.

This batch contains 39 new app headwords and one new sense of the existing headword `Shan`. Euphonic variants remain attached to their entries and do not increase the headword count.

## Admission decisions

- `Dor`, `Dar`, `Siv`, and `Kes` are proposed as new short primitive roots because “put,” “wait,” “try,” and “notice” recur constantly and the available compounds are too long or indirect for ordinary speech.
- `Dor`, `Dar`, `Siv`, and `Kes` retain the existing category `Verb`. Their proposed primitive-root status belongs in derivation and root metadata, not in the category field.
- `Dar` has one stable core—pausing while something remains pending. The interruption “wait/hold on” is a clipped imperative use of the verb, not a second category.
- `Shan` already means passing, transfer, or handoff between states. “Through/across” is proposed as a `Preposition` sense of that existing headword.
- The unsmoothed construction is the default headword. `Sivarethaen`, `Athanor`, `Nethanor`, `Rethapral`, `Belareth`, `Vethavar`, and `Vethanor` are optional euphonic variants of the corresponding unsmoothed forms and do not create additional headwords.
- All entries have a universal Common Celan meaning. The national notes below illustrate characteristic use, not ownership or restricted usage.
- The category field uses only these existing dictionary categories: `Verb`, `Noun`, `Particle`, `Quantifier`, `Interjection`, `Preposition`, and `Conjunction`. Labels such as “primitive root,” “result-state,” “aspect,” “temporal,” and “new sense” remain editorial metadata rather than categories.

## Use and collision review

Each item below gives two ordinary situations, a characteristic national application, and the nearest existing term that it must not erase.

1. `Dor`: put a cup on a table; set a trail marker beside a path. Jasaran use: placing a measured ration at its assigned station. Distinct from `Tal/Talaen`, which transfers possession or gives/receives.
2. `Vekaen`: carry a child; transport a bundle without handing it off. Pelagaean use: carrying cargo from quay to vessel. Distinct from `Shan`, which emphasizes passage or transfer between holders/states.
3. `Korinaen`: hold a rope; keep a door in position. Valkeldorian use: holding a shield line or a tool under load. Distinct from `Mb`, which is support or foundational bearing rather than active grip.
4. `Aloraen`: drop a tool accidentally; let an anchor fall deliberately. Pelagaean use: dropping an anchor or weighted line. Distinct from `Alor`, the intransitive event of falling/descent.
5. `Kaxaraen`: open a door; unseal a jar. Nivveilian use: opening a shelter vent without dismantling the shelter. Distinct from bare `Ka` and `Xar`; the compound lexicalizes removal of a functional closure.
6. `Xaraen`: close a door; cover a container. Nivveilian use: sealing a shelter against wind. Distinct from `Bel`, which binds or fastens but does not inherently make access closed.
7. `Jekaen`: turn one's body toward a sound; rotate a handle. Mechuman use: turning a valve or control wheel. Distinct from `Jek`, which names spiral/twisted form rather than the ordinary action.
8. `Sharaen`: follow a traveler; follow a route or instruction. Zarithan use: following a scout's track. Distinct from `Lorin`, which is to guide or lead from the other side of the relation.
9. `Dar`: wait for a person; wait for rain to pass; as `Dar!`, briefly interrupt with “hold on.” Pelagaean use: waiting on a tide window. Distinct from `Nethaen`, which is to sit/rest without an expected pending event.
10. `Siv`: try to repair a cup; attempt an unfamiliar route. Mechuman use: testing a new mechanism. Distinct from `Pralaen`, which describes making and does not leave outcome open.
11. `Sivathaen`: make a repair that works; reach the goal of a plan. Mechuman use: a process meets its intended specification. Distinct from grammatical `-ath`, because this verb lexicalizes successful outcome rather than aspect alone.
12. `Sivrethaen`: fail to open a stuck door; have a plan not achieve its aim. Mechuman use: a device or trial fails its intended function. Distinct from `Reth`, which is uncertainty/confusion without an attempted outcome.
13. `Tharsharaen`: decide which road to take; decide whether to accept an offer. Jasaran use: choosing a rationing or travel course under scarcity. Distinct from `Thar-ka` desire and `Varok` command; this is selection by the deciding subject.
14. `Tharshara`: choose a meal from options; record a civic choice. Marakorian use: a household or community choice negotiated among alternatives. Distinct from `Reth` “or,” which presents alternatives but does not name the selected option.
15. `Drenvaraen`: change a plan; observe weather becoming different. Verdalrisian use: describing ecological or seasonal change without implying improvement. Distinct from symbolic `Dren`; the compound is an explicit everyday change verb.
16. `Kes`: notice a distant sound; realize that an object is missing. Zarithan use: registering movement or danger during watch. Distinct from `Shalaen` seeing; noticing is mental registration and can follow any sense or inference.
17. `Velzhiraen`: remember an appointment; recall the route home. Nivveilian use: retaining a route, name, or account through a long winter. Distinct from `Rathorimaen`, whose current meaning includes memorializing and ritual remembrance.
18. `Kavelzhiraen`: forget a name; fail to perform an intended task because it left memory. Nivveilian use: losing a practical detail under fatigue or isolation. Distinct from having no knowledge in the first place.
19. `Athnor`: say that someone has already eaten; report that work was complete before arrival. Mechuman use: marking a process as complete by the reference time. Distinct from perfective `-ath`, because it scopes the clause as “already/by then.”
20. `Nethnor`: say that rain is still falling; say that someone still holds the rope. Nivveilian use: conditions continuing longer than expected. Distinct from `Thalka` “yet/still” as contrast; this is neutral continuation.
21. `Felath`: say the travelers almost arrived; say the vessel is nearly full. Jasaran use: a supply almost reaching depletion or target measure. Distinct from `Fel` “slightly,” which gives small degree without proximity to completion.
22. `Worlian`: confirm there is enough water; say an explanation is sufficient. Jasaran use: adequacy against a measured need. Distinct from `Woreth` full/abundant, since enough can be less than full.
23. `Rethshara`: take the river road instead; use wood instead of metal. Marakorian use: substituting material or plan while preserving the goal. Distinct from `Reth` “or,” which offers alternatives without signaling replacement.
24. `Servok`: build something together; travel as one company. Marakorian use: coordinated household or communal labor. Distinct from `Ser` “with” and `Vok` unity separately; this is the ordinary manner adverb.
25. `Kavok`: walk apart from the group; place two objects separately. Marakorian use: distinguishing deliberate separation from social rejection. Distinct from bare `Ka` withoutness; the compound marks loss or absence of togetherness.
26. `Zhirkor`: identify the cause of a sound; investigate the cause of an illness. Verdalrisian use: tracing what initiated crop or ecosystem change. Distinct from `Kora` “because,” which links clauses rather than naming a cause.
27. `Zhirath`: report the result of a trial; discuss the consequence of a decision. Mechuman use: recording an observed output or outcome. Distinct from `Varthen` “thus,” which sequences an inference rather than naming its result.
28. `Lianzhir`: explain the reason for leaving; ask the reason for a rule. Zarithan use: justifying an instruction or route choice. Distinct from `Kora` “because”; this is the reason as a noun and may be evidential or motivational.
29. `Rethpral`: admit taking a wrong turn; correct an incorrect measurement. Marakorian use: naming a repairable error without treating the maker as morally bad. Distinct from general `Reth` confusion and from failure, which may occur without a mistake.
30. `Belreth`: describe a jammed door as a problem; discuss a malfunction in a larger system. Mechuman use: a bounded difficulty that can be analyzed. Distinct from `Rethvok` chaos/disorder, which is broader and need not be solvable.
31. `Lianbel`: give the answer to a riddle; propose a repair plan. Mechuman use: an arrangement that resolves a stated difficulty. Distinct from `Thal'vokaen` harmonizing or reconciliation; a solution need not concern social balance.
32. `Rakel`: ask a speaker to repeat words lost in noise; interrupt when a meaning was not understood. Pelagaean use: conversational repair across wind, distance, or vessel noise. Distinct from bare `Ra` questioning and `Shalra` “well?/so then?”
33. `Serdel`: stand between two houses; choose between two options. Jasaran use: locating a path or well relative to paired landmarks. Distinct from `Ser` with/and, since this construction establishes two bounds.
34. `Shalvar`: point to the person ahead; describe the front of a moving line. Zarithan use: scouting and formation direction. Distinct from `Var` toward; this locates an entity relative to facing or motion.
35. `Vethvar`: point to someone behind; refer to the rear of a building or group. Zarithan use: guarding the unseen or rearward approach. Distinct from `Veth` hiddenness; behind need not actually be concealed.
36. `Shan`: walk through a gate; sail across a channel. Pelagaean use: movement across a boundary or navigable span. Distinct from `Var` toward and `Thal` from; `Shan` profiles traversal from one side to another.
37. `Shalnor`: finish before sunrise; refer to an event earlier than the meeting. Nivveilian use: ordering work before darkness, freeze, or storm. Distinct from bare `Nor`, which marks time without relative order.
38. `Vethnor`: meet after the meal; describe what happened later than the storm. Nivveilian use: scheduling after thaw, night, or a weather event. Distinct from future `Nor-`; this is relative sequence, not grammatical future tense.
39. `Sernor`: speak while walking; remain indoors during rain. Verdalrisian use: describing simultaneous natural and human processes. Distinct from spatial/comitative `Ser`; the compound explicitly binds events in time.
40. `Varnor`: wait until dawn; follow a road up to the bridge-opening time. Jasaran use: setting a clear temporal limit on rationing, travel, or waiting. Distinct from future `Nor-`; this identifies an endpoint rather than tense.

## Scene stress test

These are capability checks, not proposed canon example sentences.

- Domestic interruption: “Wait—what did you say? Put the bowl down and close the door. We already have enough.” Covered by `Dar`, `Rakel`, `Dor`, `Aloraen`, `Xaraen`, `Athnor`, and `Worlian`, plus existing `Ser` “and.”
- Failed repair and explanation: “I tried, but it failed. Then I noticed my mistake, found the cause of the problem, chose a solution, and changed the plan.” Covered by `Siv`, `Sivrethaen`, existing `Thalka` and `Vara`, then `Kes`, `Rethpral`, `Zhirkor`, `Belreth`, `Tharsharaen`, `Lianbel`, and `Drenvaraen`.
- Travel narrative: “Follow me. We carried the cargo together through the gate, waited until dawn, turned between the markers, and saw one traveler ahead and another behind. We left after the rain.” Covered by `Sharaen`, `Vekaen`, `Servok`, the new `Shan` sense, `Dar`, `Varnor`, `Jekaen`, `Serdel`, `Shalvar`, `Vethvar`, and `Vethnor`.

The three scenes can now be described without switching to English for the requested engine concepts. Full Celan sentence examples should be written only after the headwords and the two paired orientation decisions are approved.

## Approved editorial resolutions

1. `Kaxaraen` and `Xaraen` generalize the established cover/veil root into an approved open/close pair across doors, lids, eyes, and passages.
2. `Shalnor` and `Vethnor` establish the approved paired orientation light/front = earlier and shade/behind = later for narrative time.
3. `Belreth` is approved as narrower than chaos: a bounded and actionable problem that works for material difficulties and abstract complications.
4. `Dor`, `Dar`, `Siv`, and `Kes` are approved primitives. Approval authorizes these four meanings and only the explicitly listed derivatives rather than automatic word families.
5. `Velzhiraen` and `Kavelzhiraen` cover ordinary recall and forgetting without replacing or weakening ritual `Rathorimaen`.
