# Spatial Reference and Deixis 1

Status: **Approved for canon and integrated on 2026-09-14**

Purpose: give ordinary Celan a usable system for left and right, demonstrative distance, static interior and exterior location, and generic movement across a boundary.

Audit baseline: 1,350 app headwords, 1,428 displayed senses, and 798 approved expansion rows before this batch.

Integrated result: 1,359 app headwords, 1,438 displayed senses, and 808 approved expansion rows. The batch added nine app headwords, one spatial sense to existing `Kor`, and four teaching units that document the approved grammar. It also established three new primitive elements: `Naku`, `Sora`, and the bound deictic marker `Den`.

## Design decision

The approved design uses a **mixed spatial system**:

- body- or object-relative `Naku` and `Sora` handle immediate left and right, while existing `Kor` supplies the center;
- `Deni`, `Denya`, and `Denla` locate a referent relative to the speaker, listener, or both;
- existing celestial, wind, map, and route vocabulary remains the long-range navigational layer;
- existing `An`, `Ka`, and `Shan` supply the interior, exterior, and boundary-crossing family.

It does not propose cardinal directions or culture-wide mountain-ward, sea-ward, light-ward, or shadow-ward axes. The current canon does not establish a stable absolute frame, and Ohnosha's variable terrain makes landmark-based absolutes unsafe to assume. That question should remain separate until the world's dependable navigational anchors are known.

## Existing coverage and actual gaps

Celan already has:

| Existing form | Retained meaning |
|---|---|
| `Shalvar` | ahead of or in front of, relative to facing or motion |
| `Vethvar` | behind or at the back of, relative to facing or motion |
| `Esh` | above or over |
| `Morl` | under or beneath |
| `Serdel` | between two anchors |
| `An` | at or in a physical place |
| `Thal` | from or out of |
| `Shan` | through or across a side or boundary |
| `Var` | go, move, or travel; also to or towards |
| `Dralshara` | star route or celestial path |
| `Eshlor` | wind bearing |
| `Lormek` | compass |
| `Sharashen` | map |

The remaining dead ends are:

1. no ordinary lateral pair for left and right;
2. no defined demonstrative system, despite the source explicitly anticipating demonstratives;
3. no stable distinction among this/here, that/there by the listener, and yonder;
4. no admitted ordinary headword for an interior and no stable construction for static “outside”;
5. no generic enter/exit pair outside transportation;
6. no documented way to make speaker-directed motion explicit in a phrase such as “come here.”

`Thalor` is attested once in a source example translated as “outside,” but it is not an assembled dictionary headword. It is not recommended here. Although `Thalorim` is canonically parsed as `Thal` balance + `orim` essence, the proposed `Thalor` would look and sound too much like the beginning of the established lightning word. The source witness remains preserved without being promoted.

`nor-ka` is excluded. It is principally documented as the passive Echo particle and is also involved in a source tension around “now/this moment.” Neither reading makes it a spatial demonstrative.

## Approved grammar clarification

### 1. Lateral frame

`Naku` and `Sora` require an oriented frame. The default anchor is determined in this order:

1. a person or animal uses that being's own facing;
2. a vehicle, tool, or building with a functional front uses its own front;
3. a traveler or route uses the current direction of travel;
4. a map or diagram uses its stated orientation;
5. if no orientation is recoverable, the speaker must state or establish an anchor.

This prevents left and right from silently becoming geographic absolutes.

They may be used after a motion verb without an expressed anchor when the actor's facing is obvious:

```text
va jekaen Ya naku.
turn      you leftward
“Turn left.”
```

They may occur between a located referent and its anchor, following the established `Shalvar` and `Vethvar` pattern:

```text
rinaen nethor naku I.
stands chair left-of me
“The chair is to my left.”
```

Body-part and object labels may form transparent compounds such as `Nakukorin` “left hand” and `Sorakorin` “right hand.” These constructions are not proposed as separate dictionary headwords in this batch.

`Kor` supplies the general center or middle of a bounded whole. It is a position, not a third lateral direction:

```text
rinaen I an kor.
stands I at center
“I am in the middle.”
```

`Kordel` remains possible as a transparent construction meaning “the core or midpoint of two identified references.” Because `Del` means only “two,” `Kordel` is not proposed as a general word for middle and does not receive a headword in this batch.

### 2. Three-way demonstratives

The demonstrative particles are not nouns. Each performs one core function: it marks a referent according to whose immediate sphere contains it. The particle follows the noun it identifies:

```text
xaror deni    this room, by me
xaror denya   that room, by you
xaror denla   that room over there
```

It may appear without an expressed noun when the omitted referent is understood. This is ordinary ellipsis, not an additional noun sense:

```text
deni     this one
denya    that one by you
denla    that one over there
```

Existing `An` turns the deictic reference into a locative expression:

```text
an deni     here, in the speaker's sphere
an denya    there, in the listener's sphere
an denla    over there, away from both
```

The particles do not inflect for singular or plural. Number remains on the noun phrase, numeral, or pronoun. This keeps the system analytic and avoids creating six or more forms merely to reproduce English *this/these* and *that/those*.

Distance is relational rather than measured. Something held by the listener can be `Denya` even when the listener is only an arm's length away. Something visible across a courtyard can be `Denla` because it lies outside both participants' immediate spheres.

### 3. Motion goals and “come”

This proposal clarifies an already attested pattern: after a motion verb, `An` may mark the place reached or entered, while in a static clause it continues to mark location.

```text
rinaen I an xaror.     “I am in the room.”
var I an xaror.        “I go into/to the room.”
```

Celan does not need a new primitive meaning “come.” Speaker-directed motion is expressed through `Var` plus `Deni`:

```text
va var Ya an deni.
move   you to here-by-me
“Come here.”
```

This preserves the ordinary motion center of `Var` while allowing the demonstrative system to supply perspective.

### 4. Interior, exterior, entry, and exit

`Anor` names an interior region. `Kaan` relates something to the exterior of a boundary:

```text
rinaen I an anor.     “I am inside.”
rinaen I kaan xaror.  “I am outside the room.”
```

`Anshanaen` and `Thalshanaen` require an actual boundary crossing:

```text
va anshanaen Ya xaror.     “Enter the room / Come inside the room.”
thalshanaen I mekor.       “I exit the building.”
```

Simply walking nearer to a building is not `Anshanaen`; simply walking away from it is not `Thalshanaen` unless the person crosses from its interior to its exterior. `Kaan` describes location outside a boundary and does not itself assert that a crossing occurred.

## Root-reuse and admission review

### `Naku`, `Sora`, and spatial `Kor`

Roots considered: `Shal`, `Veth`, `Esh`, `Morl`, `Korin`, `Shara`, and `Lor`.

- `Shal` and `Veth` already support light/shadow and front/back contrasts. Reusing them would make a mundane lateral distinction sound cosmological and could attach false moral value to one side.
- `Esh` and `Morl` are vertical and environmental, not lateral.
- `Korin` names the hand and grip. Deriving universal direction from a particular hand would make anatomy the coordinate system and create problems for tools, vehicles, maps, injury, and bodily difference.
- `Shara` and `Lor` express route and guidance but cannot select one lateral side without an additional contrast.

Two independent primitive forms are therefore recommended. Their sounds are deliberately dissimilar enough to remain distinguishable in shouted instructions, bad weather, workshops, and vehicle cabins. Neither is derived from sacred symbolism, handedness, moral worth, or national geography.

General middle does not require a third primitive. `Kor` already has the active semantic center core/source/origin point, and approved words such as `Korwek` use it for a physically central component. Extending `Kor` to the middle or central region of a bounded whole passes the Ohnoshan Metaphor Test without replacing its established source and strength meanings.

`Del` was considered but means “two,” not “paired sides.” `Kordel` can therefore describe the midpoint or core of two stated references, but it cannot honestly mean every kind of middle. It remains an unlisted transparent construction.

### `Den`, `Deni`, `Denya`, and `Denla`

Roots considered: `I`, `Ya`, `La`, `Kes`, `Pel`, `Nor`, and `Lor`.

- `I`, `Ya`, and `La` already provide exactly the three participant anchors the system needs, but none marks a noun or location as demonstrated.
- `Kes` centers noticing or mental registration; a demonstrated object need not be newly noticed.
- `Pel` centers a precise edge or point and would make demonstratives too physically narrow.
- `Nor` is temporal and already overloaded across time, futurity, and the Echo particle.
- `Lor` centers guidance, sight, and directed focus rather than grammatical reference.

`Den` is therefore established as a new one-syllable **bound grammatical primitive** meaning deictic reference relative to the speech participants. It is not an independent dictionary headword. This batch authorizes only `Deni`, `Denya`, and `Denla`, not an unrestricted `Den` family.

This system extends Celan's relational person grammar: the speaker, listener, and third-person sphere organize shared attention just as person and clusivity already organize participation.

### `Anor`, `Kaan`, `Anshanaen`, and `Thalshanaen`

Roots considered: `An`, `Ka`, `Thal`, `Shan`, `Xar`, `Var`, and `-or`.

- `An` already locates something in a physical place.
- `Ka` already supplies withoutness or the absence of a stated relation. Combined with `An`, it gives the compact literal contrast “not within.”
- `Thal` continues to supply motion or origin from/out of, but `Thalor` is rejected because its surface form is liable to be parsed as part of `Thalorim` “lightning.”
- `Shan` already names passage through or across a boundary.
- `-or` already forms a place, domain, or locus.
- `Xar` was rejected because an interior need not be covered, hidden, or architecturally enclosed. One may be inside a boundary, courtyard, cave mouth, marked safety zone, or field of effect.
- `Var` remains general motion. Boundary crossing deserves the more exact `Shan` center.

`Kaan` is established as a lexicalized preposition rather than the looser two-word sequence `ka an`. Its high frequency and fixed spatial contrast with `An` justify one searchable form. It does not mean a general absence, rejection, or lack of possession.

The generic enter/exit pair is also supported by the approved specialized forms `Lorzhiranshan` “import” and `Lorzhirthalshan` “export,” which already contrast `An` inward passage with `Thal` outward passage. `Anshanaen` and `Thalshanaen` remove the commercial border component and lexicalize the ordinary physical actions.

No new primitive is required for this part of the system.

## Meaning boundaries

| Contrast | Required distinction |
|---|---|
| `Naku` / `Sora` | Lateral opposites within the same oriented frame. Neither is an absolute compass direction. |
| `Kor` / `Kordel` | Spatial `Kor` is the general center of a bounded whole. Transparent `Kordel` is only the midpoint or core of two identified references. |
| `Kor` / existing senses | The new spatial sense is additive. It does not replace `Kor` as source/strength or its handspan measure sense. |
| `Naku` / English “left” | `Naku` never means remaining, abandoned, or departed. |
| `Sora` / English “right” | `Sora` never means correct, acceptable, lawful entitlement, or political alignment. `Lia'ser` remains the acknowledgement “right/okay/understood.” |
| `Deni` / `Denya` | `Deni` is in the speaker's sphere; `Denya` is in the listener's. Physical distance alone does not collapse the contrast. |
| `Denya` / `Denla` | `Denya` remains associated with the listener; `Denla` is removed from both participants' immediate spheres. |
| `Deni` / `nor-ka` | `Deni` is spatial or referential. `nor-ka` remains governed by its temporal/passive source tension. |
| `An` / `Anor` | `An` relates something to a place. `Anor` names the interior region itself. |
| `An` / `Kaan` | `An` marks location in or at a place. `Kaan` marks location outside or beyond a boundary. |
| `Ka` / `Kaan` | `Ka` is general withoutness or absence. Lexicalized `Kaan` is specifically spatial and does not negate possession or action generally. |
| `Kaan` / `Thal` | `Kaan` is static outside-location. `Thal` marks source or movement from/out of. |
| `Anshanaen` / `Anvekaen` | `Anshanaen` is generic entry into a bounded place. `Anvekaen` specifically boards a vehicle, vessel, or transport animal for travel. |
| `Thalshanaen` / `Thalvekaen` | `Thalshanaen` is generic exit from a bounded place. `Thalvekaen` specifically disembarks after travel. |
| `Anshanaen` / `Var` | `Anshanaen` entails crossing from outside to inside. `Var` is general movement and may stop at a boundary. |

## Ohnoshan cultural fit

This system is deliberately ordinary.

The lateral pair is suited to a population coordinating bodies, care, tools, weapons, vehicles, construction, and mixed technologies after Day One. It sounds like language developed because someone needed to move a cart, bind the correct arm, open the correct panel, or warn another worker quickly—not because a priest assigned cosmic value to each hand.

The demonstratives grow from Celan's existing concern with relationship. `Deni`, `Denya`, and `Denla` do more than measure meters: they identify whose immediate sphere contains the object. A cup beside the listener is grammatically different from a tower distant from everyone. That makes shared attention part of the language without turning an everyday exchange into ceremony.

The interior/exterior family reflects the practical importance of boundaries in Ohnosha: shelter walls, city gates, vehicle hulls, safe zones, anomaly perimeters, homes, workshops, and storm cover. `Shan` makes entry and exit acts of crossing a real boundary, `Anor` names the interior, and `Kaan` locates something outside it.

Long-distance navigation remains observational. Ohnoshans can use star routes, wind bearings, compasses, maps, currents, passes, and named destinations without pretending that every settlement shares one permanent landmark axis. The result is a layered system: bodily coordinates nearby, relational deixis in conversation, and observed bearings on the road.

## Cadence and collision review

- `Naku` and `Sora` have distinct initial consonants, vowels, and final rhythms. They should not collapse into one another when shouted.
- Existing `Kor` gives the center a shorter form than either lateral direction without manufacturing a false three-root paradigm.
- `Deni`, `Denya`, and `Denla` repeat the audible `Den-` system marker while preserving the established person anchors.
- `Anshanaen` and `Thalshanaen` are longer than the lateral terms because they describe a complete boundary transition. Their shared `-shanaen` cadence makes the opposition learnable.
- Exact app-headword checks found no collision for any of the nine new candidates.
- `Sora` remains distinct from `Savor` “care professional” in consonant order, vowel pattern, and meaning.
- `Anor` occurs as a letter sequence inside other words but has no exact headword collision.
- `Kaan` has no exact headword collision. Its two adjacent vowels preserve the audible boundary between established `Ka` and `An`.
- Rejected `Thalor` remains only in its preserved source example; it is not treated as a shortened `Thalorim` or a new dictionary entry.
- `Sora` must not inherit any English polysemy from *right*. The established Celan acknowledgement remains `Lia'ser`.

## Approved constraints and future review points

1. **`Naku` and `Sora` are new primitive roots.** They are intentionally mundane and semantically arbitrary in the way basic directional words often are. This approval authorizes only the two listed relational meanings, not automatic derivative families.
2. **`Den` is a new bound grammatical primitive.** The three demonstratives establish a grammatical system, not merely three unrelated vocabulary items.
3. **The speaker/listener/distal contrast is a stronger commitment than English this/that.** The creator has chosen to retain all three because they extend Celan's relational person system, despite the additional choice required in use.
4. **Spatial `Kor` is an additive sense of an existing headword.** Its source/strength and handspan-measure senses remain visible. `Kordel` is not approved as another headword through this proposal.
5. **`Kaan` lexicalizes two established grammatical elements.** Its approved scope is the ordinary spatial contrast with `An`; it does not inherit the broader meanings of `Ka`.
6. **The older `thalor` example remains unresolved as a source witness.** Rejecting it as the modern outside term does not rewrite or silently reinterpret that sentence.
7. **The motion-goal use of `An` is an approved grammar clarification.** It is supported by motion examples, while the core source gloss remains “at/in.”
8. **No absolute directional system is included.** If Ohnosha has a stable planetary, celestial, magnetic, magical, or geographic orientation used in ordinary speech, that should become a later evidence-led proposal rather than being inferred here.

## Approved scope

The batch was approved in four coordinated units:

1. **Lateral frame:** `Naku`, `Sora`, the additive spatial sense of `Kor`, and their orientation rule. `Kordel` remains unlisted and narrow.
2. **Demonstratives:** bound `Den`, the three particles `Deni`, `Denya`, `Denla`, and their syntax.
3. **Interior/exterior:** `Anor` and `Kaan`; preserved source `thalor` is not promoted.
4. **Boundary motion:** `Anshanaen`, `Thalshanaen`, and the motion-goal clarification for `An`.

All four units are integrated. Approval of a root does not authorize unlisted derivatives.
