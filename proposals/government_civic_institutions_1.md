# Government and Civic Institutions — Proposal 1

**Status:** Approved for canonical integration on 2026-09-12  
**Scope:** Universal Celan vocabulary  
**Target layer:** Government, civic institutions, public authority, elections, administrative decisions, citizenship, jurisdictions, and formal agreements  
**Deferred to Pass 2:** Stewardship, apprenticeship, expertise, guilds, occupations, labor, ownership, buying, selling, wages, and profit  
**Integration state:** Approved entries are maintained in the app-facing expansion layer.

## Design commitments for this batch

1. Existing Celan meanings remain primary. This proposal does not replace, expose, or reinterpret an intentionally hidden root-analysis sense.
2. These meanings are universal rather than nationally owned. National origin and national usage should remain blank if approved.
3. No new dictionary category is proposed. Every entry uses an existing word type.
4. The unsmoothed form is the headword. Any alternate form shown is only the optional euphonic pronunciation and spelling.
5. Every written letter contributes to pronunciation. No spelling below depends on a silent consonant.
6. The active inventory includes both inherited roots and recently approved roots. This pass requires no new primitive root.
7. Approval would authorize only the listed headwords and meanings, not every possible derivative of their components.

### Protected `Khumlor` decision

The conceptual source material once illustrates `Khum + -lor` as “a circle / a council,” but the established dictionary headword `Khumlor` deliberately displays **chosen family, circle of belonging**. This proposal does not expose “council” as an added `Khumlor` sense. The civic body receives the separate form `Khumkel`, preserving both the displayed family meaning and the project's intentional root-description suppression.

## Meaning boundaries and contrast tests

| Pair | Required distinction |
|---|---|
| `Belor` / `Lorbel` | A `Belor` is any enduring organized institution. `Lorbel` is specifically the system of government. A library, guild, temple, or research body may be a `Belor` without being the government. |
| `Lorbel` / `Belthal` | `Lorbel` names the governing system and its administration. `Belthal` remains the broader condition or principle of social/systemic order. |
| `Lorbelaen` / `Varok` | To govern is to administer recognized public authority over time. `Varok` is the narrower act of commanding or directing and can occur outside government. |
| `Khumkel` / `Khumlor` | `Khumkel` is a deliberative civic or advisory body. `Khumlor` remains chosen family or a circle of belonging. |
| `Khumkelral` / `Lorbelral` | A representative speaks and decides for a constituency in a governing body. A public official holds an authorized administrative office and need not represent voters. One person may be both. |
| `Kalrin` / `Belvoklor` | `Kalrin` holds supreme authority over a state or polity. `Belvoklor` leads a clan or local people and is not automatically sovereign. |
| `Welkelthar` / `Keltharaen` / `Tharsharaen` | `Welkelthar` is the collective election process. `Keltharaen` is one person's formal act of voting. `Tharsharaen` remains ordinary deciding or choosing. |
| `Lorshara` / `Thalbel` / `Kalkel` | A policy guides recurring decisions; a law establishes a codified legal rule; a decree is a particular authoritative instruction. |
| `Belrinpel` / `Rinpel` | `Rinpel` is the seal, stamp, or authenticating impression. `Belrinpel` is the permit or authorization whose institutional force may be carried by such a seal. |
| `Shental` / `Weltal` | Allocation decides who or what receives measured portions. Distribution carries or divides those portions among recipients or destinations. Allocation may occur before distribution. |
| `Kelreth` / `Belreth` / `Reth` | `Kelreth` is an active dispute between claims or interests. `Belreth` is a bounded actionable problem. `Reth` is uncertainty, confusion, doubt, or “or.” |
| `Thalkelreth` / `Khumelaen` | Arbitration hears a defined dispute and seeks a binding determination. Reconciliation restores relationship and need not decide a legal or resource claim. |
| `Welrin` / `Rinshen` | `Welrin` is the person recognized as a citizen. `Rinshen` is an identifying record that may provide evidence of identity or status. |
| `Felbelor` / `Varthas` | A district is an administrative or functional subdivision. `Varthas` remains a city or town; a city may contain several districts. |
| `Lorzhir` / `Lianrethxar` / `Ndara` | `Lorzhir` divides jurisdictions. `Lianrethxar` bounds a reality anomaly. `Ndara` is a physical wall and need not coincide with either boundary. |
| `Kelvok` / `Nkuvath` | A contract states enforceable or formally recognized obligations. `Nkuvath` remains a promise or bond of trust and may be intimate, moral, or informal. |
| `Verkalkelaen` / `Ka` | A veto is an institutional act that blocks a public proposal through recognized authority. `Ka` remains ordinary refusal or withoutness. |
| `Kaweleth` / `Vethoreth` or `Xareth` imagery | Private means access is restricted to a person or defined group. It does not mean hidden, mysterious, shadowed, or physically covered. |

## Root-reuse review

No new primitive root is justified in this pass.

- **Institution and government:** `Bel` already carries systems, restrictions, civilization, and binding; `Lor` carries guidance and socially recognized direction. Their division gives `Belor` the broad institutional category and `Lorbel` the narrower governing system.
- **Council:** `Khum` supplies patient listening and reconciliation; `Kel` supplies ordered speech and definition. `Khumlor` was not reused because doing so would expose a deliberately suppressed source-analysis sense beneath an established displayed headword.
- **Authority roles:** `Kal`, `Rin`, `Lor`, `Belvok`, and the narrowly entrusted role element `Ral` support sovereign, clan leader, representative, and official without inventing nation-specific titles.
- **Election and voting:** `Wel`, `Kel`, and `Thar` already express community, formal expression, and will. `Tharshara` remains ordinary choice, while these compounds lexicalize collective civic procedure.
- **Policy, decree, and permit:** `Shara` gives an adopted course, `Kalkel` gives authority to a declared word, and `Rinpel` supplies the existing authorizing-seal concept. None requires a new permission primitive at this stage.
- **Allocation and distribution:** `Shen` distinguishes measured assignment; `Wel` distinguishes shared delivery; `Tal` supplies transfer in both.
- **Dispute and arbitration:** `Kel` turns broad uncertainty into competing stated claims; `Thal` adds the balancing procedure. `Khumelaen` was not reused because reconciliation repairs relationship rather than determining a claim.
- **Civic identity and territory:** `Wel + Rin` creates community-recognized identity, `Fel + Belor` creates an institutional subdivision, and `Lor + Zhir` creates the line of jurisdictional authority.
- **Contract and veto:** `Kel + Vok` distinguishes stated formal obligation from a personal promise. `Ver + Kalkel + -aen` makes veto an authoritative institutional negation rather than ordinary refusal.

## Cadence and lineage review

The batch uses Celan's established rhythmic contrasts rather than forcing a new sound family into a semantic field where it has no inherited support.

- Short civic anchors—`Belor`, `Lorbel`, `Kalkel`, `Shental`, `Weltal`, `Welrin`, `Lorzhir`, and `Kelvok`—keep frequent words compact.
- Longer forms occur where the meaning is institutionally layered: `Khumkelral`, `Belrinpel`, `Thalkelrethaen`, and `Verkalkelaen`.
- The recurring **l/r**, **k**, **w**, and open **a/e** movement connects the batch to established Celan without reducing it to Germanic-looking concatenation.
- `Khumkel`, `Belvoklor`, and `Welkelthar` carry distinct oral rhythms in a spoken civic sequence: “the council, the clan leader, the election.”
- Optional euphony is listed only where a consonant boundary may invite release. The unsmoothed construction remains primary.
- No `Mb-`, `Nk-`, `Tl-`, `Nd-`, or `Nj-` form is added cosmetically. Those lineages remain available when a later work, craft, or social meaning genuinely grows from them.

## Collision and family audit

- Exact headword scanning found no current dictionary collision for any of the 28 proposed primary spellings or seven optional euphonic forms.
- `Kalrin` is the one non-headword form with prior source evidence: it appears in the canonical phrase `kelka an Kalrin`, “a king's decree.” Admission would promote that attested form into a gender-neutral standalone ruler/sovereign headword; it would not alter the phrase.
- `Khumkel` avoids adding a second displayed sense to `Khumlor`.
- `Belrinpel` does not alter `Rinpel`; the physical/authenticating seal remains independently usable.
- `Kelvok` does not change the unusual established duality of `Vok`: noun `Vok` is unity, while `vokaen` breaks unity. The proposed noun compound is built from the noun-side bond meaning.
- `Weleth` and `Kaweleth` create a public/private contrast without redefining cover, shadow, secrecy, or mystery roots.
- No proposed derivative becomes an automatic productive family.

## Risks requiring explicit review

1. **`Kalrin` promotes a previously phrase-internal form.** Its source example translates it as “king,” while this proposal generalizes it to the gender-neutral office “ruler/sovereign.” Approve only if that broader title is a natural extension of power embodied in a person.
2. **`Khumkel` deliberately does not use the latent council analysis attached to `Khumlor`.** This preserves the established displayed meaning “chosen family, circle of belonging” and the intentional suppression policy. Approving `Khumkel` should not expose a second `Khumlor` sense.
3. **`Ral` remains role-bound, not a generic person suffix.** `Khumkelral` and `Lorbelral` are justified as entrusted caretakers of represented speech and public administration. Their approval would not authorize unrestricted `-ral` professions.
4. **`Belrinpel` is narrower than general permission.** It is a formal civic or institutional permit. Ordinary interpersonal permission remains a later information-and-communication question unless scene testing proves a phrase sufficient.
5. **`Thalkelreth` and `Thalkelrethaen` are the densest compounds in the batch.** They preserve the important distinction between arbitration and reconciliation, but should be rejected or shortened if their spoken rhythm feels too administrative for repeated use.
6. **`Kaweleth` must not acquire a moral meaning such as selfish, antisocial, or secretive.** Its boundary is access restricted to an individual or defined group.
7. **`Verkalkelaen` names institutional veto, not cancellation generally.** Ordinary refusal remains `Ka` or `Verka`; cancellation and repeal should be reviewed separately if needed.

## Approval boundary

Approval of this proposal would authorize exactly **28 universal headwords** and **seven optional euphonic variants**. It would not authorize:

- canonical integration before explicit approval;
- any national origin or national usage field;
- a new dictionary category;
- a second displayed definition for `Khumlor`;
- replacement of any existing displayed definition;
- unrestricted derivatives from `Ral`, `Wel`, `Bel`, `Kel`, or any other reused root;
- general permission, repeal, cancellation, taxation, courts, trials, policing, or punishment;
- or the deferred Pass 2 vocabulary for work, guilds, occupations, ownership, buying, selling, wages, and profit.
