# Food, Agriculture, and Preservation 2

Status: **Approved and integrated — 2026-09-11**

Scope: second of two food-category passes. Pass 1 covers food production, cultivated places, gathering, hunting, fishing, livestock, and gathered foods. This pass completes the system with staple processing, ingredients and recipes, cooking methods, preservation, food condition, and feeding.

All candidates are universal Celan. National origin and national usage remain blank. Proposed euphonic alternatives are optional searchable variants; every primary form is the unsmoothed construction.

## Proposed new primitives

Approval of this batch would authorize only the four listed roots and the explicitly listed derivatives, not automatic word families.

### `Saren` — wheat

Closest roots considered: `Lak` grain or bread material, `Morlak` bread or grain, `Bal` growth, and `Pralbal` cultivated crop.

Why they were not sufficient: these terms identify grain, food substance, growth, or crops generally. Wheat is a particular cereal with recurring agricultural, milling, baking, rationing, and trade importance. A stable species-level staple term preserves the difference between wheat and other grains without redefining `Lak`.

### `Olan` — oil

Closest roots considered: `Dren` liquid, `Verdvin` sap or plant extract, `Livar` preservation, and `Mek` artificial making.

Why they were not sufficient: oil may come from plants, animals, minerals, or manufacture and is not ordinary water, necessarily sap, inherently preservative, or always synthetic. `Olan` supplies a material class that supports edible oil here and oilskin, fuel, lubrication, cosmetics, and finishes in later reviewed vocabulary.

### `Mura` — smoke

Closest roots considered: `Krez` fire, `Esh` air, `Arshor` atmospheric envelopment, `Velarshor` fog, and `Fah` darkness.

Why they were not sufficient: smoke is a physical product of combustion, not fire itself, ordinary air, generic envelopment, water fog, or darkness. It recurs in cooking, preservation, fire safety, industry, weather, and signaling, so it merits a stable independent center.

### `Ruvan` — organic rot

Closest roots considered: `Livar` preservation, `Ka` absence, `Fah` darkness or fear, `Veth` hidden or sleeping processes, and `Rethvok` chaos.

Why they were not sufficient: rot is a positive physical process of uncontrolled organic decay, not merely the absence of preservation or a metaphorical form of darkness, hiddenness, or disorder. `Ruvan` distinguishes the process, its occurrence, and its result-state without converting `Livar` into a general life-and-death opposition.

## Root-reuse and collision review

1. `Saren` is specifically wheat. `Lak` remains grain as a general substance, `Morlak` remains bread or grain, and named breads and porridges remain particular foods.
2. `Fellak` is flour or meal after grinding. It does not mean intact grain, finished bread, or every fine powder. `Drenfellak` requires flour structured by added liquid; porridge, stew, paste, and batter remain distinguishable by composition and consistency.
3. `Olan` is the broad oil class. `Emilolan` is the edible subtype, while `Morolan` is fat in a normally solid or semi-solid state. Approval would not make every oil edible or every bodily fat an oil.
4. `Pralfel` is a deliberately selected component in a preparation. It does not replace `Fel` as the general diminisher or make every small object an ingredient.
5. `Emilshara` is a repeatable preparation path containing instructions and proportions. It does not replace `Shara` as a literal or figurative journey and is not the name of one specific dish.
6. `Emilpralaen` is the broad cooking verb. The method verbs remain narrower: `Xarkrezaen` retained enclosed heat, `Krezdrenaen` boiling liquid, `Eshkrezaen` exposed surrounding dry heat, `Pelkrezaen` concentrated direct heat, `Olankrezaen` hot oil or fat, and `Eshdrenaen` heated water vapor. Their lexical definitions supply the food domain without repeating `Emil` inside every everyday verb.
7. Baking and roasting deliberately form a pair. Both use sustained dry heat, but baking retains heat around food in an enclosure while roasting leaves a solid food exposed to radiating or circulating heat.
8. `Felthamaen` reduces material to particles. `Tham` remains the broader breakdown, mashing, or softening root, and `Pelvaraen` remains scraping a surface rather than grinding a substance throughout.
9. `Pelaen` lexicalizes ordinary cutting or slicing. It does not specify chopping, carving, sawing, stabbing, or a particular cutting tool.
10. `Drenpralaen` is broader than `Fennildren`, a tea or herbal infusion, and broader than any named fermented drink. It names the act of brewing rather than the resulting beverage; its lexical definition prevents it from meaning every act of making with water.
11. `Seldrenvinaen` specifies purification or concentration through vaporization and condensation. It does not replace ordinary cleaning, filtering, drawing liquid, or `Drosel` clean drinking water.
12. `Balemilaen` uses living growth specifically for controlled fermentation. Ordinary aging, sprouting, uncontrolled rot, and magical transformation do not become fermentation.
13. `Mura` remains distinct from `Velarshor` water fog and `Arshor` atmospheric envelopment. `Muraen` is deliberate exposure to smoke, not accidental smoke damage or the mere production of smoke.
14. `Vindrenaen` is an intentional moisture-extraction action. `Kadreneth` remains the condition of being dry or waterless, and `Feldrenaen` remains dripping.
15. `Livaraen` is the broad act of curing food through a controlled anti-decay treatment. It does not replace particular results such as `Drenlivar` salted fish or `Kreztorin` smoked and dried meat.
16. `Vellivaraen` is cold preservation as an action. The established `Velalivar` remains the noun “preservation through cold or dormancy”; the optional verb form preserves that familiar euphonic pattern without making it the new primary form.
17. `Zhaeleth` describes recent, retained original quality. It does not mean clean, young, newly invented, raw, or merely unspoiled.
18. `Ruvan`, `Ruvanaen`, and `Ruvaneth` distinguish organic decay as a thing, a process, and a resulting condition. They do not replace aging `Kav`, preservation `Livar`, injury `Aivkor`, or sickness `Mav`.
19. `Emilpraleth` and `Kaemilpraleth` are an explicit cooked/raw pair. Raw does not mean spoiled, unsafe, cold, or inedible; cooked does not mean warm, preserved, or ready to serve in every context.
20. `Talemilaen` is lexicalized because feeding recurs across childcare, hospitality, animal care, healing, and survival. `Tal` remains general giving and `Velaen` remains eating by the consumer.
21. Exact scanning found no current headword, stored variant, or preserved source witness matching any proposed primary or euphonic form.

## Risks requiring explicit review

1. `Saren` is a species-level staple root. Approving it does not automatically authorize names for every Earth cereal, but it does establish that frequently encountered cultivated species may earn independent Celan names.
2. `Olan` is shared with the clothing-and-materials review because oilskin depends on the same universal oil concept. It should be approved once as a single root, not counted independently in both batches.
3. The six cooking-method verbs deliberately encode contrasts through setting and medium: enclosure, boiling liquid, exposed dry heat, concentrated heat, oil, and steam. They should be scene-tested together so no pair differs only by its English label.
4. `Zhaeleth` derives “fresh” from bloom and renewal. Approve it only if freshness as retained recent vitality feels like a normal Celan metaphor for gathered and prepared food.
5. `Ruvan` is a new decay root rather than a negative form of `Livar`. This preserves rot as an active material process and avoids overloading preservation, but it also creates a root likely to recur later in ecology, medicine, sanitation, and metaphor.
6. `Emilpraleth` and `Kaemilpraleth` treat cooked and raw as a deliberate lexical pair. Neither term evaluates whether the food is culturally acceptable, safe, or complete.
