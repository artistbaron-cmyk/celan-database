# Tools and Machines 1

Status: **Approved and integrated — 2026-09-12**

Scope: first pass in the broader Tools, Machines, Transport, and Built Infrastructure expansion. This pass establishes general tools and machines, repair, common working instruments, measuring and navigation instruments, and foundational mechanical and energy-system parts. Transport and fixed infrastructure remain separate subsequent passes.

All candidates are universal Celan. National origin and national usage remain blank. Every primary form is the unsmoothed construction; listed alternatives are optional euphonic variants of the same entry and do not count as separate headwords.

## Root-reuse and collision review

1. No new primitive roots are proposed. The pass deliberately reuses both source-established roots and approved created roots, especially `Mek`, `Kar`, `Vek`, `Kes`, `Kren`, `Shan`, `Zhael`, `Bel`, `Pel`, `Jek`, `Rav`, `Shen`, and `Zhir`.
2. `Karmek` is one general working implement. `Karvek` remains a portable set or toolkit, `Kareth` remains a hammer, and `Karethaen` remains the act of using or wielding a tool.
3. `Kalmek` is the general machine. `Kormek` is specifically the engine or motor at the machine's functional core. A machine may contain no engine, and an engine may be one component of a larger machine.
4. `Felmek` is a constructed functional part. `Pralfel` remains an ingredient or selected component in a mixture and is not broadened into a machine part.
5. `Zhaelpralaen` uses renewal plus skilled making because `Mekral` already establishes the repair occupation without defining the repair action. It does not mean healing a body, renewing a ritual, or merely making a new object.
6. `Thamvek` and `Thamkar` lexicalize the complementary mortar-and-pestle pair. They do not replace the open serving vessel `Drenvor`, cooking vessels `Krezvek` and `Krezpral`, or the broad acts of mashing and stirring.
7. `Zhirpel`, `Pelkorin`, `Delpel`, `Delkorin`, and `Pelkar` distinguish tools by fine piercing, hole-making, paired cutting, paired gripping, and edge-driven shaping. `Peleth` remains the general knife or blade.
8. `Drenkavvek` is portable, closable, and narrow-opening. `Drenvor` remains an open bowl or cup, while `Kavvek` remains the broader closable jar used for food, liquid, or small materials.
9. `Krezdrenvek` is designed for heating and controlled pouring. It does not replace a cooking pot, drinking cup, or generic liquid vessel.
10. `Rinpel` ties authentication to identity rather than treating every mark as a seal. Approval would lexicalize both the authorizing impression and its specialized implement as one closely connected ordinary noun.
11. `Kelpel` and `Kelpral` extend the established speech, naming, and inscription family into a writing instrument and information-bearing surface. They do not define writing itself or replace books, scrolls, archives, or future electronic displays.
12. `Shenzhir`, `Shenmek`, and `Shenlianaen` distinguish a calibrated length tool, a system-state measuring instrument, and the action of bringing an instrument into agreement with a standard. `Lianrethshen` remains a specific anomaly reading.
13. `Lormek` indicates direction; it is not a map. `Sharashen` represents spatial relations; it is not the territory, route, chart, or act of navigation.
14. `Normek` measures or displays time. It does not replace `Nor` time, calendars, schedules, or alarms.
15. `Belpralmek` is a weaving apparatus and `Jekzhirvek` is a wound-line holder. Neither is licensed merely because any woven object or coil is mechanically possible.
16. `Kesmek` detects a condition without human awareness. `Kes` remains a person's act of noticing or registering something in awareness.
17. `Zhelkor` produces usable energy, `Zhelkav` stores it, and `Zhelbel` distributes it. These three functions remain deliberately separate.
18. `Shanmek` is an enclosed constructed passage. `Awekshara` remains specifically a spring-fed canal and `Drobel` remains a waterworks system.
19. `Krenzhir` is metallic wire or conductive cable. `Zhirin` remains rope or cord, `Zhirbel` remains a securing strap, and `Belzhir` remains a communications network.
20. `Selshan` and `Selshanaen` concern selective physical passage and removal. They do not automatically include censorship, social exclusion, or software-data filtering.
21. `Ravshan` uses the physical drawing sense of `Rav`; it does not turn the pump into a metaphor for desire. `Belshan` controls a passage and does not replace the general open/close verbs `Kaxaraen` and `Xaraen`.
22. `Wekmek`, `Korwek`, `Jekmek`, `Ravkar`, `Wekrav`, and `Jekkal` distinguish wheel, axle, gear, lever, pulley, and mechanical spring by their physical functions. `Jekkal` does not replace `Awek` natural water spring or `Zhaelor` the blooming season.
23. Exact scanning found no current app headword or stored variant matching any proposed primary or euphonic form.

## Risks requiring explicit review

1. `Karmek` and established `Karvek` differ by one consonant and would occur in the same practical conversations. Approve `Karmek` only if the compact tool/toolkit contrast is useful enough to justify that phonetic proximity.
2. `Kalmek` and `Kormek` establish the machine/engine distinction. They should be reviewed together so `Kor` retains its functional-core meaning and `Kal` retains broad transmitted power.
3. `Zhaelpralaen` is longer than several ordinary Celan verbs. Its roots are honest and its meaning is broad across crafts, but a future shortening would require a separate lexical decision rather than silently clipping the approved form.
4. `Rinpel` intentionally treats an authorizing impression and the specialized implement producing it as one ordinary noun. Split them before approval if Celan should distinguish stamp-tool from seal-mark in normal speech.
5. `Kelpel` and `Kelpral` extend `Kel` from speech and naming into physical inscription and displayed information. This should be accepted only if writing is understood as durable speech within Celan metaphor.
6. `Selshan` is limited here to physical material passage. Digital, social, legal, and cognitive filtering remain outside this meaning unless later approved.
7. `Shanmek` spans pipes for liquids and gases as well as protective conduits for energy or lines. If that feels too broad, it should split into fluid pipe and technical conduit before approval.
8. `Krenzhir` covers both individual wire and bundled conductive cable. A separate cable word may be warranted during transport and infrastructure review if cable-supported structures and communications require the distinction often.
9. `Korwek` follows the approved special euphony rule for `Wek` as the second root: only `Korewek` and `Korowek` are proposed, never `Koravek` or `Korawek`.
10. This pass does not approve transport or infrastructure vocabulary. It supplies the mechanical foundation those later passes will reuse without automatically licensing vehicle, road, bridge, building, or utility compounds.
