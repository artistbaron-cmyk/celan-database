# Environment, Geography, and Ecology

## Combined Lexical Review Report — Passes 1 and 2

**Status:** Approved and integrated  
**Scope:** Universal Celan vocabulary; no national origins assigned  
**Canon effect:** 72 new headwords and 72 displayed senses  
**Current dictionary baseline:** 1,113 headwords and 1,191 displayed senses  
**Dictionary after integration:** 1,185 headwords and 1,263 displayed senses  
**Review method:** Whole Canon Dictionary reconciliation, active-root reuse, exact-headword collision check, hidden-root policy review, Ohnoshan Metaphor Test, conversational distinction, cadence testing, register control, and headword-admission review

## Executive finding

Celan already describes several prominent features of Ohnosha: earth, soil, mountains, stone, rivers, oceans, lakes, springs, islands, forests, cultivated land, rain, snow, storms, fog, seasons, animals, and many named species. The missing layer is the connective environmental vocabulary that allows speakers to describe an ordinary landscape as a system.

The proposal therefore does not rename existing terrain or reproduce the eight nations' proper names. It adds the language needed to say what kind of place something is, what lives there, how air and water behave, what environmental damage is occurring, and whether resources are abundant, scarce, protected, or wasted.

- **Pass 1 — Physical Environment and Climate:** 32 approved headwords.
- **Pass 2 — Living Ecology, Damage, and Resources:** 40 approved headwords.
- **New primitives:** `Mbu` (fungal life), `Nali` (species), and `Sul` (resource or usable supply).
- **Latent-root promotion:** `Gorm` (dirt or grime), already present inside established `Gormeth`.
- **No added senses to existing headwords.**
- **No new dictionary categories.**

The candidates are universal. A marsh in Pelagae, a mountain pass in Nivveil, a polluted canal in Mechuma, and a scarce ore supply in Marakor use the same core vocabulary. Nation-specific cultural terms may later compound with these words, but the environmental headwords themselves receive no nation of origin.

## Register and length decision

Common landscape words remain short where the existing roots permit it. Longer forms are retained only when their visible components prevent ambiguity in scientific, civic, or emergency use.

| Register | Function | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Common | Frequent description of terrain, weather, plants, animals, and supplies | `Felmorl`, hill; `Drenfel`, stream; `Nali`, species; `Sul`, resource |
| Technical | A distinction whose internal structure matters to specialists or public institutions | `Eshweknor`, climate; `Rathshannali`, invasive species; `Xilnalior`, habitat fragmentation |
| Transparent phrase | Predictable action that does not yet justify another long derivative | extract a `Sul`; measure `Eshnor`; protect a `Nalior`; remove `Versul` |

This is a register tendency, not a new grammatical rule.

## Pass 1 semantic separations

- `Felmorl` is a smaller raised landform; `Morl` remains mountain without an imposed numerical height boundary.
- `Pelmorl` is an exposed steep face. `Morlshara` is a route across mountain terrain, and `Shanmorl` is the natural open trench-like landform through which movement may occur.
- `Shanmorl` does not replace `Morlshan`, the established built or enclosed tunnel. Root order preserves the distinction: passage-shaped stone land versus a stone-built passage.
- `Kadromor` names a persistent arid region. `Kadro` names a time-bounded shortage event that can affect deserts, forests, farms, or cities.
- `Drenverd` is the general ecological class. Marsh, swamp, and bog may be distinguished descriptively by vegetation, water movement, or soil rather than receiving three premature near-synonyms.
- `Mahrmorl` is the submerged geographic structure. `Neshmorl` remains coral as a biological or material substance.
- `Drenjor` is a traveling surface disturbance; `Mahrwel` is sustained directional flow within water.
- `Eshnor` is current or short-term weather. `Eshweknor` is the long-duration recurring pattern called climate.
- `Drenalor` remains rain; `Drenesh` is the cloud that may or may not produce it.
- `Dralorim` remains the general storm. `Eshjor`, `Sadarjor`, and existing `Eshvelaneth` identify a dangerous dominant medium: wind, sand, or hail.
- `Velxar` is a surface coating. `Velk` is ice generally, `Eshvelor` is snowfall, and `Velmav` from the health expansion is systemic cold injury.
- `Terradren` remains fertile wet soil; `Welmor` already covers mud; `Felsadar` is fine mineral sediment deposited or carried by water.
- `Krezshen` is a general thermal measurement despite using the heat root. Its measured value may be hot, temperate, or cold; `Krez` supplies the physical dimension being measured rather than a high reading.

## Pass 2 semantic separations

- `Verd` remains botanical life as a domain; `Verdin` is one plant; `Verdmarin` is the narrower woody branching form called a tree.
- `Morlzhir` is the biological plant structure. It does not replace abstract root-source metaphors or the general physical thread `Zhir`.
- `Balvek` is the reproductive growth carrier called a seed. `Balshan` is the transfer process of pollination; it does not mean seed dispersal in general.
- `Mbu` is neither `Verd` nor `Zor`. This distinction lets Celan discuss mushrooms, molds, yeasts, decomposition, medicine, and food without misclassifying fungi as plants.
- `Shalrin` is one living organism. `Nali` is its species or biological kind. `Welnali` is the local population of one species. `Ohmshal` includes many organisms plus the nonliving environment and their interactions.
- `Nalior` is where a species ordinarily lives and meets its needs. It can be a forest, reef, city structure, body of water, or other environment; it is not simply a house or geographic region.
- `Kornali` and `Shannali` are historical-range terms, not moral judgments. `Rathshannali` adds the necessary finding of ecological harm; not every introduced species is invasive.
- `Rathnali` means the species is in danger. `Dumanali` means the entire species has ended. Neither is ordinary individual injury or death.
- `Gavin` is defined by being pursued in the present hunt or feeding relation. `Vershara` remains an established specific prey-animal class, and `Gavral` remains the hunter.
- `Rathzor` is the ecological role of predator. `Vethral` remains a particular pack-hunter class, while `Kalrath` remains an apex predator.
- `Welzor` deliberately serves as the universal animal-group umbrella. Context, species names, or descriptive compounds distinguish herd, flock, school, pack, and swarm when that precision matters.
- `Zornethor` covers animal shelters across species. The exact structure—nest, den, burrow, lair—can be clarified through animal and material context before separate words are admitted.
- `Emilzhir` is linear and directional; `Emilbel` is a network with intersecting feeding relationships.
- `Ruvan` is rot within organic matter. `Mavverd` is plant disease. `Gorm` is unwanted surface dirt. `Welgorm` is environmental pollution dispersed through shared surroundings. `Terramav` is the broader loss of ecological health or function.
- `Pelnor` is the physical wearing-away process. `Terramav` may be a result of erosion but is not synonymous with it.
- `Terrakav` protects what remains; `Terrazhael` actively assists recovery after damage.
- `Sul` is a usable supply of any kind, not merely money, raw material, or natural wealth. Existing allocation words describe how a resource is distributed.
- `Worsul`, `Kasul`, and `Kavsul` distinguish present abundance, present insufficiency, and deliberately preserved future supply.
- `Weksul` names replenishment through a usable cycle; it does not mean inexhaustible.
- `Versul` is socially treated as discarded or unusable material. Reuse can return `Versul` to `Sul`, which gives Celan a natural cultural metaphor for recovery of value.

## Primitive and latent-root review

### `Mbu` — fungal life

Fungi are a basic living category with roles in food, medicine, decay, soil, disease, and ecological exchange. Building the meaning from `Verd` would canonically classify fungi as plants; building it from `Ruvan` would define all fungi through rot or harm. Neither is accurate. `Mbu` supplies a compact, strongly articulated Ohnoshan root without forcing the familiar `Mb-`, `Nk-`, or `Tl-` patterns onto every new word.

Approval would authorize only the meaning “fungus or fungal life.” It would not automatically authorize a larger `Mbu` family.

### `Nali` — species

No existing root distinguishes an individual living being from a recognized biological kind extending across many individuals and generations. `Zor` excludes plants and fungi; `Verd` excludes animals; `Wel` supplies collectivity but not shared biological identity. A two-syllable primitive is justified because species is the organizing unit for habitat, population, native range, introduction, endangerment, and extinction.

Approval would authorize `Nali` and only the explicitly listed compounds in this report.

### `Sul` — resource or usable supply

Celan can already express possession, allocation, abundance, and particular substances. It lacks the higher-level notion that water, food, timber, ore, medicine, energy, time, or stored goods are available supplies usable toward a need. Deriving this entirely from trade or ownership would erase communal and ecological resources. `Sul` therefore earns a compact primitive because it is frequent, universal, and highly productive across civic and environmental life.

Approval would authorize `Sul` and only `Worsul`, `Kasul`, `Kavsul`, `Weksul`, and `Versul` from this report.

### `Gorm` — latent-root promotion

`Gorm` is not a newly invented semantic base. The established word `Gormeth`, “dirty or soiled,” is already analyzed in the source material through `Gorm`, grime. Promoting `Gorm` to a visible noun gives the existing adjective an explicit base and permits the transparent environmental compound `Welgorm` without changing or exposing any unrelated hidden root-analysis sense.
