# Transportation and Vehicle Operations 1

Status: **Approved and integrated — 2026-09-12**

Scope: universal vocabulary for ordinary vehicles, vehicle classes, travel operations, motion control, transported people and goods, and shared-route movement. This pass is intentionally separate from fixed built infrastructure: roads, bridges, stations, platforms, garages, tunnels, and transit terminals remain for the infrastructure pass.

Every primary form is the unsmoothed construction. Any listed alternative is only an optional euphonic pronunciation and spelling of that same headword. National origin and national usage remain blank because the meanings are universal across Ohnosha.

## Existing vocabulary deliberately retained

This pass does not rename concepts Celan already handles:

| Existing word | Retained coverage |
|---|---|
| `Mahrvek` | boat, ship, or sea vessel |
| `Sharavok` | caravan or traveling company; the group is not a single vehicle |
| `Tharokh`, `Harven` | culturally established riding and pack animals |
| `Var`, `Min` | general going or walking without specifying a conveyance |
| `Vekaen` | carrying or transporting an object, creature, or person |
| `Shara` | path, journey, or route in general |
| `Jekaen` | ordinary turning, including turning a vehicle when context is sufficient |
| `Dar` | waiting or holding temporarily; it can be used for an ordinary stop without creating a separate transport verb |
| `Zhaelpralaen` | repairing a damaged vehicle or transport system |
| `Kormek`, `Wekmek`, `Korwek`, `Krenzhir` | engine, wheel, axle, and cable as machine components rather than vehicle classes |

## Root-reuse and collision review

1. No new primitive roots are proposed. The pass deliberately depends on the enlarged approved inventory, especially `Vek`, `Var`, `Shara`, `Kormek`, `Wek`, `Zhir`, `Lor`, `Esh`, `Mahr`, `Dor`, `Jor`, `Vel`, `Bel`, and `Rav`.
2. `Varvek` is the general vehicle. It was chosen instead of `Sharavek` because that form would sit too close to established `Sharavok` “caravan” in both sound and transport conversation.
3. `Felvek`, `Morvek`, and `Pralvek` distinguish a small cart, a substantial cargo wagon, and a runner-supported sled by size/use, grounded load, and support surface. Approval does not make `Fel`, `Mor`, or `Pral` automatic vehicle classifiers outside the listed words.
4. `Kormekvek` is a general engine-driven land vehicle. “Car” and “jeep” are treated as body-style and use differences inside that class rather than forced into separate basic headwords before Ohnosha-specific vehicle designs are fully documented.
5. `Delkormek` and `Delwek` are a powered two-wheeler and a rider-powered two-wheeler. `Delwek` follows the approved special euphony rule for second-root `Wek`: only `Delewek` and `Delowek` are offered, never an `-awek` form.
6. `Zhirvek` is one rail vehicle; `Belzhirvek` is the linked operating unit; `Zhirshara` is the fixed route itself. This keeps vehicle, train, and railway distinct in instructions and reports.
7. `Shalilvek` makes the established idea “move lightly” concrete as a low-resistance conveyance across more than one medium. It is not restricted to aircraft and does not replace `Eshvek`.
8. `Eshvek`, `Mahrxarvek`, `Krenzhirvek`, and `Mahrpral` distinguish air vehicle, enclosed underwater vehicle, cable-supported vehicle, and flat open watercraft. Established `Mahrvek` remains the general boat or ship.
9. `Vekvaraen` marks being carried in travel. It does not mean walking, transporting cargo by hand, or operating the vehicle.
10. `Kormekaen` focuses on powered operation; `Lorvekaen` focuses on directional control. A driver may do both, while a person steering a sailboat or mount need not operate an engine.
11. `Mahrvaraen` and `Eshvaraen` are medium-specific travel verbs. The first includes wind, current, and powered sailing; the second includes creatures as well as vehicles because flying is a mode of movement, not solely a machine operation.
12. `Anvekaen` and `Thalvekaen` form the board/disembark pair. They apply to vehicles, vessels, and transport animals, but not to entering or leaving an ordinary room.
13. `Weksharaen` requires a repeated regular route. One repeated trip or an occasional journey does not automatically count as commuting.
14. `Dorvekaen` covers both parking a land vehicle and berthing a vessel because its core distinction is deliberate placement at rest. It does not name the parking or berthing location.
15. `Jorvar`, `Jorvaraen`, and `Velvaraen` distinguish speed, increasing speed, and reducing speed. `Veleth` remains the broader adjective “cold, cool, or slow,” while `Jor` remains a surge or burst.
16. `Belvekaen` is controlled mechanical resistance, not merely stopping. Ordinary stopping can still be expressed with established motion and waiting language when no brake action is intended.
17. `Ravvekaen` uses the approved physical-pull sense of `Rav`; it does not broaden emotional desire into transportation.
18. `Vekmor`, `Vekin`, `Lorvekral`, and `Eshlorral` separate what is carried, a carried person, a general transport controller, and a specialist navigating an aerial or three-dimensional vehicle.
19. `Welvek` is the interacting collective flow, not simply a large number of parked vehicles. `Felshara` is one designated lane, not an entire road, rail system, or sea route.
20. Exact scanning found no current app headword or stored variant matching any proposed primary or euphonic form.

## Coverage tests

- “The driver slowed the wagon, steered into the left lane, and parked beside the gate.” Covered by `Lorvekral`, `Velvaraen`, `Morvek`, `Lorvekaen`, `Felshara`, and `Dorvekaen`.
- “We two boarded the train, but the passengers disembarked when the rail was found damaged.” Covered by `Anvekaen`, `Belzhirvek`, `Vekin`, `Thalvekaen`, and `Zhirshara`.
- “Tow the broken jeep; its engine works, but its brakes do not.” Covered by `Ravvekaen`, `Kormekvek`, `Kormek`, and `Belvekaen`.
- “The pilot accelerated the aircraft above the storm while the cable cars stopped below.” Covered by `Eshlorral`, `Jorvaraen`, `Eshvek`, and `Krenzhirvek`, with existing aspect and location grammar.
- “She commutes by bicycle, while her sibling rides the bus.” The batch covers the recurring travel and bicycle with `Weksharaen` and `Delwek`, and the general riding relation with `Vekvaraen`; a bus remains a contextual subtype of `Kormekvek` unless public-transit design later proves a separate headword necessary.

## Risks requiring explicit review

1. `Varvek` extends `Vek` from carrier/vessel into the broad modern category “vehicle.” Approve it only if a constructed travel-carrier feels like the normal Celan abstraction across carts, cars, aircraft, and other conveyances.
2. `Felvek` and `Morvek` depend on lexicalized whole-word meanings. Approval authorizes cart and wagon only; it does not automatically make every small carrier a cart or every material carrier a wagon.
3. `Kormekvek` deliberately groups car and jeep. If Ohnosha routinely distinguishes road cars from rugged field vehicles in ordinary speech, that split should happen before approval rather than through unreviewed compounds later.
4. `Shalilvek` turns an established imperative and manner expression into a transport noun. Approve it only if “light-flow carrier” feels natural for cross-medium gliders and boards.
5. `Kormekaen` includes powered machine operation as well as vehicle driving. This is useful for a technical culture but broader than English “drive”; narrow it before approval if Celan should reserve the word for conveyances.
6. `Mahrvaraen` includes powered water travel as well as wind-driven sailing. The distinction here is travel by vessel on water, not sailcloth as the motive technology.
7. `Eshvaraen` applies to living creatures and vehicles. This is intentional because the universal action is airborne movement; pilotage remains separately expressed.
8. `Vekin` uses an attested person-forming `-in`, but it is a compact lexicalization rather than a fully transparent agent compound. Its meaning must remain passenger/rider and must not replace identity-based meanings of `Rin` or `Theon`.
9. `Eshlorral` covers pilots of aircraft and other specialized vehicles moving through three-dimensional space. Ordinary ship navigation remains within `Lorvekral` unless later scene testing proves a separate navigator term necessary.
10. No terms here are approved merely by appearing in this proposal. Integration, examples, editorial policy, and generated dictionary files must wait for explicit human approval.

## Reserved for the next infrastructure pass

Road, bridge, station, platform, terminal, stop, dock, harbor, garage, parking area, tunnel, transit tube, stairway, elevator, cable support tower, canal, aqueduct, reservoir, and other fixed built systems remain unapproved here.

`Zhirsharaen` was discussed as a possible verb meaning “to travel by rail” and explicitly declined. It is not approved or added by this batch; ordinary rail travel remains expressible with established motion syntax plus `Zhirshara` or `Belzhirvek`.
