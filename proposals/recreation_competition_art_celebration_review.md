# Recreation, Competition, Art, and Celebration

## Combined Lexical Review Report — Passes 1 and 2

**Status:** Approved and integrated  
**Scope:** Universal Celan vocabulary  
**Canon effect:** 47 added headwords and 48 added displayed senses  
**Dictionary baseline:** 1,022 headwords and 1,099 displayed senses  
**Dictionary after integration:** 1,069 headwords and 1,147 displayed senses  
**Review method:** Canon Dictionary audit, active-root reuse, collision review, Ohnoshan Metaphor Test, cadence testing, and headword-admission review

## Executive finding

Celan can already describe movement, skill, joy, communal food, ritual, writing, and crafted objects, but it cannot yet discuss recreation and the arts as an ordinary connected system. Speakers can say that someone moves quickly, succeeds, rejoices, makes something, or speaks a book. They cannot yet cleanly distinguish playing from competing, a game from a sport, a race from ordinary fast travel, music from speech, a song from a story, art from useful craft, or a festival from a feast.

The proposed solution contains two review passes:

- **Pass 1 — Recreation and Competition:** 22 proposed headwords.
- **Pass 2 — Performance, Story Arts, and Celebration:** 26 proposed dictionary entries, consisting of 25 new headwords and one added sense of existing `Thael`.

The approved and integrated dictionary effect is:

- **47 additional headwords**
- **48 additional displayed senses**
- **2 new primitive roots:** `Dov` and `Nak`
- **1 latent-root promotion:** `Rim`
- **1 source-defined compound promoted to ordinary vocabulary:** `Aennor`
- **1 controlled additive sense:** `Thael` as a Noun meaning rhythm

These figures were verified after rebuilding the Canon Dictionary.

# Pass 1 — Recreation and Competition

## Capability gaps

Pass 1 addresses the inability to distinguish:

- recreation from work or accidental movement;
- unstructured play from a rule-bounded game;
- games from physical sports;
- an ordinary attempt from an outcome-comparing competition;
- competitors, teams, opponents, officials, rules, and prizes;
- winning, losing, and drawing from generic success or failure;
- racing from ordinary speed;
- skating and surfing from generic riding or travel.

## Primitive-root decision

### `Dov` — play and recreation

The closest roots were `Vial` joy, `Siv` attempt, `Wek` repetition, and `Pral` skilled activity. None names voluntary activity pursued primarily for enjoyment. Using any of them would define play as an emotion, an uncertain attempt, repetition, or work. A new primitive is therefore justified.

### `Nak` — competition

The closest roots were `Siv` attempt, `Shen` measurement, `Thal` reciprocal balance, and `Rath` hostility. Competition combines comparison, opposed striving, and a recognized outcome without necessarily being hostile. No existing root carries that semantic center without distortion. A new primitive is therefore justified.

Approval of either primitive would authorize only the headwords explicitly listed in this report, not an automatic derivative family.

## Pass 1 semantic boundaries

- `Dov` does not mean happiness; the emotional state remains `Vial`.
- `Nak` does not mean hostility, war, or dispute. Those remain `Rath`, `Rathnor`, and `Kelreth` families.
- `Shalnak` and `Vethnak` are competitive outcomes. They do not replace generic `Sivathaen` and `Sivrethaen`.
- `Sivvok` may describe teams outside sports because its center is a group united around a shared effort. It does not mean family, clan, army, or permanent institution.
- `Nakshara` governs a competition or game. It is not civic law, public policy, or life-path destiny.
- `Naklorral` uses `Ral` for an entrusted practiced role, consistent with current policy; it does not authorize unrestricted `-ral` derivation.
- `Trakshalilaen` replaces the earlier draft's ice-only `Velshalilaen`. The generic action is defined through foot-borne gliding, while `Pelveltrakor` remains the specific ice-skate object.

## Pass 1 Ohnoshan metaphor findings

The result family passes the Ohnoshan Metaphor Test as a matched system:

- `Shal` supplies forward or light-facing position: victory.
- `Veth` supplies the behind or shaded position: defeat.
- `Thal` supplies equilibrium: a draw.

This continues the established front/behind orientation already used by `Shalvar`, `Vethvar`, `Shalnor`, and `Vethnor`. The three terms should be approved, revised, or rejected together.

# Pass 2 — Performance, Story Arts, and Celebration

## Capability gaps

Pass 2 addresses the inability to distinguish:

- music from speech or undifferentiated sound;
- a song from music generally;
- singing, dancing, and drumming as ordinary activities;
- rhythm, melody, and chorus;
- stories from the books or scrolls that may contain them;
- expressive art from skilled useful craft;
- a live performance, performer, audience, and stage;
- celebration and festival from joy, ritual, or a shared feast.

## Root-reuse decisions

### Music uses canonical `Aen + Nor`

The conceptual-root canon already records `Aen + Nor = Music`. `Aennor` is therefore an admitted source-defined compound, not a newly invented root or an added sense of `Aen`.

### Rhythm and dance use `Thael`

`Thael` already denotes gentle motion and breeze-like rhythm. Dance is deliberate bodily movement organized by that rhythm. The report proposes a new ordinary Noun sense for `Thael`, while preserving its existing displayed Adjective/root description unchanged.

### Celebration uses latent `Rim`

Both established analyses of `Welrim` identify `Rim` as celebration. Promoting `Rim` recovers a latent canonical component and allows speakers to distinguish celebration generally from `Welrim`, the specifically food-centered shared feast.

### No additional primitive is needed

Story, art, performance, audience, song, melody, dance, and drumming can be expressed through semantically direct combinations of `Aen`, `Nor`, `Thael`, `Zhir`, `Kel`, `Rin`, `Pral`, `Khum`, `Kes`, and `Kar`. Inventing new primitives for these concepts would ignore productive material already present in Celan.

## Pass 2 semantic boundaries

- `Aennor` means intentionally organized sound through time. Ordinary noise, speech, breath, and hearing remain in the established `Aen` family.
- `Aenthael` is a song, not every spoken poem, chant, call, or piece of music.
- `Thael` as rhythm does not replace its existing gentle-motion and breeze-like description. Approval must preserve both displayed meanings.
- `Karthael` is an instrument defined by rhythmic striking. It does not add an instrument sense to `Kareth`, the hammer or striking tool.
- `Zhirkel` is narrative content. `Lianbelrin` remains the book or scroll that may contain a story.
- `Rinpral` distinguishes expressive art from `Pral` craftsmanship generally. Useful craft can be artistic, but it is not automatically `Rinpral` unless expressive form is salient.
- `Norpral` requires live or unfolding presentation. A completed physical artwork remains `Rinpral`.
- `Khumkes` names people gathered in shared attention; it does not mean a council, family circle, crowd generally, or the abstract public.
- `Rim` is celebration generally; `Welrim` remains a shared feast and `Khumeth` remains a sacred ritual or ceremony.
- Every `Ral` compound in this pass names a genuine practiced performance role. The batch does not authorize unrestricted agent-noun creation with `Ral`.

## Forms rejected as unnecessary or misleading

| Rejected approach | Reason |
|---|---|
| Add “song” directly to `Aen` | Would overload speech, breath, sound, and song in one high-frequency word |
| Add “story” to `Lianbelrin` | Confuses narrative content with its physical or recorded medium |
| Use `Pral` alone for art | Erases the distinction between expressive art and skilled useful craft |
| Add “drum” to `Kareth` | Treats an instrument as merely a striking tool and weakens both categories |
| Add “celebration” to `Welrim` | Makes every celebration a feast and prevents non-food celebrations |
| Use `Vial` for celebration | Joy is an emotional state; celebration is an observable social action or event |
| Invent a new root for music | Canon already provides `Aen + Nor = Music` |
| Invent separate roots for song, dance, story, or art | Existing roots express their semantic structures without distortion |

# Cadence and scene testing

The candidates were tested in mixed phrases rather than only as isolated dictionary forms:

```text
Dovaen felka dovath ser dovin.
The child plays a game with the player.

Nakaen sivvok ser thalnakin an jornak.
The team competes against its opponent in the race.

Trakshalilaen Lalin an velk; shalnakaen ser jornak.
They skate on the ice; the friend wins the race.

Aennoraen aennorral aennor; aenthaelaen welaenthael servok.
The musician plays music; the chorus sings together.

Karthaelaen aennorral karthael; thaelaen thaelral an norpralor.
The musician drums; the dancer dances on the stage.

Aen zhirkelral zhirkel an khumkes.
The storyteller tells a story to the audience.

Norpralaen norpralral rinpral nor rimor.
The performer presents art during the festival.

Rimaen Ilin shalnak; rinaen welrim an welpralor.
We celebrate the victory; the feast is in the public square.
```

The combined set preserves Celan's alternating rhythmic attack and vowel release. `Nak`, `Dov`, `Kar`, `Trak`, and `Zhir` give compact percussive anchors; `Aennor`, `Thael`, `Shalil`, `Rimaen`, and the optional euphonic forms provide open continuation. No silent letters or unpronounced compound components are proposed.

# Cross-national adequacy

The meanings remain universal, but the system supports the practices that exposed the gaps:

- ice racing, skating, hearthside songs, and storytelling;
- mountain tournaments, public drums, wearable art, and martial demonstrations;
- desert fire dances, caravan stories, and night celebrations;
- forest choruses, shaping competitions, and living sculpture;
- current-gliding races, floating stages, music, and ocean festivals;
- invention competitions, public exhibitions, and technological performance;
- courtyard music, passage trials, and civic festivals;
- logic contests, board games, ascension trials, and formal public presentation.

Because the words describe recurring needs across Ohnosha, no candidate receives a national origin or national-usage field.

# Collision and integrity findings

- No proposed new spelling collides with an existing Canon Dictionary headword.
- `Thael` is the only intentional same-headword case. Its proposed Noun sense must be additive.
- No existing definition is proposed for replacement, deprecation, or hiding.
- `Aennor` and `Rim` recover relationships explicitly present in source analyses rather than manufacturing new etymologies.
- `Dov` and `Nak` passed the two-syllable primitive limit and the active-root necessity review.
- The result terms do not weaken generic success and failure.
- The art terms do not redefine every useful craft as art.
- The story term does not alter the meaning of book, scroll, text, or historical memory.
- The celebration terms preserve the distinctions among joy, ritual, feast, celebration, and festival.

# Approved decisions

1. **`Dov` is approved as the primitive for play and recreation.** Its listed family is limited to `Dov`, `Dovaen`, `Dovath`, `Dovin`, and `Kaldov`.
2. **`Nak` is approved as the primitive for competition.** Its listed family and compounds are limited to the Pass 1 entries in this report.
3. **`Shalnak`, `Vethnak`, and `Thalnak` form one approved metaphorical result system.**
4. **The revision from `Velshalilaen` to `Trakshalilaen` is approved.** The latter means skating generally; ice is supplied by equipment or scene context.
5. **`Aennor` is approved as the ordinary headword realization of canonical `Aen + Nor = Music`.** This adds no new primitive.
6. **The additive Noun sense of `Thael` is approved with its existing gentle-motion/breeze-like meaning displayed unchanged.** This is a controlled sense-preservation exception.
7. **`Rim` is approved as a latent-root promotion from established `Welrim`.** `Rim` means celebration, while `Welrim` remains feast.
8. **The seven `Ral` performance-role compounds are approved as practiced roles.** Their approval does not authorize additional automatic `Ral` formations.
