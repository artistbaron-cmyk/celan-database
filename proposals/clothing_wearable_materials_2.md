# Clothing, Wearable Protection, and Ordinary Materials 2

Status: **Candidate review — not approved; not in canon**

Scope: second material pass. This pass distinguishes common architectural stones, sand, specific metals, skeletal and marine materials, wax, resin, colorants, and alloy. It excludes materials already covered by `Morl` stone, `Mormek` worked stone, `Kren` metal, `Tir` copper, `Tirkal` bronze, `Lem` clay, `Lemath` ceramic, `Selmek` glass, `Zem` wood, `Rask` hide, `Mekrask` leather, `Olan` oil, and `Morolan` fat.

All candidates are universal Celan. National origin and national usage remain blank. Proposed euphonic alternatives are optional searchable variants; every primary form is the unsmoothed construction.

## Primitive and promoted-root review

Approval of this pass would authorize only `Sadar`, `Eran`, `Osar`, `Dresh`, and `Hir` with the meanings and derivatives explicitly listed here. It would not authorize automatic families.

### `Sadar` — sand

Closest roots considered: `Mor` matter or soil, `Morl` stone, `Fel` smallness, and `Xil` fragments.

Why they were not sufficient: sand is not simply small matter, any fragmented substance, or a collection of small stones. It is a recurring mineral material with characteristic granular flow, erosion, construction use, dunes, storms, and filtration behavior. A stable sand root will also support later environmental vocabulary without forcing “small stone” into every expression.

### `Eran` — iron

Closest roots considered: `Kren` generic metal, `Tir` copper, `Kal` strength, and `Morl` endurance.

Why they were not sufficient: iron is a chemically and materially specific metal, not merely strong metal. `Kren` already provides the category and `Tir` identifies a different metal. A dedicated root preserves ordinary distinctions among iron, copper, bronze, generic metal, and alloys.

### `Osar` — bone

Closest roots considered: `Zor` animal, `Rask` skin or raw hide, `Morl` hard stone, and `Kal` strength.

Why they were not sufficient: bone is both a bodily structure and a widely worked material. Treating it as animal stone would erase its biological identity, while hide and leather refer to exterior tissue. The root is expected to recur in anatomy, injury, tools, ornaments, fasteners, weapons, and animal remains.

### `Dresh` — crystal

Closest roots considered: `Sel` physical clarity, `Shal` light, `Xil` shard or hidden crystal detail, `Morl` stone, and established `Dreshal` crystal device.

Why they were not sufficient: clarity and light describe frequent crystal properties but do not define an ordered mineral solid; `Xil` emphasizes fragments rather than the material class. `Dreshal` already establishes a recognizable `Dresh-` form in technological crystal use. Promoting `Dresh` makes that latent material center explicit without changing `Dreshal`.

### `Hir` — color or hue

Closest roots considered: `Shal` light, `Sel` clarity, `Rin` essence, and existing individual color adjectives such as `Faheth`, `Veleth`, and `Verdeth`.

Why they were not sufficient: light, clarity, and essence cannot name color generally, and individual color words do not provide a category for asking about color, comparing hues, making pigments, or dyeing materials. `Hir` supplies that missing perceptual and craft center.

## Root-reuse and collision review

1. `Kalmorl`, `Krezmorl`, and `Brosmorl` lexicalize recognizable stone classes through strength, fire-origin, and layering. They do not redefine every strong, volcanic, or layered stone as granite, basalt, or slate; the narrow meanings belong to the complete headwords.
2. `Sadar` is loose mineral grain. `Sadarmorl` is consolidated stone made from such grains. Neither replaces `Mor` soil or matter, `Morl` rock or mountain, or `Mormek` prepared masonry stone.
3. `Eran` is iron specifically. `Kren` remains metal as a class, `Tir` remains copper, and `Tirkal` remains bronze.
4. `Osar` covers bone as both anatomy and material. It excludes teeth, horn, shell, raw hide, leather, and stone unless later review explicitly establishes those relations.
5. `Dresh` names crystal as material. `Dreshal` remains the established crystal calculation device, `Selmek` remains manufactured glass, and `Xil` remains shard, fragment, or hidden crystal detail.
6. `Neshmorl` includes living coral colonies and the mineral structure they produce. It does not turn every aquatic animal into coral or replace a later generic reef term.
7. `Zorxar` is an animal-made hard outer covering. `Xarnesh` remains a shellfish—an aquatic creature characterized by such covering—while `Rask` and `Mekrask` remain hide and leather.
8. `Kavolan` is wax, not any oil or fat. Its lexical definition requires the characteristic moldable, coating, sealing, and water-resistant material behavior. `Olan` remains oil and `Morolan` remains fat.
9. `Verdbel` is resin as a plant-derived binder that may harden. It does not include animal glue, manufactured adhesive, oil, sap generally, or every sticky plant liquid.
10. `Hir` names color itself, `Hirmor` names a color-imparting material, and `Hiraen` names the action of imparting color. Existing individual color adjectives remain unchanged.
11. `Belkren` is deliberately combined metal. It does not mean an object merely assembled from separate metal parts. `Tirkal` remains bronze specifically and becomes one possible alloy rather than the definition of alloy as a whole.
12. Exact scanning found no current headword or stored variant matching any proposed primary or euphonic form.

## Tallow and alloy decision

### Keep `Belkren` alloy

Alloy is structurally important, not decorative. Ohnosha already distinguishes generic metal, copper, bronze, forged tools, conductive technology, weapons, vehicles, and engineered infrastructure. Speakers need to contrast a pure metal with a deliberately combined metal and to explain that bronze is one alloy rather than the universal model of alloy. `Belkren` achieves this by reusing approved roots and is likely to recur across craft, trade, defense, construction, and technology.

### Defer tallow

Tallow is useful but not yet independently necessary. `Morolan` already names fat and can support ordinary reference to animal fat in context. The specifically rendered material becomes important mainly when discussing wick-fed lights, soapmaking, fuel, waterproofing, or industrial processing—concepts reserved for later tool, household-maintenance, and energy passes. Creating a tallow headword now would front-load a specialized processing distinction before the recurring objects and practices that justify it.

Reconsider tallow when candle, wick, soap, grease, rendering or melting, and household fuel are reviewed together. If those scenes make it recurrent, a short lexicalized compound from established `Zor`, `Morolan`, or a future rendering-process term should be preferred over a new primitive.

## Risks requiring explicit review

1. `Kalmorl`, `Krezmorl`, and `Brosmorl` use strong Celan metaphors to lexicalize Earth-comparable stone classes. Approval accepts those narrow whole-word meanings but does not make the roots chemically diagnostic.
2. `Dresh` is a promoted latent material root inferred from `Dreshal`. Approval would establish that analysis moving forward while preserving the older device meaning unchanged.
3. `Neshmorl` deliberately reflects coral's animal origin and stone-like structure. Approve it only if “aquatic-creature stone” feels culturally natural across both living reefs and worked coral.
4. `Kavolan` treats wax as an enduring, shapeable oil-like substance. It must remain distinct from liquid oil and ordinary fat in actual scene use.
5. `Hir` adds a general color root because the language currently has individual color adjectives but no ordinary category for hue, pigment, or dyeing.
6. Tallow is explicitly deferred, not rejected. No Celan headword for tallow is proposed or authorized by this document.
