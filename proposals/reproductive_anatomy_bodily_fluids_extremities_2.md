# Reproductive Anatomy, Bodily Fluids, and Extremities 2

**Status:** Approved and integrated into canon on 2026-09-15  
**Scope:** 20 proposed universal headwords covering the 18 requested concepts  
**New primitive roots proposed:** 9  
**Existing roots reused:** Shan, Sorl, Xar, Uren, Jor, Dren, Kor, Pir, Morlka, Vesh  

## Design ruling

This batch keeps reproductive anatomy separate from `Azan` feminine essence and `Kadren` masculine essence. Body structures do not determine a person's gendered essence, and gendered essence does not predict anatomy. The terms are therefore anatomically direct and usable for any person who has the structure.

`Shan` is used only where passage is physically central: the vagina and urethra. `Vek` is not used as a generic substitute for hollow organ. `Tash` is not attached mechanically to every organ. Direct roots are admitted where a compound would define a structure through only one function or would produce false anatomy.

## Root-admission review

### Noka — vulval anatomy

`Tlamor` names sexual activity, not a body structure. `Xarmetha` names skin generally. `Azan` names feminine essence and cannot safely classify anatomy. A specific root is therefore required. `Nokashan` then uses `Shan` honestly for the internal canal adjoining that region.

### Kiri — clitoris

No current root identifies this structure. `Jor` could describe a surge of response but would define the organ only through stimulation. `Kor` could suggest a focal point but would still fail to identify the anatomy. A direct root avoids both reductions.

### Mara — uterus

`Vek` would portray the uterus only as a container, while `Tash` would merely say organ. A direct root is necessary for the structure. `Marasorl` then follows a real anatomical relation: the cervix is the narrow neck of the uterus.

### Seki — ovary

No approved root distinguishes an ovary, ovum, or gonad. `Bal` concerns living growth and plant seed formation; extending it here would revive the false `Balvek` seed analogy. A direct organ root is cleaner until sperm, ovum, and gonad terminology receives its own review.

### Tava — penis

`Shan` can name the urethral passage but not the complete organ around it. `Kadren` names masculine essence, not anatomy. `Mor` names matter or physical substance, not a bodily member. The frequent concrete structure warrants a direct root.

### Nderu — testicle

`Balvek` is a plant seed, and a testicle is not a vessel holding seed. `Osar` bone is anatomically false. A direct root is required. `Nderuxar` then transparently names the protective skin-and-tissue covering around the testicles.

### Fena — anus

`Fek` supplies feces, but `Fekshan` would collapse anus, rectum, and intestinal passage, while `Fekor` could be understood as a place for feces rather than a body structure. A direct root preserves the distinction and leaves possible future `Fena` derivatives open without authorizing them.

### Leya — sexual arousal

`Theraen` yearning and desire is psychological; `Tlamor` is sexual activity; `Jor` is a general surge. None distinguishes involuntary physiological arousal. `Leya` supplies that state, while `Leyajor` uses the established surge root for the bounded climax without making arousal and orgasm synonymous.

### Ruma — human hair

`Sura` is explicitly animal hair or fur as natural covering and material. Human hair deserves an ordinary unmarked term. `Ruma` is proposed as a historical phonological sister to `Sura`: their resemblance records related bodily material without making one a transparent compound or silently expanding `Sura`.

## Existing-root reuse

- `Shan` contributes physical passage only to `Nokashan` and `Urenshan`.
- `Sorl` contributes the established neck relation to `Marasorl`.
- `Xar` contributes protective covering to `Nderuxar`.
- `Uren` supplies urine to `Urenshan`.
- `Jor` supplies bounded surge to `Leyajor`.
- `Dren` supplies bodily fluid or flow to saliva and tears.
- `Kor` contributes grip and central function to `Korpir`.
- `Pir` remains the generic digit term and continues to include the thumb when contrast is unnecessary.
- `Morlka` and the newly approved `Vesh` produce the heel as the rear part of the foot.

## Distinctions that must remain explicit

- Anatomy does not determine `Azan` or `Kadren` essence.
- Sexual arousal does not prove desire, consent, willingness, pleasure, or intended action.
- Orgasm does not prove consent, satisfaction, fertility, ejaculation, or reproduction.
- Vulva is external anatomy; vagina is an internal canal.
- Uterus and vagina are different structures; the cervix connects them.
- Ovary and testicle are gonads, not vessels containing a generic “seed.”
- Scrotum and testicle are different structures.
- Urethra and vagina are different passages.
- Tears do not necessarily indicate sadness; saliva is not generic drinking water.
- `Pir` remains valid when speakers do not need to distinguish thumb from other fingers.
- `Sura` remains animal hair or fur; `Ruma` is person/human hair.

## Euphony and cadence

Primary forms remain unsmoothed. The following optional forms may be approved as searchable variants if desired:

- `Urenashan` /oo-rehn-ah-shahn/ for `Urenshan`
- `Kelvoradren` /kehl-vohr-ah-drehn/ for `Kelvordren`
- `Lorinshaladren` /loh-reen-shahl-ah-drehn/ for `Lorinshaldren`

Cadence test:

> Noka. Nokashan. Kiri. Mara. Marasorl. Seki.  
> Tava. Nderu. Nderuxar. Urenshan. Fena.  
> Leya. Leyajor. Kelvordren. Lorinshaldren.  
> Korpir. Morlkavesh. Ruma.

The set alternates compact open-vowel roots with grounded compounds and one articulated prenasal onset. It does not rely exclusively on the newest `Nd-`, `Mb-`, or `Nk-` possibilities, nor does it avoid them as though they were ornamental exceptions.

## Scope boundary

This proposal does not automatically add terms for labia, rectum, sperm, semen, ovum, erection, lubrication, ejaculation, contraception, infertility, or pregnancy. Those remain legitimate later considerations but require their own semantic review rather than mechanical generation from this batch.

All proposed entries are universal overall. National origin and national usage should remain blank unless a later discussion identifies a genuinely nation-characteristic form rather than merely a local medical practice or euphemism.
