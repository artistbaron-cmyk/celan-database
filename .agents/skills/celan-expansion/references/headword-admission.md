# Headword Admission

Use this reference while proposing or reviewing candidates. It guides judgment; it does not automatically approve terms.

## A Candidate Should Earn Its Place

A strong candidate normally does at least one of the following:

- removes an ordinary conversational dead end
- recurs across several plausible scenes or domains
- names a recurring reality of Ohnosha
- preserves an important distinction that Celan currently collapses
- supplies a genuinely useful primitive or productive root
- lets a nation speak with greater precision about its lived world
- supports explanation, negotiation, storytelling, or metaphor beyond one lore sentence

High-frequency mundane language often has greater value than a narrow ceremonial or technical compound.

## Required Review

For each candidate, establish:

- proposed headword and category
- explicit pronunciation
- concise universal core definition
- derivation and meaning contributed by each component
- relevant source-established and approved created roots considered during the root-reuse pass
- why a plausible existing root was rejected, or why a new primitive is genuinely necessary
- nation of characteristic origin, when applicable
- believable national application without national ownership
- optional euphonic forms and their pronunciations
- at least two natural use situations
- nearest existing terms and why the candidate does not replace them
- whether it should be a headword, a new sense, a transparent phrase, an alias, or a proper name

## Reject or Rework When

- the form duplicates an existing meaning without a useful distinction
- the definition is reverse-engineered from roots that do not support it
- it uses an established root as generic filler
- it is comprehensible only through its English gloss
- it is so narrow that speakers would rarely use it
- it is so broad that it erases established words
- it exists only because a derivational matrix permits it
- its smooth and unsmoothed forms are incorrectly counted as separate lexemes
- the pronunciation is unsettled
- examples sound like dictionary demonstrations rather than things people would say

## Primitive Roots

Before proposing a new primitive, search both `data/roots_and_morphology.csv` and `data/expanded_root_database.csv` by form, meaning, function, evidence, and related derivatives. Newly approved roots participate fully in this search; do not treat them as less available than roots inherited from the source volumes.

Propose a new primitive when a recurring concept cannot be expressed cleanly without overloading an existing root. Document the closest roots considered and the specific mismatch that prevents honest reuse. A new primitive needs a stable semantic center and evidence that it can support more than a single decorative compound. Do not generate a derivative family automatically when approving the primitive.

## Approval Semantics

Discussion, enthusiasm, or permission to continue brainstorming is not canon approval. Treat a term as approved when the user clearly accepts it, approves an identified group, or asks to add that settled group. If the approved set is ambiguous, continue refining without writing to the canon layer.
