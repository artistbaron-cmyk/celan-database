# Capability Audit Framework

Use this framework to find lexical dead ends and decide what Celan should develop next. It is an expandable diagnostic map, not an ontology of permitted words.

## Functional Language Layer

Audit whether speakers can perform these actions with ordinary Celan:

- begin, sustain, redirect, and end conversation
- ask and answer questions
- request, offer, command, refuse, permit, and apologize
- identify, describe, compare, quantify, and locate
- express cause, consequence, condition, possibility, uncertainty, and intention
- emphasize, hedge, hesitate, correct, clarify, and repair misunderstanding
- shift among intimate, casual, professional, formal, and ceremonial registers

Functional gaps often matter more than missing nouns because they affect every subject domain.

## Lived-World Domains

1. Body, health, bodily sensation, illness, and care
2. Emotion, thought, identity, kinship, friendship, and relationships
3. Household, food, clothing, personal possessions, and daily maintenance
4. Physical action, movement, perception, texture, and physical states
5. Place, geography, weather, travel, transport, and navigation
6. Work, craft, tools, materials, construction, and technology
7. Trade, ownership, value, law, governance, rank, and institutions
8. Flora, fauna, ecology, agriculture, seasons, and natural processes
9. Time, number, measurement, shape, scale, sequence, and comparison
10. Communication, knowledge, education, records, evidence, and information
11. Art, music, storytelling, recreation, competition, and celebration
12. Danger, conflict, defense, survival, injury, loss, and death
13. Spirituality, ethics, philosophy, metaphysics, and post-Fracture reality

Domains may overlap. Add or split a domain when recurring lexical gaps justify it.

## Cross-Cutting Cultural Lenses

Examine every functional need and lived-world domain through:

- the characteristic experience of each nation
- universal meaning versus locally specialized application
- intimate, casual, market, professional, official, and sacred registers
- geography and settlement type
- historical period and technological context
- literal, figurative, and idiomatic use

A nation's specialized resolution can create a universal Celan word. Preserve the stable core definition while recording the characteristic national application.

## Evidence of a Real Gap

A gap is stronger when one or more of these occurs:

- several plausible scenes require the same missing concept
- a speaker must switch to English to finish an ordinary thought
- existing circumlocution is confusing or implausibly long for frequent use
- one established word carries too many unrelated physical meanings
- a nation cannot speak fluently about a central part of its lived world
- Celan lacks a common contrast, such as clean/dirty, shallow/deep, lend/owe, or remember/forget
- speakers cannot explain or negotiate an unfamiliar concept using existing language

Do not infer importance from an English frequency list alone. Test concepts against Ohnoshan life and actual scene needs.

## Milestone Audit

At major milestones, test representative scenes rather than declaring the language complete from its count. Include domestic life, care or injury, travel, trade, work, disagreement, relationship talk, public institutions, danger, and at least two nationally distinctive settings.

Log each lexical dead end and classify it as:

- missing headword
- missing sense of an existing headword
- missing grammatical or discourse construction
- acceptable circumlocution
- proper name or one-off technical label
- cultural concept needing human development

Use the results to select the next batch.
