# Celan Road to 2,000: Gap Treatment Audit

**Audit date:** 2026-09-15  
**Scope:** Determine whether previously identified semantic needs require a new headword, an added sense, a conventional expression, transparent phrasing, or prior worldbuilding.  
**Canon status:** Read-only lexical audit. No Celan form, sense, expression, root, category, or lore decision is approved by this document.

## Executive finding

Celan currently contains **1,372 app headwords**, **1,451 displayed senses**, **1,575 underlying assembled records**, and **189 stored variants that do not count as separate headwords**. The language is no longer broadly deficient in objects, institutions, or specialist terminology. Its strongest remaining gaps cluster in cognition, ordinary social repair, psychological interiority, bodily anatomy, bodily functions, material behavior, exchange obligations, civic harm, and a small number of setting-specific systems.

The earlier semantic gap map must not be converted directly into headwords. A deeper treatment pass shows five different outcomes:

1. some needs are already handled by grammar or current vocabulary;
2. some are best expressed through ordinary compositional phrasing;
3. some should become searchable conventional expressions;
4. some warrant an added sense or lexicalized compound built from existing Celan material;
5. some expose genuinely missing semantic material and may justify a new primitive after root review.

Several Ohnosha-specific areas require a sixth outcome: **worldbuilding first**. The language cannot honestly name distinctions that Ohnoshans have not yet been established as recognizing.

The audit therefore does not supply 628 words to fill the numerical distance to 2,000. It identifies a smaller, stronger development spine and a method for discovering later gaps through scene use.

## Treatment key

| Code | Treatment | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `COV` | Existing coverage | Current Celan already expresses the intended meaning adequately; remove it from the gap ledger. |
| `GRAM` | Grammar | A productive grammatical construction performs the function; a narrative noun or verb may still require separate testing. |
| `PHR` | Transparent phrase | Existing words should express the meaning compositionally without creating a dictionary entry. |
| `EXP` | Conventional expression | Existing words can carry the meaning, but a socially recognized formula should be tested and, if approved, stored in Expressions. |
| `SENSE` | Added-sense candidate | An existing displayed headword may naturally extend to the meaning, subject to the Ohnoshan Metaphor Test and contrastive examples. |
| `LEX` | Lexicalized construction | Existing roots appear capable of building the meaning, but the recurring whole concept may deserve a headword. No form is proposed here. |
| `PRIM` | Primitive territory | Existing semantic material does not cleanly encode the distinction. A new one- or two-syllable primitive may be justified after the full root-reuse and cadence review. |
| `CULT` | Cultural decision | Whether the distinction exists, or how Ohnoshans divide it, must be decided before lexical treatment. |
| `LORE` | Worldbuilding first | The underlying institution, technology, phenomenon, or classification is not established sufficiently to support vocabulary. |

`PRIM` is not approval of a primitive. It means only that current Celan resources appear inadequate without semantic distortion.

## 1. Cognition, understanding, and interpersonal repair

This is the strongest first development area because its gaps recur across every nation and register.

### Section conclusion

Direct questions and refusals are not missing capabilities. Apology should begin as expression work. Knowing has inherited semantic support. Understanding and general assistance are the clearest primitive-level candidates.

Suspicion and culturally marked patience/stubbornness already have expression-level coverage. Curiosity can probably develop from existing query/wonder material. Jealousy, envy, embarrassment, boredom, resentment, loneliness, and anxiety are the clearest new emotional territory.

## 8. Salvage, provenance, and displaced artifacts

This category is directly supported by canon: lost settlements and civilizations leave artifacts without stable pasts, and places and objects can be displaced across incompatible histories. Current Celan has magical artifact `Rinvek`, ordinary tools and cargo, archives and records, timeline `Norzhir`, temporal displacement `Norshan`, reality anomaly `Lianreth`, instability `Rethkal`, and field safety language.

| Semantic need | Current resources | Finding | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|

| Salvager or recovery worker | occupational-role resources | Derive only after the activity is settled; not every scavenger animal supplies a human occupational metaphor. | `LEX` |
| Ruin or destroyed settlement/structure | building, destruction, remains | A recurring landscape category broader than one damaged building. | `PRIM` or `LEX` |
| Relic or surviving object from an earlier society | `Rinvek` magical artifact; age/preservation; records | A relic need not be magical. Its importance comes from historical survival and association. | `LEX` |

| Timeline of origin | `Norzhir` timeline; source/history | Transparent technical phrase may be adequate. | `PHR` |

### Section conclusion

Salvage, ruin, nonmagical relic, and provenance are the strongest lexical candidates. Temporal displacement is already covered. Salvage rights and custody rules require institutional worldbuilding.

## 9. SuperBeing civic language

Canon distinguishes magic, SuperBeing status, and cosmic scale. Current Celan can name magic, practitioners, specific powers, channeling, spells, artifacts, spirits, cosmic relation, power instability, compulsion, and backlash. It cannot name the civic category itself.

| Semantic need | Current resources | Finding | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| SuperBeing as neutral civic category | person terms, `Kal` power, `Lianeth` ability, `Arural` practitioner, `Songaeth` cosmic | No current combination automatically encodes exceptional capacity affecting the shared world irrespective of source. This is genuine new territory. | `PRIM` or carefully designed civic `LEX` |
| Exceptional capacity itself | `Kal` power; `Lianeth` can/able; specific magical techniques | Ordinary ability and raw power do not encode exceptional-world-affecting capacity. | `LEX`, possible shared new root |
| Person possessing exceptional capacity | person morphology plus the settled capacity/category | Derivative after the category is defined; do not duplicate “SuperBeing” automatically if one form can serve both. | `LEX` |
| Manifestation or use of an ability | general action/use; specific power verbs; magic use | A general term is useful for powers whose mechanism is unknown or nonmagical. | `LEX` |
| Involuntary manifestation | lack of intent/control plus manifestation | Derive or phrase after manifestation is established. | `PHR/LEX` |
| Controlled versus uncontrolled capacity | control/guidance, stability `Rethkal`, discipline | `Rethkal` covers unstable power but not every socially uncontrolled use. Phrase first. | `PHR/LEX` |
| Ability-related public harm | power plus general harm, which is itself missing | Derive after general harm and exceptional capacity are established. | `LEX` later |
| Temporary suppression or containment of capacity | `Kaaruaen` suppress magic; metaphysical seals; restraint | Magic suppression is narrower than suppressing every exceptional capacity. General treatment depends on mechanisms in lore. | `CULT/LEX` |
| Civic recognition or declared status | records, citizenship, public/private, authority | Canon says the category is civic/legal, but does not establish a universal registration system. | `LORE` before headword |

The neutral civic category and exceptional-capacity core are genuine semantic territory. Hero, villain, and vigilante remain excluded because canon does not establish those as Ohnoshan classifications.

## Recommended development sequence

1. **Cognition and interpersonal repair:** first perform phrase and expression tests for ask/reply, agreement, apology, forgiveness, explanation, belief, and persuasion. Develop primitives only for the residual comprehension and assistance gaps.
2. **Psychological interiority:** settle which emotions Ohnoshans distinguish, especially jealousy/envy, guilt/shame, pride/humility, and confidence/courage.
3. **Anatomy and bodily functions:** create a compact body system optimized for actual speech and healing, not an exhaustive textbook.
4. **Material behavior:** develop contrasts in small connected sets and test them in kitchens, workshops, clothing repair, infrastructure, salvage, and magical containment.
5. **Obligation and exchange:** establish the neutral debt/owe core, then build culturally flexible payment, lending, wages, inheritance, quotas, and public contribution.
6. **Harm and restoration:** establish harm, theft, accountability, and evidentiary roles before deciding national justice solutions.
7. **Salvage and provenance:** develop the recurring work and evidentiary concepts supported by Day One, while leaving salvage law unsettled.
8. **SuperBeing civic language:** define the category and capacity without importing hero/villain morality or assuming registration systems.
9. **Re-audit through scenes:** after roughly 80–120 admitted headwords and expressions, retest domestic life, intimate conflict, medicine, work, trade, law, travel, salvage, power incidents, and at least two nationally distinctive settings.

## Admission rule for the road to 2,000

A semantic need should not become a headword merely because the English gloss is absent. It earns lexicalization when at least one of the following is true:

- ordinary phrasing repeatedly becomes awkward or ambiguous;
- the meaning preserves a contrast Celan otherwise collapses;
- multiple domains require the same compact concept;
- Ohnoshan life makes the distinction recur socially or professionally;
- a conventional phrase has become semantically narrower than the literal sum of its words;
- or the concept supplies productive semantic material that several later words genuinely need.

Conversely, clear grammar, transparent phrases, searchable expressions, and culturally specific usages are successful language—not failed headword counts.
