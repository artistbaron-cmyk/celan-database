# Canon Dictionary Semantic Continuity and Polysemy Audit

**Audit date:** 2026-09-13  
**Status:** Audit completed; seven approved senses integrated as Semantic Continuity and Polysemy 1  
**Scope:** The Canon Dictionary as displayed in the app

## Canon Dictionary boundary

For this audit, **Canon Dictionary** means the complete app-facing dictionary:

- **1,022 displayed headwords**
- **1,099 displayed senses**
- **1,374 unique Celan–English example pairs** represented by 2,620 entry-to-example links
- **129 searchable euphonic or alias variants**, kept attached to their primary entries and not treated as separate words

The 1,205 underlying assembled records are not themselves the audit population. Source-only rows, merged witnesses, and intentionally suppressed technical root descriptions do not become lexical candidates merely because the data pipeline can see them. `Shan` remains the original specifically approved exception in which an established root description is also displayed as a lexical sense. This batch adds `Sel` as a second named exception because its physical-clarity root sense was already visible and must remain visible beside mental clarity.

Every displayed headword and sense was included in the inventory review. The audit then concentrated example-level review on established multi-sense words, heavily reused semantic families, and plausible extensions found across the full dictionary.

## Executive conclusion

The expansion has done what it was supposed to do: it has given several old, heavily burdened words more precise neighbors without invalidating their earlier uses. No approved expansion is shown to have overwritten a previously displayed lexical definition.

The full audit produced three initially strong candidates and four initially conditional candidates. The user approved all seven after reviewing their boundaries, and they are now integrated with contrastive examples. The audit also identifies twenty-two meanings that work better as ordinary usage or idiom than as separate dictionary senses, ten meanings better served by a distinct construction, and twelve protected or rejected extensions.

The central editorial finding is that **semantic relief and semantic preservation must happen together**:

- New precise words may become the preferred forms in careful speech.
- Earlier broad or idiomatic constructions remain valid Celan.
- A new sense may add a reading, but must not retroactively narrow, replace, or reinterpret an established reading.
- The goal is not to increase the displayed-sense count. It is to identify extensions Celan speakers would naturally make through their own cultural metaphors.

## The six-gate test

Every proposed extension is evaluated against six requirements:

1. **Semantic relationship:** the proposed use preserves the word's existing conceptual center.
2. **Conversational clarity:** ordinary speakers can distinguish the readings without constant explanation.
3. **Hidden-root integrity:** the proposal is not merely an intentionally suppressed root-analysis description.
4. **Context reliability:** syntax, participant type, register, or scene reliably selects the intended reading.
5. **Comparative value:** the extension enriches Celan more naturally than a new compound or short circumlocution.
6. **Backward compatibility:** every established phrase, example, compound, ritual expression, and displayed meaning using the word remains interpretable as before.

A proposal failing the sixth gate cannot be repaired by rewriting old examples. It must be narrowed, moved to usage or idiom, given a distinct compound, or rejected.

## Quantitative shape of semantic load

The dictionary now contains **63 headwords with multiple displayed senses**: 50 have two senses, 12 have three, and `Kel` has four. The 77 sense rows beyond the one-sense-per-headword baseline include several different phenomena:

- genuine related polysemy, such as `Lun` as lake and Moon;
- lexical and grammatical multifunction, such as `Var` as “go/move” and “to/toward”;
- contextual or historical extensions, such as `Velmarin` as ancient tree and honorary elder;
- overlapping source witnesses, such as the three closely related `Krezor` glosses;
- potentially fragile homonymy or directional contrast, such as `Tal` and `Talaen` carrying both giving and taking uses.

Family metadata shows where derivational activity concentrates. Excluding the verb-forming `-aen` family, the largest families are `Bel` and `Vel` at 39 headwords each, `Vek` at 38, `Pral` at 35, `Ral` at 32, `Dren` and `Xar` at 30, `Esh` and `Zhir` at 28, and `Mek` at 26. These counts are a watchlist, not a diagnosis: a productive root can build many narrow compounds without gaining unrelated standalone senses.

## Approved additions: strongest initial candidates

These three candidates satisfied all six tests and were subsequently approved and integrated.

### `Kel` — text or connected written words

**Existing displayed meanings:** say or talk informally; name; word  
**Proposed boundary:** a text or connected body of written or encoded words, but not information or data generally

| Gate | Finding |
|---|---|
| Semantic relationship | **Pass.** A text is language stabilized beyond one spoken word. |
| Conversational clarity | **Pass.** Noun syntax and a writing or library context distinguish text from “say” and personal name. |
| Hidden-root integrity | **Pass.** `Kel` is already a displayed lexical headword with several senses. |
| Context reliability | **Pass.** `rinaen kel an velkelor` is already translated “The text is in the library.” |
| Comparative value | **Pass.** A frequent general word for text is more natural than a compound for every written work. |
| Backward compatibility | **Pass with a hard boundary.** Existing “say,” “name,” and “word” examples remain unchanged. The example `dor mekral kel an belzhirkavor`, translated with “information,” must not be used to broaden `Kel` to all data. |

**Integrated decision:** “Text” is an additive displayed sense. All three earlier senses remain visible.

### `Anvekaen` — load cargo into transport

**Existing meaning:** board or enter a vehicle, vessel, or transport animal for travel  
**Proposed meaning:** put cargo aboard or into transport for travel

| Gate | Finding |
|---|---|
| Semantic relationship | **Pass.** Both uses are entry into a carrier in preparation for travel. |
| Conversational clarity | **Pass.** A person boards; an operator loads a cargo object. |
| Hidden-root integrity | **Pass.** This is an ordinary expansion verb. |
| Context reliability | **Pass.** Participant roles and object type distinguish the readings. |
| Comparative value | **Pass.** The alternation is shorter and more natural than another long cargo compound. |
| Backward compatibility | **Pass.** All seven current examples contain passengers or first-person travelers and retain the boarding reading. |

**Integrated decision:** Approved as a pair with `Thalvekaen`.

### `Thalvekaen` — unload cargo from transport

**Existing meaning:** disembark or come out of transport after travel  
**Proposed meaning:** remove cargo from transport after travel

| Gate | Finding |
|---|---|
| Semantic relationship | **Pass.** Both uses are outward movement from a carrier after travel. |
| Conversational clarity | **Pass.** A person disembarks; an operator unloads a cargo object. |
| Hidden-root integrity | **Pass.** This is an ordinary expansion verb. |
| Context reliability | **Pass.** A cargo object plus a `thal` source phrase distinguishes unloading. |
| Comparative value | **Pass.** It completes the same passenger/cargo alternation as `Anvekaen`. |
| Backward compatibility | **Pass.** All three current examples contain passengers and retain the disembarking reading. |

**Integrated decision:** Approved as a pair with `Anvekaen`.

## Backward-compatibility findings

### What the current expansion did not break

- The Canon Dictionary still displays all established lexical senses alongside later approved additions.
- `Shan` still displays both passing/transfer and through/across.
- `Lun` still displays lake and Moon.
- New bodily death vocabulary did not redefine sleep, destruction, memory persistence, or metaphysical un-making.
- New literal infrastructure did not convert `Mbalan` into an architectural pillar.
- New precision around drinking, trade, movement, government, tools, and conflict gives speakers alternatives without automatically invalidating older broad constructions.

### What cannot yet be proven from examples alone

The Canon Dictionary contains 189 headwords that do not occur as exact standalone tokens in any unique Celan example. Some are affixes or bound forms, but the set also includes lexical roots such as `Pral`, `Kor`, `Khum`, `Trak`, `Zhir`, `Jek`, `Tham`, and `Zhel`. Their definitions can be audited, but phrase stability cannot be fully demonstrated until they receive direct examples.

Therefore, no added sense should be approved for an example-poor word on semantic intuition alone. It first needs at least:

1. one example with the established literal reading;
2. one example with the proposed extension;
3. one contrastive scene in which both readings could plausibly occur and context still distinguishes them.

## Required integration protocol for any approved candidate

1. Snapshot every displayed sense, usage note, example, compound, variant, and visibility state associated with the reused headword.
2. Add the approved sense as a new row. Do not edit or repurpose the earlier row to make room for it.
3. Add contrastive examples that distinguish the old and new readings.
4. Rebuild the app and verify the old and new senses all appear.
5. Compare the before/after sense map for the reused headword, not merely total dictionary counts.
6. Confirm that no hidden technical root description became visible unless that exact exception was explicitly approved.
7. Re-run every existing example containing the headword and confirm its earlier translation remains available without reinterpretation.
8. Record the decision and its semantic boundary in the editorial policy.

## Recommended decision sequence

1. Review `Kel` as bounded **text**.
2. Review `Anvekaen` and `Thalvekaen` together as the load/unload alternation.
3. Scene-test `Keldoraen`, `Njor`, `Sel`, and `Ruvan` with old and new readings in the same conversational environments.
4. Add approved usage examples for the usage-only group without creating extra displayed senses.
5. Protect the rejected boundaries and the existing multi-sense watchlist in all future expansion batches.
6. Re-run this audit after each approved polysemy batch; do not wait for the next numerical milestone.

## Integration outcome

The seven approved senses are canonical under ED-0022 and IDs `LX-SCP1-0001` through `LX-SCP1-0007`. They add **seven displayed senses and zero headwords**. Sixteen supporting and contrastive examples were added. All established displayed senses of the seven reused headwords remain present. No category, national origin, euphonic variant, or previously displayed definition was removed or replaced.
