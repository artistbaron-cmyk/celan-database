# Everyday conversations — Volume 3, English-first plan

This is a planning draft, not a Celan translation or recording script. Savi and Dalen prepare shared supplies while Meva carries goods and messages between places. Each conversation should stand alone, but the larger task gives their choices consequences. Most scenes have two speakers; Meva joins only when the third person's own decision changes the exchange. Spoken lines will be checked against the Dictionary App before they enter a Celan master script. Stage directions are not spoken.

## Thirty scene beats

| Scene | Working title | What happens | Main distinction |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | One for Meva | Dalen counts the travelers but forgets that the person carrying their water also needs some. | Four versus five; a count changes the plan. |
| 2 | The empty place | Savi expects a container to be full; Dalen has left it empty so it can carry something else. | Empty versus full; purpose matters. |
| 3 | One too many | Meva cannot carry an extra item, so the group chooses which one stays. | Exact count; a limit has a consequence. |
| 4 | Each person | A shared pile looks sufficient until Savi counts what each traveler needs. | Each versus all. |
| 5 | The missing coins | Dalen finds a short count and traces it to a separate pouch. | Coins and exact quantity. |
| 6 | Water before the road | Meva asks for water; Savi has some but must keep enough for the people staying. | Some versus enough. |
| 7 | Half of the whole | Two unequal containers cause a disagreement about what half the combined water means. | `unar thal del` as a proportion, not one container. |
| 8 | A quarter for later | Savi keeps a quarter of the shared supply for the return trip. | `unar thal quen`; stated whole. |
| 9 | Five-eighths | Meva asks for five-eighths of a stated stock; Dalen checks the amount against the whole. | `peth thal oven`; general fraction pattern. |
| 10 | Enough for whom? | Dalen says the supplies are enough; Meva asks whether that includes the people at the destination. | Sufficiency depends on the stated need. |
| 11 | Measure the length | Dalen measures a needed length; Savi notices that the chosen unit was misunderstood. | Number plus length unit. |
| 12 | A stride apart | Meva places two objects too close together and adjusts their spacing. | Strides as measured distance. |
| 13 | Count the measures | A trade quantity sounds like a coin amount until the unit is made clear. | Measures of goods versus coins. |
| 14 | The weight of the load | One item makes the load too heavy; the three decide what to move. | Weight, amount, and a practical limit. |
| 15 | Check it once more | Savi and Dalen use the same reference point to resolve different measurements. | Shared measurement reference. |
| 16 | This one by me | Dalen reaches for the wrong item when Savi points from across the room. | `deni` from the speaker's position. |
| 17 | That one by you | Meva identifies an item in Dalen's reach, changing which person moves it. | `denya` from the speaker's position. |
| 18 | The one beyond | An item is neither near Savi nor Dalen; they locate it before anyone walks over. | `denla` as a third position; requires a verified place frame. |
| 19 | Turn the other way | Meva follows a direction from the wrong viewpoint, then corrects course. | Left and right depend on orientation. |
| 20 | Which side of the door? | Savi sends Dalen for an item; it is on the other side of the boundary. | Inside versus outside. |
| 21 | Behind the load | A needed object is hidden by the packed goods. | In front versus behind. |
| 22 | Between the two | Meva finds one of two similar items using two reference objects. | Between with two anchors. |
| 23 | Through, then across | The shorter route requires passing a boundary that Meva cannot use. | Route choice and spatial relation. |
| 24 | Leave it outside | A carried item must stay outside while the rest goes in. | Boundary and deliberate placement. |
| 25 | The closer stop | Meva compares two destinations from the same starting point. | `nevu ther` versus `arin ther`. |
| 26 | How far from here? | Dalen asks for distance; Meva answers that the destination is actually near. | `arin … sha?` is a neutral distance question. |
| 27 | The long way | A physically near place takes longer to reach by the available route. | Distance versus travel time. |
| 28 | At least five | Savi sets a minimum quantity for the destination's needs. | Inclusive `mbetu` bound. |
| 29 | No more than two | Meva sets a maximum that the load can bear. | Inclusive `senu` bound. |
| 30 | What arrives | The group checks what Meva will carry and what remains, then parts with a clear plan. | Count, location, and quantity in one natural exchange. |

Some beats deliberately name a meaning that still needs a verified Celan sentence frame, especially the full `an denla` locative use and ordinary trade phrasing. Before writing the master script, revise each English exchange to fit established Celan or develop the missing expression openly. Do not silently change the English meaning to fit a nearby word.

## Dialogue standard for the full draft

The scene beats above are prompts, not lines to speak. Each finished scene needs a concrete reason for the exchange, a reply that changes what someone does, and enough room for the moment to finish. Do not end a scene immediately after the target number or location word appears. Let a person misunderstand, hesitate, refuse, joke, or notice something when that fits the situation. Put visible actions in stage directions instead of making a character narrate them. Use three speakers only when the third person's choice matters. Vary the length of the exchanges; forcing every scene into the same number of turns will make them sound rehearsed.

The fraction, pointing, and distance groups need particular care. The characters should be dividing supplies, reaching for something, or choosing a route; they should not explain grammar to one another. If a line sounds like a lesson, rewrite the situation before translating it.

## Opening conversation draft — 1. One for Meva

*Dalen sets four full water flasks beside Meva's bag. Meva checks the bag while Savi closes the supply cupboard.*

```text
Dalen: Four flasks for the village. That's everything.
Meva: And my water?
Dalen: You brought your own flask, didn't you?
Meva: I left it here yesterday.
Savi: It's by the door. Empty.
Dalen: I'll fill it.
```

*Dalen fills the spare flask and hands it to Meva.*

```text
Dalen: Five flasks. Can you carry all of these?
Meva: Yes. Let me drink before I close the bag.
```

*Meva drinks, puts the flask in the bag, and closes it.*

```text
Meva: All right. I'm leaving now. Did I miss anything?
Savi: I'm still checking the list.
```

*Meva pauses with the bag in hand.*

The fifth flask is for the person carrying the others. Dalen had a plausible reason to think Meva already had hers; Savi knows where it was left. Meva gets a drink and prepares to go, but Savi's unfinished list makes her pause. The spoken Celan version should keep those relationships without forcing a line-by-line imitation of the English wording.

## Tone check — 7. Half of the whole

*Dalen sets a small water container beside Savi and puts a larger one with Meva's supplies.*

```text
Savi: That's all you're leaving here?
Dalen: I left you one.
Savi: The other one holds much more.
Dalen: You asked for half.
Savi: Half the water.
```

*Dalen looks at the containers, then takes the larger one back.*

```text
Dalen: I'll measure it.
Meva: Please do. I need to leave soon.
Savi: Give us a moment.
```

The disagreement comes from the unequal containers. No one defines a fraction for the listener; they need to settle what they are actually sharing. This is an English draft, so any Celan rendering still needs a line-by-line meaning check.
