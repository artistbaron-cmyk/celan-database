# Volume 2 — five English-first conversation trials

These are replacement **drafts for Scenes 25–29**, kept apart from the current Volume 2 script and ElevenLabs copies. Each scene was written as an English exchange first. The Celan below it carries the same spoken meaning turn by turn. Stage directions are for actors and are **not spoken**. Neither language is a prompt for the recording tool yet.

The door opens inward. A chair from their earlier repair is inside, behind it. Nara and Toren cannot see the chair until the door opens a little. This physical detail is what makes the failed repair and Toren's eventual discovery possible.

## 25. Again?

**English first**

```text
Toren: I can't get the door open
Nara: Again?
Toren: Don't laugh
Nara: I'm not laughing  Need a hand?
Toren: Yes
```

*Nara gets the toolkit. The two try to fix the door.*

**Celan**

```text
Toren: Sivrethaen I kaxaraen vorkor
Nara: Wek'aen?
Toren: Va ver havaen
Nara: Ver havaen I  Ra mbaen?
Toren: Lia
```

Nara's “Again?” calls back to the other household mishaps. Toren responds to the teasing before accepting help.

## 26. I tried

*Nara has worked on the door. Toren tries it.*

**English first**

```text
Nara: Open the door
Toren: I still can't get the door open
Nara: I tried to repair it
Toren: I know
Nara: Can you help me?
Toren: Yes
```

**Celan**

```text
Nara: Va kaxaraen Ya vorkor
Toren: Sivrethaen I kaxaraen vorkor nethnor
Nara: Tha-siv I zhaelpralaen vorkor
Toren: Varin I
Nara: Ra mbaen Ya I?
Toren: Lia
```

“I tried” does not claim the door was fixed. The second failure makes Nara ask for Toren's help.

## 27. The chair

*The door opens just enough for Toren to see inside.*

**English first**

```text
Toren: Wait  The chair is behind the door
Nara: The chair?
Toren: I put the chair here
Nara: You forgot the chair?
Toren: Yes  Don't laugh
Nara: I'm not laughing
```

**Celan**

```text
Toren: Dar  Rinaen nethor vethvar vorkor
Nara: Nethor?
Toren: Tha-dor I nethor an deni
Nara: Ra tha-kavelzhiraen Ya nethor?
Toren: Lia  Va ver havaen
Nara: Ver havaen I
```

*Nara is smiling. Toren moves the chair away from the door.*

The joke has a cause: Toren left the repaired chair in the door's path. “Don't laugh” now has a second meaning because Nara has made the same denial before.

## 28. It opens

**English first**

```text
Nara: Open the door
Toren: I opened it!
Nara: Again?
Toren: No
Nara: Water?
Toren: Food
```

**Celan**

```text
Nara: Va kaxaraen Ya vorkor
Toren: Tha-kaxaraen I vorkor!
Nara: Wek'aen?
Toren: Ka
Nara: Dro?
Toren: Velrin
```

“I opened it” is an amazed reaction after several failed tries. Nara's “Again?” asks for another test. Toren refuses it and asks, in one word, to eat instead.

## 29. After the meal

*They sit down to eat. The door remains open.*

**English first**

```text
Nara: You didn't close the door
Toren: I know
Nara: Will you close the door?
Toren: After the meal
Nara: Fruit?
Toren: Yes
```

*Nara passes Toren the fruit. They eat. The door can wait.*

**Celan**

```text
Nara: Ver tha-xaraen Ya vorkor
Toren: Varin I
Nara: Ra nor-xaraen Ya vorkor?
Toren: Vethnor velrin
Nara: Vilor?
Toren: Lia
```

Nara has not forgotten the door. Toren has not forgotten the food. The final “Fruit?” is an offer, and “Yes” is enough; the handoff happens without either person narrating it.

## Check before recording

- **`Vethvar`** means “behind”; **`vethnor`** means “after” or “later than.” Their similar starts do different jobs in Scenes 27 and 29.
- **`Ra mbaen?`** is an established clipped offer meaning “Need a hand?” or “Want help?” It does not need a full subject in the casual scene.
- **`Dro?`** is an established short offer of water. The Celan line is not a generic question about what Toren wants.
- **`Sivrethaen I kaxaraen vorkor`** follows the prior volume's pattern for failing to get the door open. Its English here says “can't get the door open,” without claiming a permanent inability.
- The English and Celan lines are paired by turn. No spoken stage direction or implied thought is smuggled into the translation.
