# New Volume 2 — Celan recording pass

These are Celan-vernacular versions of the **new English-first Volume 2 conversations**. The English lines follow what the Celan says naturally, rather than forcing an unavailable English expression into it. The ElevenLabs input is separate so sound spellings and performance tags do not alter the language shown to learners. ## Scene 26 — Stay a moment

**Vernacular choice:** `lialor` means “certainly” and carries the warm “of course” reply. Its approved pronunciation is /lee-ahlohr/.

**Situation:** Nara sees that Toren has not eaten. Toren wants company more than food or water.

| Speaker | Written Celan | English meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nara | `ver tovaenath Ya Ya-ka velrin.` | You haven't touched your food. |
| Toren | `aivaen emiltash-ian.` | My stomach aches. |
| Nara | `ra drenaen Ya dren thar-ka?` | Do you want to drink water? |
| Toren | `ka. ra nor-varan Ya ser I nor aenor?` | No. Will you stay with me for a moment? |
| Nara | `Lialor. nor-varan I ser Ya.` | Of course. I'll stay with you. |

**Pronunciation anchors:** written `I` is **ee**; `Tovaen` is /toh-vah-ehn/; `Aivaen` is /eye-vah-ehn/; `Emiltash` is /eh-meel-tahsh/; `Drenaen` is /dreh-nah-ehn/; `Thar-ka` is /thahr-kah/; `Varan` is /vah-rahn/; `Aenor` is /ah-ehnohr/. The aspect form `tovaenath` combines the approved verb with the completed-action ending `-ath`.

**ElevenLabs copy, one line per speaker turn:**

```text
Nara: [concerned] ver toh-vah-eh-nahth ya ya-ka velrin
Toren: [quietly] eye-vah-ehn emiltash-ee-ahn
Nara: [gently] ra drenaen ya dren thar-ka?
Toren: [softly] ka ra nor-varan ya ser ee nor aenor?
Nara: [reassuring] lee-ahlohr nor-varan ee ser ya
```

The sound spellings in this last block are recording input only. They are not Celan spelling or subtitles. The deliberate pronunciation rewrite is limited to the completed `tovaenath` and `aivaen`, where the English-reading model is likely to compress `ae`; all other words retain their Celan spelling unless auditioning shows a concrete problem.

## Scene 29 — Speak outside

**Vernacular choice:** `kel` is ordinary casual talk. A yes-or-no question with `ra` can invite a change of place in conversation. Nara asks whether there is a problem; Toren answers no and gives the everyday reason. The scene ends when Nara agrees to follow.

**Situation:** Toren wants to move the conversation so a sleeping child can rest.

| Speaker | Written Celan | English meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Toren | `Ra kel Inko thalor?` | Shall we talk outside? |
| Nara | `Ra rinaen belreth?` | Is there a problem? |
| Toren | `Ka. vethoraenal felka.` | No. The child is sleeping. |
| Nara | `Lia'ser. va noraen Ya I.` | I see. Lead me. |

**Pronunciation anchors:** `Inko` is /een-koh/; `Thalor` is /thah-lohr/; `Felka` is /fehl-kah/; `Belreth` is /behl-reth/; the written pronoun `I` is **ee**. `Vethoraenal` adds the continuing-action `-al` to the established sleep verb.

**ElevenLabs copy:**

```text
Toren: [softly] ra kel inko thalor?
Nara: [concerned] ra rinaen belreth?
Toren: [hushed] ka vethoraenal felka
Nara: [understanding] lia'ser va noraen ya ee
```
