# Celan: a 12-week beginner course

## Course promise

In twelve weeks, a new learner moves from hearing and arranging short Celan sentences to reading a short connected text and composing a controlled exchange. The course uses Celan's approved dictionary and learner-facing Grammar Guide as its authority. The existing [Week 1 lesson](week_01.md) supplies the opening sequence.

**Audience:** English-speaking beginners with no prior Celan. **Pace:** three 20–30 minute lessons plus two 10-minute reviews each week. **Study paths:** every week offers **Build Words** (roots, meaning, pronunciation) and **Fix Sentences** (word order, markers, transformations). Learners can switch paths; both lead to the same weekly check.

The course teaches useful, controlled communication. It does not promise unrestricted fluency after twelve weeks.

## Weekly rhythm

1. **Encounter:** hear or read two to five approved examples; notice one new pattern.
2. **Build:** arrange supplied words or parts; practice the pattern with immediate feedback.
3. **Use:** answer a scene prompt, repair a sentence, or write a short response without cards.
4. **Review:** revisit older objectives in a different exercise form, including one delayed recall task.

Each lesson should contain a model, a plain-English explanation, 6–10 practice items, one independent task, and a link to the relevant dictionary or Grammar Guide entry. Audio is optional until recorded models exist; text must stand on its own.

## The twelve weeks

| Week | Theme and learner outcome | Lesson A → B → C | Weekly check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. First Motion** | Hear clear syllables, pronounce the pronoun `I` as “ee,” recognize verb–subject–object (VSO) order, and give simple `Va` commands. | Hear and mark the action → build commands → complete the First Motion scene. Follow [Week 1](week_01.md). | Identify the action in five sentences; produce five supported commands and a three-line scene. |
| **2. People and Questions** | Use `I`, `Ya`, `La`, `Ilin`, `Yalin`, and `Lalin`; put `Ra` before a question. | Match pronouns to participants → turn statements into questions → ask and answer a short exchange. | Choose the right person marker in six contexts and form four questions from approved models. |
| **3. No, Yes, and Missing Information** | Place `Ver` directly before the verb, including after `Ra` in a negative question; recognize optional content-question tails when context needs them. | Hear positive/negative pairs → repair misplaced markers → solve a simple information-gap scene. | Transform four statements into negatives and distinguish a question from a negative question. |
| **4. Words That Grow** | Recognize recorded roots and compounds; form regular plurals with `-in` after a consonant and `-n` after a vowel. | Find a shared root → break approved compounds → compare one and many in short sentences. | Explain three recorded compounds from their documented parts and pluralize five approved nouns. |
| **5. Time in the Verb** | Recognize present, `tha-` past, and `nor-` future in controlled sentences. Introduce continuous `-al` for recognition. | Sort events by time → transform one approved model through time → narrate a three-event sequence. | Read six sentences for time and write three short time-marked sentences with a model available. |
| **6. Place and Movement** | Distinguish destination from location and use documented place relations, especially `An` for physical location. | Match places to approved words → locate or move a participant → give and follow short directions. | Complete a route scene with four accurate statements or commands. Use only motion-verb frames attested in the guide. |
| **7. Belonging and Absence** | Distinguish standalone `ka` (“without/no”) from attached possessive `-ka`; recognize personal possessive suffixes `-ian`, `-ya`, and `-eshen`. | Sort ownership/absence examples → repair spacing and attachment → describe personal and shared items. | Explain the meaning difference in four pairs and produce four correctly attached forms. |
| **8. Number and Daily Exchange** | Count with the approved base and scaling patterns; use additive number building and familiar quantity words in practical scenes. | Build number phrases → read quantities in context → complete a small exchange or inventory. | Interpret five quantities and give three accurate quantity responses. |
| **9. People, Respect, and Voice** | Choose familiar versus respectful address, identify `Li-` and `-en`, and recognize that national voice can change imagery and tone. | Match address to relationship → revise a short exchange for respect → compare two approved cultural examples. | Revise a four-line exchange for its addressee and explain two choices. Cultural comparison is interpretive, not a claim that each nation has separate grammar. |
| **10. Reading Celan on the Page** | Apply the guide's ordinary lowercase pattern and meaningful capitals; read a short connected passage using known grammar. | Notice capitalization → restore a passage's structure → answer literal and inferred questions. | Read a 75–100-word curated passage and answer five questions, citing the words that support each answer. |
| **11. Linking Ideas** | Recognize `Rath` conditional clauses and `Lior` relative clauses; link short familiar ideas without losing VSO order inside clauses. | Match two-clause meanings → repair clause order → write a guided explanation. | Interpret four linked sentences and produce two approved-frame linked sentences. Keep this week introductory. |
| **12. Living Use** | Bring together words, markers, time, relation, and social tone in an independent but bounded task. | Review personal weak spots → rehearse a scenario → complete a reading-and-writing capstone. | Read a 100–150-word curated passage, answer six questions, and write a six-to-eight-line exchange or description using at least four course patterns. |

The creator-supplied podcast transcript offers memorable scenes and conceptual hooks. Its [course review](podcast_1005_review.md) identifies forms to correct and shows where those ideas can enter the lessons. Treat the transcript as a discussion prompt, not a pronunciation or grammar authority.

## Assessment and progression

- At the start of each week, spend five minutes on a cumulative retrieval check. Repeat missed items in that session, then the next day.
- A weekly check passes at **80%** plus at least one independent production item. If a learner misses a core pattern, offer a smaller repair task and retest that pattern; do not require repeating every lesson.
- At Weeks 4 and 8, use a mixed check drawn from all earlier weeks. Week 12 combines reading, interpretation, and controlled production rather than memorizing isolated forms.
- Track mastery by objective: **New → Introduced → Practiced → Secure → Mastered**. “Secure” requires correct work across two sessions with one unhinted answer. “Mastered” requires a successful later review after at least seven days, including production. These thresholds follow the [Learning App Build Manual](../docs/celan_learning_app_build_manual.md).

### Minimum viable package and production order

1. **Audit the starter material.** Review Week 1 examples and select a small approved word and sentence set for Weeks 1–4. Record source IDs and editorial decisions in the example bank.
2. **Make Weeks 1–4 teachable.** Produce twelve complete lessons, four checks, a vocabulary deck, answer keys, and text-based scene cards. Pilot these before scaling the format.
3. **Complete Weeks 5–9.** Add tense, place, possession, number, and social-use materials; create new approved examples only through normal editorial review.
4. **Complete Weeks 10–12.** Write and review connected readings and the capstone; verify that every word and construction has already been taught or glossed.
5. **Add delivery features.** Convert the reviewed packs into the Learning Center's curriculum and exercise data, progress tracking, and spaced review described in the [Learning App Build Manual](../docs/celan_learning_app_build_manual.md). Record audio after scripts and pronunciations are approved.

For planning, 36 lessons at 6–10 practice items each implies roughly **216–360 practice items**, plus weekly checks and delayed reviews. Reuse a verified example in several exercise forms when it teaches different skills; count the forms separately only when each has its own prompt, accepted answers, and feedback.

## Content rules for lesson authors

- Select teaching words and model sentences from `dictionary/app_data/` and check each chosen sense and example by hand. The app's example placements are not a blanket certification of translation or grammar accuracy.
- Use `dictionary/app_data/grammar_guide.json` for the learner-facing rule. `data/grammar_rules_working.csv` is useful for planning but does not override the current app guide.
- Only decompose a word when its derivation is recorded. Do not present a learner-built combination as an established Celan word unless it is an approved headword.
- Keep Week 1's instructional arc, but review its individual sample forms against current app-facing records before publishing exercises or recording audio.
- For tense, possession, and other rules with older conflicting source wording, use the current learner-facing form and mark any unresolved example for editorial review.
- For Weeks 10 and 12, commission or curate connected texts and verify every sentence before using them as assessment material. The suggested passage lengths are targets, not claims that suitable approved passages already exist.
