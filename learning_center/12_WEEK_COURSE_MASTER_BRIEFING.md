# Celan 12 Week Course: master briefing

**Project snapshot: 30 October 2026**

## The decision in one paragraph

We have the beginnings of a real Celan learning system: a grammar backbone, an approved dictionary, audio for words and examples, 36 written lesson packs, and 90 recorded everyday conversations. The 12 week course gives these pieces an order. Its purpose is to help a beginner *hear, understand, choose, and use* Celan in situations that mean something, while Ohnosha becomes a place worth knowing. The lesson packs are a teaching draft, not a locked curriculum. The next job is to connect the best material into a learner journey, test what people actually learn, and then build that journey into the existing app.

## 1. Why this course exists

Celan is not yet a language people arrive knowing they need. The course therefore has two jobs at once. It teaches a usable language, and it lets learners discover why speaking it matters in Ohnosha: relationships, respect, work, place, care, humor, and different ways of noticing the world. A lesson that only explains a rule completes the first job poorly and the second job hardly at all. A beautiful scene that cannot be understood or reused does not teach enough. The course needs both.

We discussed a large learning platform with videos, quizzes, accounts, progress, rewards, timed lessons, and an experience comparable in capability to course platforms. That remains possible. The product goal is not to imitate Udemy or Thinkific. It is to make the best place to learn *this* language, using the strengths already in the app: the dictionary, grammar, expressions, cultural context, and now audio. Features should serve that experience. A timer, badge, or video earns its place by helping the learner hear, remember, understand, or use Celan.

The course promise in the current outline is deliberately bounded: after twelve weeks, a beginner can hear and arrange short Celan sentences, understand familiar exchanges, read a short connected text, and compose a controlled conversation. It does not promise unrestricted fluency. The audience is an English-speaking beginner. The planned rhythm is three lessons of about 20–30 minutes and two short review sessions each week.

## 2. The learning philosophy behind the 36 lessons

The 36 lessons are **three encounters per week for twelve weeks**. Their original theory is cumulative practice. A learner first notices a form in a meaningful example, then handles it with support, then uses it with less support. The weekly check asks whether the skill survived outside the model. Older skills return in later weeks, so the course is not twelve isolated topics.

Each pack contains an objective, two model examples, a short explanation, six practice prompts, an independent task, a hint, answer guidance, and links to language sources. Where a topic supports both, it offers **Build Words** work with forms and meanings and **Fix Sentences** work with structure and usage. Those are two routes through one course, not two separate curricula. A learner should be able to switch routes while keeping the same progress.

The five planned stages describe what the learner can *do*: **Recognize → Assemble → Produce → Adapt → Operate**. Progress should be recorded by objective, rather than treating a finished screen as mastery. The draft rule is New → Introduced → Practiced → Secure → Mastered; a later, unhinted answer is part of mastery. The proposed weekly passing mark is 80% plus an independent production task, with a smaller repair exercise when a core pattern is missed. These thresholds are starting settings to test with learners, not established facts about how fast someone will learn Celan.

The most important refinement from our conversation is that **natural use should lead the teaching**. We saw this clearly while writing dialogue. An exchange can be grammatically tidy and still sound strangely empty. We shifted to a human purpose first: what does one person want, what changes, what does the other person actually say? Then we find faithful Celan wording. English translations must be inferable from the Celan turn and its immediate context; they must not quietly add a different action, person, time, quantity, or negation. That standard belongs in the course too. Exercises should grow from conversations a person would want to have, with the grammar explained at the moment it becomes useful.

## 3. The current twelve week spine

This is the sequence written in the course outline. It is a **working spine**. The user has not adopted every lesson as final, and audio may give us a better opening for some topics.

| Week | Focus | What the learner should be able to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 — First Motion** | Sounds, written **I** pronounced **ee**, action-first order, simple **Va** commands | Hear the action, assemble a short sentence, give a supported command. |
| **2 — People and Questions** | Person markers and **Ra** | Tell speaker from listener, then ask a simple question. |
| **3 — No, Yes, and Missing Information** | **Ver**, negative questions, information gaps | Refuse or negate an action and ask what is missing. |
| **4 — Words That Grow** | Roots, approved compounds, regular plurals | Notice how a word is built and distinguish one from many. |
| **5 — Time in the Verb** | Present, **tha-** past, **nor-** future; recognize continuous **-al** | Place familiar actions in time. |
| **6 — Place and Movement** | Location, destination, documented uses of **an** | Say where something is and follow or give a short route. |
| **7 — Belonging and Absence** | Standalone **ka**, attached **-ka**, personal forms including **-ian**, **-ya**, **-oshen** | Tell possession, relation, and absence apart in real utterances. |
| **8 — Number and Daily Exchange** | Base numerals, scaling, practical quantities | Count and resolve a small quantity problem. |
| **9 — People, Respect, and Voice** | Address, **Li-**, **-en**, cultural voice | Choose a form that fits the person and setting. |
| **10 — Reading Celan on the Page** | Lowercase pattern, meaningful capitals, connected reading | Read a short passage and point to evidence for an answer. |
| **11 — Linking Ideas** | Introductory **Rath** and **Lior** clauses | Understand and write a supported linked idea. |
| **12 — Living Use** | Cumulative reading and production | Handle a bounded capstone exchange or description. |

This order is useful but should not become a cage. The conversations already make number, belonging, place, and social address matter before their formal week arrives. Learners can encounter a form naturally, understand it through context, then study the rule later. The course needs a vocabulary and grammar map for each scene so early listening stays comprehensible.

## 4. What has actually been made

### Language backbone

- The [Master Grammar Guide](../docs/CELAN_MASTER_GRAMMAR_GUIDE.md) and its [PDF](../docs/CELAN_MASTER_GRAMMAR_GUIDE.pdf) are the course's grammar backbone. The guide was developed to be readable, with examples and cultural context, not a list of raw rules.
- The Dictionary App holds the approved words, meanings, examples, expressions, and audio links. For teaching content, use its current learner-facing data and the Grammar Guide. Older working rules can explain history but should not replace current forms.
- The [podcast review](podcast_1005_review.md) preserves useful ideas from the supplied language podcast while marking pronunciation, grammar, and spelling that needed correction. It is a source of teaching ideas, not the authority for forms.

### Course material

- The [12 week outline](12_week_course.md) defines the weekly objectives and proposed checks.
- All [36 lesson packs](lessons/README.md) exist as editable Markdown drafts, with a [combined PDF](lessons/CELAN_36_LESSON_PACKS.pdf). This satisfies the request to *write* all 36. It does not mean every pack has been adopted, classroom tested, or converted to app exercises.
- The [Learning App Build Manual](../docs/celan_learning_app_build_manual.md) describes shared objectives, the two study paths, five stages, feedback, progress, review, data files, and a build order. It is an implementation proposal from an earlier point in the project. Its first-release choice of local-only progress is not a final decision against accounts.

### Everyday conversation library

We now have **three distinct volumes of 30 scenes each**, with written Celan and English and a separate performance script:

| Volume | Job | Written script | Audio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | Meet people and inhabit ordinary life: family, teacher, clothing, words, songs, work, weather, and small choices. | [Volume 1](everyday_conversations_volume_01.md) | [30 scene recordings](../audio/conversations/volume_01/README.md) |
| **2** | Handle plans, mistakes, requests, errands, and problems in natural speech. | [Volume 2 Celan pass](everyday_conversations_volume_02_celan_vernacular.md) | [30 scene recordings](../audio/conversations/volume_02/README.md) |
| **3** | Speak precisely: which one, how many, how much, size, measure, and where. Quantity matters because it changes a decision. | [Volume 3 Celan pass](everyday_conversations_volume_03_celan_vernacular.md) | [30 scene recordings](../audio/conversations/volume_03/README.md) |

The volumes are **standalone useful scenes**, not a 90 episode plot and not simply the 36 lessons read aloud. Volume 1 and Volume 2 should keep different jobs; Volume 3 should make precision practical rather than inserting numbers into old situations. Volume 3's foundation was checked in a [quantity, measure, and space audit](volume_03_quantity_measure_space_audit.md). We chose not to make “half full” a required scene merely to fill a category.

Each volume's recording copy uses turn-level mood cues in square brackets, restrained punctuation, and sound spellings for ElevenLabs. The learner script keeps real Celan orthography. Written **I** is spoken **ee**. The MP3s exist and passed technical file checks; the recording READMEs call them **first takes** and ask for a listening pass before final learner release. A file opening correctly does not certify every pronunciation or performance choice.

### Word and example audio

The app also has recorded word and example audio beyond the scenes. The recent distance and quantity batch added **Nevu, Arin, Mbetu, and Senu** with **11 example clips**, documented in the [audio guide](../audio/DISTANCE_QUANTITY_AUDIO.md). The clips were linked into the Dictionary App. This matters because the learner can hear the small piece, hear it in a sentence, and then hear people use the same kind of distinction in a scene. That three-level connection is a major new teaching opportunity.

## 5. How the pieces should work together

The course should be a guided route through a wider language world, rather than the only place content lives.

1. **Hear a person mean something.** Open with a short scene or a compelling line. The learner sees what is at stake.
2. **Notice one useful form.** Replay the line, show written Celan and a faithful English meaning, and highlight the action, marker, word part, or social choice.
3. **Practice a small choice.** Build Words may ask what a root or approved compound contributes. Fix Sentences may ask the learner to place a marker, reorder a sentence, or repair one error. Some objectives call for both; some do not.
4. **Use it with a purpose.** Give the learner a response that changes the situation: correct the count, ask the way, say whose object it is, or address a teacher respectfully.
5. **Return to it later.** Review the form in a new voice, sentence, or setting, not only the original card.

This suggests a reusable content chain: **dictionary word audio → example sentence audio → conversation turn → exercise → short independent response → delayed review**. The learner can also leave the course to explore the full conversation library, dictionary, expressions, and grammar. The course should remember which skill they were working on when they return.

The **Phrase Builder** has a related but different role. The original ambition was more than assembling words: English to Celan and Celan to English, with context, meaning, and cultural nuance. The present Phrase Builder support layer includes a phrasebank, evaluation set, concept map, and retrieval material; the visible surface is still underdeveloped. It could become a practice companion: a learner tries to say something, sees a faithful translation and explanation, and follows links back to what the course taught. Free translation needs stronger evaluation before it can grade course answers. Controlled lesson answers should remain checkable and explainable.

## 6. The Learning Center product we discussed

The intended home is **inside the existing Celan app**, alongside Dictionary, Expressions, Grammar Guide, and Phrase Builder. A learner should be able to continue the next lesson, choose Build Words or Fix Sentences, replay audio, review due skills, and browse conversations. The course should make the app feel like one connected learning environment, not several unrelated tools.

We discussed features common to larger learning platforms:

| Capability | Sensible Celan use |
| --- | --- |
| **Accounts and saved progress** | Let a learner keep their place across devices, retain objective-level history, and return to saved scenes. Design stable user and objective IDs from the start. Local progress can support a first build, with export/import and a clear path to account sync. |
| **Quizzes and checks** | Give immediate, specific feedback and accepted answer variants; mix recognition with production. Use weekly checks and cumulative Weeks 4, 8, and 12 checks. |
| **Audio controls** | Replay a full scene, one turn, a phrase, or one word; compare slow study with natural performance. Show learner spelling beside the sound. |
| **Video** | Use only when a visual action, place, gesture, or cultural situation makes a line easier to understand. It should carry meaning, not decorate a lecture. |
| **Rewards** | Reward demonstrated progress, return after a delay, and successful use in context. Avoid replacing learning with a streak chase. |
| **Timed activities** | Offer them as an optional later fluency mode after untimed comprehension and accuracy. A timer is a poor first teacher for unfamiliar sounds. |
| **Permissions and roles** | Allow learner progress, and later give an author or teacher a controlled way to manage lessons and media. Publishing a lesson should not require changing dictionary canon. |

No account system or Learning Center interface is currently present in the inspected app files. The build manual's local storage proposal is a starting architecture, while the later conversation makes clear that **accounts should be anticipated**. A practical design is a versioned progress record with stable IDs and a clean sync boundary. That lets an initial local course work without painting the product into a corner.

## 7. What remains before this is a complete course

The biggest missing piece is **integration and learner testing**, not another stack of lesson text.

1. **Map lessons to real media.** For every objective, choose a specific conversation turn, word clip, example clip, and grammar passage. Record what is taught before the turn and what is only inferred from context.
2. **Refine the 36 packs.** Check clarity, pace, naturalness, translation fidelity, pronunciation notes, accepted variants, and whether the independent task truly tests the stated objective. Some early packs may deserve a different scene-led opening now that audio exists.
3. **Create the exercise bank.** The current packs contain six prompts each, but they are prose lessons, not app-ready items with stable IDs, answer rules, feedback, audio references, and safe variants. At six to ten practice items per lesson, the plan implies about **216–360 practice items**, before checks and reviews.
4. **Write assessments and readings.** The outline calls for twelve weekly checks, mixed checks at Weeks 4 and 8, a 75–100 word Week 10 reading, and a 100–150 word Week 12 capstone reading. These need verified Celan, faithful English, answer evidence, and an answer key.
5. **Prepare a small weekly vocabulary path.** Select only the words the learner needs; attach sound, meaning in context, an example, and recall prompts. The full dictionary stays available for curiosity.
6. **Listen through course audio.** The 90 scene files are first takes. Review pronunciation, voice assignment, mood, pacing, and Celan/English alignment before using them as final teaching audio. Retake only specific failures. Add turn-level playback or clips if the lesson interface needs it.
7. **Build the Learning Center.** Add curriculum data, the two study modes, feedback, progress, review scheduling, audio controls, accessibility, and a usable conversation library to the existing app. Plan stable IDs and eventual accounts from the beginning.
8. **Pilot with learners.** Weeks 1–4 are the first meaningful test. Watch where someone understands a scene but fails a drill, or passes a drill but cannot answer a person. Use that evidence to change the teaching sequence.

The supporting resources named in the course outline still matter: a curated example bank; weekly vocabulary deck; printable practice sheets; scene cards; culture and address notes; teacher and self-study guidance; and a corrected podcast companion. They should be developed around the actual lesson sequence, rather than produced as disconnected documents.

## 8. A sensible next production sequence

**First: make one complete learner unit.** Take Weeks 1–2, select a small set of strong audio scenes, refine the six lesson packs around them, and write their exercises and checks in app-ready form. This proves the teaching approach and the media mapping.

**Second: build the Learning Center shell and progress model.** The learner should be able to enter, continue, choose a study path, complete a lesson, hear the linked media, and see a due review. Keep stable IDs and a versioned progress record so accounts can be added without rewriting course content.

**Third: pilot and adjust.** Let a few true beginners attempt the unit. Note confusion, boredom, pronunciation trouble, surprising interpretations, and where a scene creates curiosity. Revise the course before converting all 36 packs.

**Fourth: expand in curriculum batches.** Build Weeks 3–4, then 5–8, then 9–12. Put connected readings and the capstone under the same translation and audio standards as the conversations. Release the conversation library alongside the course, with helpful routes into relevant lessons.

This sequence preserves the large ambition. It also gives us a real product milestone: a person can learn something, remember it, and use it in a scene. That is a stronger proof than having 36 PDFs or 90 MP3s alone.

## 9. Decisions to carry forward

- **Celan learner spelling stays canonical.** Pronunciation spellings and mood tags belong in performance copy, not subtitles or lesson answers.
- **English must match the Celan closely enough to verify it.** Natural English is welcome; invented extra meaning is not.
- **Human purpose comes before the grammar display.** Each conversation and exercise should have a reason to speak.
- **The three conversation volumes have distinct jobs.** Volume 1 is ordinary relationships and life; Volume 2 is plans and problems; Volume 3 is precision. They can serve the course without being squeezed into it.
- **All 36 packs are drafts open to refinement.** Their existence is a production achievement, not a decision that the original theory and every example are final.
- **The approved Dictionary App and Master Grammar Guide are the language backbone.** Teaching material may sequence and explain them, but should not quietly invent a form.
- **Audio changes the course design.** It allows listening, replay, turn-level study, and sound-to-meaning practice from the start; the course should take advantage of that.
- **Accounts are a product direction.** The initial implementation can be simple, but the data model should support learners returning on another device.
- **A compelling course builds interest in Ohnosha and Celan together.** The best sign of success is that a learner wants to understand the next person, not merely finish the next screen.
