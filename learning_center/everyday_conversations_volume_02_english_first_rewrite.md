# Volume 2 — English-first rewrite, Scenes 25–29

This is a **conversation draft**, separate from the Volume 2 master and ElevenLabs scripts. The English scene is written first. Below it, each spoken line is checked against current Celan. An empty Celan slot means the intended meaning has **not** been translated yet; it is not permission to replace it with a different meaning. Stage directions are not spoken.

Nara and Toren arrive home after the earlier strain between them. The door opens inward and catches at the top. The hinge needs attention. Toren is tired; Nara wants to solve the problem quickly. The five scenes let both of those needs matter.

## 25. At the door

*Toren tries the door. It barely moves.*

```text
Toren: It's catching at the top
Nara: Pull harder
Toren: That's how we broke it last time
Nara: Let me try
```

*Nara tries. The door still catches.*

```text
Toren: Harder?
Nara: Get the toolkit
```

The second “Harder?” gives Toren a little satisfaction without either person explaining the joke.

## 26. The hinge

*Nara finds the loose hinge while Toren holds a lamp.*

```text
Nara: The hinge is loose
Toren: That's why I didn't pull harder
Nara: You could have said that
Toren: I said it was catching at the top
Nara: Right  I missed that
Toren: You did
Nara: Hold the lamp
```

Nara was listening for a fix; Toren was already describing the problem. Neither character has to become a fool for the disagreement to work.

## 27. Still standing

*Nara works on the hinge. Toren keeps the door from moving.*

```text
Nara: Hold the door steady
Toren: I am
Nara: You're shaking
Toren: I told you I was tired
Nara: I heard you
Toren: You kept going
Nara: I'm sorry  Sit down
Toren: When we're inside
```

This is the turn in the relationship. Toren is trying to help but needs Nara to take the fatigue seriously. Nara stops defending the repair and answers the person.

## 28. One more minute

*Nara adjusts the hinge while Toren holds the door.*

```text
Nara: One more minute  Can you hold it?
Toren: For a minute
Nara: That's all I need
```

*Nara finishes the adjustment.*

```text
Nara: Try it
Toren: It opens
Nara: Close it  Open it again
Toren: No
Nara: You don't trust it?
Toren: I trust the door  I don't trust you to stop testing it
```

The last line lets Toren be funny without erasing the earlier disagreement. Nara's request for another test is reasonable; Toren's refusal is reasonable too.

## 29. At the table

*Nara brings out a bowl of berries left from the market trip.*

```text
Nara: Berries?
Toren: You saved some?
Nara: For us
```

*Toren pushes the bowl toward Nara so they can share it.*

```text
Toren: You're hungry too
Nara: Yes
Toren: Sit with me
```

The final line answers Nara's “Sit down” from Scene 27. The repair is over, and they both get to rest.

## Celan rendering, line by line

These are **not recording scripts**. The Celan lines below use established forms where they can carry the English meaning. A blank cell names the exact meaning still needed. A short English fragment can be a complete spoken turn; it does not need extra words merely to fill a grammar template.

### 25. At the door

| English turn | Celan rendering or gap |
| --- | --- |
| It's catching at the top | **Gap:** ordinary *catch/stick* of a door, plus its upper part. |
| Pull harder | **Gap:** physical *pull* as a verb and an ordinary *harder/with more force* instruction. **`Rav`** is a noun for pull or yearning; its existence does not authorize a new verb form. |
| That's how we broke it last time | **Gap:** ordinary damage to an object and a natural “that's how … last time” construction. **`Pelvokaen`** is specifically to snap with a sharp crack. |
| Let me try | **Gap:** a natural request to take a turn. **`Siv`** supplies *try*, but a faithful everyday permission pattern still needs a settled form here. |
| Harder? | **Gap:** the comparative force word, now used as a teasing echo. |
| Get the toolkit | `Va vekaen Ya karvek.` — “Bring the toolkit.” In this situation, bringing it is the requested action; if “get” is meant without bringing it back, the English must change. |

### 26. The hinge

| English turn | Celan rendering or gap |
| --- | --- |
| The hinge is loose | **Gap:** hinge and the physical state *loose*. |
| That's why I didn't pull harder | **Gap:** depends on the physical pull and degree of force from Scene 25, and needs a causal reply that preserves *that's why*. |
| You could have said that | **Gap:** missed opportunity in the past plus *that* referring to Toren's observation. |
| I said it was catching at the top | **Gap:** reported speech can be built from **`Aen`**, but *catching at the top* still lacks an exact rendering. |
| Right. I missed that | `Lia'ser.` covers “Right.” **Gap:** “I missed that” means *I failed to notice what you said*, not “I forgot.” |
| You did | **Gap:** clipped confirmation of “you missed that.” A plain **`Lia`** would mean “yes” but would lose the pointed echo. |
| Hold the lamp | `Va korinaen Ya shalkor.` — “Hold the lamp.” |

### 27. Still standing

| English turn | Celan rendering or gap |
| --- | --- |
| Hold the door steady | `Va korinaen Ya vorkor.` means “Hold the door.” **Gap:** *steady* matters to the repair and should not disappear. |
| I am | `Korinaen I vorkor.` — “I am holding the door.” English leaves the repeated words understood. |
| You're shaking | `Rivaen Ya.` — “You're trembling/shaking.” **`Rivaen`** covers small involuntary motion from strain as well as cold or fear. |
| I told you I was tired | **Gap:** a natural reported-speech construction that keeps the earlier warning and past tense. **`Felkal`** supplies *tired*. |
| I heard you | `Tha-aenaen I Ya.` — “I heard you.” |
| You kept going | **Gap:** *continue working despite what I said*, rather than physically go somewhere. |
| I'm sorry. Sit down | **Gap:** an ordinary apology distinct from “pardon?” or “excuse me.” `Va nethaen Ya.` — “Sit down.” |
| When we're inside | **Gap:** a natural “when” condition for entering, distinct from **`ra … nu?`**, which asks *when?* |

### 28. One more minute

| English turn | Celan rendering or gap |
| --- | --- |
| One more minute. Can you hold it? | **Gap:** short duration and *one more*. **`Korinaen`** supplies *hold*; the request's exact modal and object wording still need checking. |
| For a minute | **Gap:** duration *for a minute*, used as a limit Toren sets. |
| That's all I need | **Gap:** *need* plus *that's all*, referring to the limited time. |
| Try it | `Va siv Ya kaxaraen vorkor.` — “Try to open the door.” The English “it” refers to that same attempt. |
| It opens | **Gap:** a door opening as the subject of the event. `Kaxaraen I vorkor` means “I open the door,” which is a different line. |
| Close it. Open it again | `Va xaraen Ya vorkor. Va kaxaraen Ya vorkor wek'aen.` — “Close the door. Open the door again.” |
| No | `Ka.` — “No.” |
| You don't trust it? | **Needs usage decision:** **`Seraen`** means *trust/ally with* and has examples with a person or an ideal. Applying it to a repaired door could be a natural extension, but the current examples do not establish that use. |
| I trust the door. I don't trust you to stop testing it | **Gap:** the contrast depends on the door use of *trust*, the ordinary action *stop*, and the ongoing action *test*. A simpler line about being tired would lose the joke. |

### 29. At the table

| English turn | Celan rendering or gap |
| --- | --- |
| Berries? | `Vilor?` — “Berries?” **`Vilor`** names small sweet fruit; “berries” keeps this offer specific. |
| You saved some? | **Gap:** *set aside/keep a portion for someone to eat later*. The dictionary's “save” words concern survival or recorded information. |
| For us | **Gap:** a purpose or beneficiary phrase meaning *for the two of us*. **`Ilin`** means *we/us* inclusively, but possession is not the same as this line. |
| You're hungry too | `Rinaen Ya emilrav sera.` — “You're hungry too.” |
| Yes | `Lia.` — “Yes.” |
| Sit with me | `Va nethaen Ya ser I.` — “Sit with me.” |

## What this attempt shows

The English scene can now stand without translation. The Celan check reveals a **small cluster of highly reusable everyday needs**: a door hinge, something catching or being loose, physical pulling and degrees of force, setting food aside, a plain apology, a short duration, and stopping an ongoing action. It also surfaces questions about reported speech, permission to take a turn, and trusting a working object. Those are language-development questions, not reasons to make Nara and Toren speak less naturally.

No candidate headwords have been added to the dictionary here. The next language pass should decide which needs a new word, which can use an existing root or ordinary phrase, and which needs a grammar or usage example. Then every line can receive Celan and a fresh English back-translation before any audio is recorded.
