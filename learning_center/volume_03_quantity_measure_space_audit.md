# Volume 3 foundation audit: quantity, measure, and space

This audit reads the **current Dictionary App**: its visible word meanings, examples, and Grammar Guide in [dictionary/app_data](../dictionary/app_data/README.md). It also checks what the [Volume 1](everyday_conversations_volume_01.md) and [Volume 2](everyday_conversations_volume_02_celan_vernacular.md) conversations already use. It proposes no new Celan spellings. A missing form here means the current app content does not give a clear, general expression for it; it does not mean the idea could never be expressed in Celan.

## What Volume 3 can teach

Volume 3 can move from **identifying a thing** to **being precise about it**:

1. **How many?** Exact counts, larger numbers, and counts in a real exchange.
2. **How much?** Some, few, many, all, each, enough, full, and empty.
3. **How large or heavy?** Handspans, strides, journeys, stone-weights, and measured quantities.
4. **Which one?** This by me, that by you, that yonder.
5. **Where?** Here, left, right, between, in front, behind, inside, outside, through, and across.
6. **How far?** Existing numeric distance examples work. A general near/far pair and a nearer/farther comparison are not yet explicit in the app.

The strongest scene pattern is a small consequential correction: “three, not two”; “that one by you, not this one”; “outside the door, not inside”; “enough water for this task, but not for the next.” Each correction makes the distinction worth saying.

## 1. Exact number system

### Base words

| Value | Celan | Value | Celan |
| ---: | --- | ---: | --- |
| 0 | **verin** | 6 | **senar** |
| 1 | **unar** | 7 | **sethe** |
| 2 | **del** | 8 | **oven** |
| 3 | **tren** | 9 | **nevar** |
| 4 | **quen** | 10 | **tenar** |
| 5 | **peth** |  |  |

The app has full-sentence counting examples: `var tren serin an dren.` (“Three friends go to the water”), `rinaen quen trakorin an xaror.` (“There are four boots in the room”), and `rinaen verin serin an lorash.` (“There are zero friends at the shrine”). One is also attested with a singular noun: `rinaen unar shalkor an xaror.` (“There is one lamp in the room”).

**Count-noun caution.** Examples with three or more often use a plural noun, but `nakaen del sivvok an jornak.` (“Two teams compete in the race”) uses `sivvok` without a visible plural ending. Volume 3 should teach the actual approved phrase for each counted noun rather than announcing an automatic plural rule after every numeral.

### Scales and arithmetic

| Construction | Value | Attested model |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `tenar unar` | 11 | 10 + 1 |
| **Ten-** + number | tens | `Ten-del` = 20 |
| **Hek-** + number | hundreds | `Hek-quen` = 400 |
| **Mel-** + number | thousands | `Mel-unar` = 1,000 |
| **Fen-** + number | tens of thousands | `Fen-peth` = 50,000 |
| **Hek-Mel-** + number | hundreds of thousands | `Hek-Mel-quen` = 400,000 |
| Larger block then smaller block | addition by sequence | `Hek-oven Ten-quen del` = 842 |
| **ven** between numbers | subtraction | `Hek-quen ven Hek-del` = 400 − 200 |

The hyphen binds a scale marker to its number. A space starts the next block. The [Master Grammar Guide §12](../docs/CELAN_MASTER_GRAMMAR_GUIDE.md#12-counting-and-measure) explains this with full equations. The app also gives `Mel-quen Hek-sethe Ten-oven sethe` = 4,787.

**What not to infer from the chart:** addition is expressed by adjoining number blocks; the app does not establish a general spoken word for “plus.” The multiplication sign in the teaching equations explains the scale prefix; it is not a Celan multiplication word. Subtraction has **ven**. General division, multiplication, equality, and fractions are not given comparable spoken patterns.

### Numbers in lived sentences

The app already supports several scene-ready shapes:

| Celan | English |
| --- | --- |
| `var tren serin an dren.` | Three friends go to the water. |
| `rinaen quen trakorin an xaror.` | There are four boots in the room. |
| `nakaen del sivvok an jornak.` | Two teams compete in the race. |
| `rinaen Mel-unar welrinin an Varthas.` | There are 1,000 citizens in the city. |
| `rinaen I Ten-del tharvinwek kaveth.` | I am 20 years old. |

The last example writes **tharvinwek** as one word. The current headword for “year” is **Tharvin-Wek**, and its entry explicitly accepts **tharvinwek** as the fused spelling. Volume 3 can teach the headword first and recognize the fused form in sentences. The form **tenar** also has a separate noun meaning “radiance”; context distinguishes the numeral.

## 2. Amount without an exact count

| Form | Current meaning | Supported use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ilinor** | all, entire, whole | Before the noun. |
| **ekor** | each, every | Before the noun; attends to members individually. |
| **som** | some, a few, part of | Before the noun. |
| **melen** | many, much, numerous | Before the noun. |
| **felin** | few, little, small amount | Before the noun; app examples use both people and food. |
| **worlian** | enough, sufficient for the need | The app's complete examples are predicative: `rinaen dren worlian.` (“The water is enough”). |
| **tek** | just, only, merely | Limits what is being named. |
| **fel** | slightly, a bit | Diminishes an action or description. |
| **felath** | almost | Near completion or realization, **not** physical nearness. |

The Grammar Guide's ordinary amount words precede the noun: `var som serin.` (“Some friends go”) and `var felin serin.` (“Few friends go”). **Worlian** deserves its own pattern in the course. Its app examples say `rinaen dren worlian.` and `rinaen droshen worlian.`; they do not show it preceding a noun.

For supplies and containers, the app also has **woreth** (“full, abundant”), **zun** (“empty”), **worsul** (“resource surplus”), and **kasul** (“resource shortage”). The clean contrast is `rinaen drenvor woreth.` (“The bowl is full”) beside an appropriately established empty-container sentence. The app already records `rinaen drenvor woreth felath.` (“The bowl is almost full”). **Feleth** can mean light in weight, small, or new; **maganeth** can mean heavy, big, or imposing. Let the object and situation establish the intended sense.

### Comparisons

**Ther** follows a description to open “more than”; **thaal** after it means “most.” The [Grammar Guide §3.4](../docs/CELAN_MASTER_GRAMMAR_GUIDE.md#34-describing-and-comparing) gives `rinaen dren-ian shaleth ther La-ka dren.` (“My water is brighter than their water”) and `rinaen dren-ian shaleth thaal.` (“My water is brightest”). The app does **not** give an equally clear general marker for “less than,” “fewer than,” or “the same amount as.” Reversing a comparison may communicate an idea, but it should not be presented as an approved fixed expression for those English meanings.

## 3. Measuring the world

| Domain | Form | Meaning and use | Existing model |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small length | **korineth** | Handspan; useful in craft and short distances. | `Rinaen zhirin Ten-del korineth.` — The rope is 20 handspans long. |
| Length sense | **kor** | A handspan length measure; also a separate “center/core” noun. | The `korineth` example is clearer for beginners. |
| Medium distance | **shar** | Stride or pace. | `rinaen kaleth Hek-unar shar thalor.` — The fortress is 100 strides outside. |
| Long distance | **kinnshara** | Day's journey, a travel-distance benchmark. | `Rinaen Varthas Ten-peth kinnshara.` — The city is 50 day's-journeys away. |
| Heavy goods | **morkal** | Stone-weight. | `Rinaen morthera maganeth an Hek-unar morkal.` — The beast of burden is heavy by 100 stone-weights. |
| Trade quantity | **shen** | A measure or count unit after a numeral. | `tal I peth shen morlak.` — I give five measures of grain. |
| Brief time | **aenor** | Moment or instant; also a separate “ear” sense. | `an aenor` — in a moment. |
| Day | **kinn** | Day. | Useful as a period; do not confuse with the distance unit `kinnshara`. |
| Moon cycle | **lunwek** | Month or full lunar cycle. | `unar lunwek` — one month. |
| Year | **Tharvin-Wek** | Full cycle of seasons. | Current headword spelling. |

The broad pattern is **number before the unit**: `Ten-del korineth`, `Hek-unar shar`, `peth shen`. These units do different jobs. A **kinnshara** is a distance described through travel time; it is not an ordinary day. A **morkal** is a weight benchmark, not a generic unit for every kind of measurement.

**Shen is deliberately polysemous.** Its visible meanings include measure, currency, and “to measure/to count.” Other trade words include **shena** (“coin”), **shenakar** (“coinage/currency system”), and **belshen** (“price”). In a trade scene, `peth shen` needs enough surrounding context for a learner to tell whether the speakers mean measures or money.

The dictionary has words for measurement itself and its tools: **shenlian** (reference standard), **shenzhir** (ruler or measuring rod), **shenmek** (gauge or meter), **normek** (clock), **lormek** (compass), and **shenlianaen** (calibrate). It also names measured properties such as **krezshen** (temperature), **morshen** (mass), **jorvar** (speed), and **ndavi** (vertical level). These give Volume 3 settings beyond shopping: building, travel, weather, and careful work. The app does not yet supply a unit and everyday sentence pattern for every property it names.

## 4. Which one: Celan's three-way pointing system

| Form | Whose sphere? | Current meaning | App example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **deni** | Speaker's immediate sphere | this | `dor Ya karvek deni an pralor.` — Put this toolkit on the table. |
| **denya** | Listener's immediate sphere | that by you | `ra rinaen karvek denya lianeth?` — Is that toolkit by you reliable? |
| **denla** | Away from both | that yonder | `kes I mekor denla.` — I notice that building over there. |

This is more useful than a simple English *this/that* pair. It lets one speaker distinguish **my reach**, **your reach**, and **a third place**. The app explicitly gives `va var Ya an deni.` (“Come here”). Current Volume 2 scenes also use `an denya` for a place by the listener. The app's direct examples do not yet provide a matching stand-alone locative sentence with **denla**. For a Volume 3 “come/go there” lesson, establish full place sentences for all three positions before teaching them as a uniform paradigm.

**Deni, denya, and denla are pointing words, not distance units.** Denla can identify something away from both people. It does not by itself say whether that thing is ten strides or ten days' journeys away.

## 5. Where: position, orientation, boundary, and movement

### Place and direction

| Form | Relation | Existing sentence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **an** | at, in, or on a place; context matters | `var I an dren.` — I go to the water. |
| **lo** | where, in a content question | `ra rinaen karvek lo?` — Where is the toolkit? |
| **naku** | left relative to a facing or route | `va jekaen Ya naku.` — Turn left. |
| **sora** | right relative to a facing or route | `va jekaen Ya sora.` — Turn right. |
| **serdel** | between two anchors | `rinaen krezor serdel nethlor ser pralor.` — The hearth is between the bed and table. |
| **shalvar** | ahead of, in front of | `rinaen seren shalvar vorkor.` — The guardian is in front of the door. |
| **vethvar** | behind, at the back of | `rinaen seren vethvar vorkor.` — The guardian is behind the door. |
| **esh** | above, over | `var I esh dren.` — I go above the water. |
| **morl** | under, beneath | `var I morl dren.` — I go beneath the water. |
| **shan** | through or across a boundary | `var I shan vorkor.` — I go through the door. |

Left and right require a **point of view**. `rinaen nethor naku I.` means the chair is to **my** left; `rinaen vorkor sora I.` means the door is to **my** right. A scene should make the person's facing or direction of travel clear. “Left” on a map and “to your left” are not automatically the same instruction.

### Inside, outside, toward, and from

| Form | Job | Existing sentence or meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **anor** | interior as an area | `rinaen dro an anor xarvek.` — Water is inside the chest. |
| **kaan** | outside/beyond a boundary, with an anchor | `dor Ya karvek kaan vorkor.` — Put the toolkit outside the door. |
| **thalor** | outside as a position | `rinaen mekral thalor.` — The mechanic is outside. |
| **anshanaen** | enter, cross from outside to inside | Established verb meaning. |
| **thalshanaen** | exit, cross from inside to outside | Established verb meaning. |
| **var** | go/move; also “to/towards” | Destination and movement depend on the whole sentence. |
| **thal** | from/out of | Marks source or outward relation. |
| **varan** | reside, stay, be located | `varan I an Varthas.` — I reside in the city. |
| **dor** | put/place at a location | `dor Ya karvek deni an pralor.` — Put this toolkit on the table. |
| **min** | go/walk/head over, casual | Useful in everyday dialogue. |
| **sharaen** | follow a person or route | `va sharaen Ya sharavok denla.` — Follow that caravan over there. |
| **jekaen** | turn or change facing | `va jekaen Ya naku.` — Turn left. |

The [Grammar Guide §11](../docs/CELAN_MASTER_GRAMMAR_GUIDE.md#11-relations-of-place-time-and-accompaniment) explains that a motion verb can take a destination directly in some established frames, while **an** makes a relation explicit. Volume 3 should use complete attested sentence frames; it should not treat every place word as interchangeable after every motion verb.

## 6. What the first 60 scenes already cover

This count checks the current written scene scripts, not the recordings. Volume 1 and Volume 2 each have 30 scenes.

| Topic | Already present | Volume 3 opportunity |

| Left/right | Both occur in Volume 2's route scene. | Use changing viewpoint and correction, beyond a single turn instruction. |
| Front/behind/between | **vethvar** appears; **shalvar** and **serdel** are absent. | Build location puzzles with two anchors. |

Volume 3 therefore has real new territory. It need not repeat the existing conversations with different numbers inserted.

## 7. Meanings that need a clear form or pattern

The app's visible word meanings and examples do **not** currently establish the following as general everyday expressions:

1. **Near / far as relative location:** The app sometimes translates **an** as “near” in context, but **an** also means at/in/on. **Felath** means “almost,” not spatially near. **Denla** points away from both speakers but does not measure distance. A direct near/far pair is the largest Volume 3 gap.
2. **Nearer / farther and closest / farthest:** **Ther** and **thaal** can compare an established adjective, but a general distance adjective is not supplied. Do not coin one silently by combining roots.
3. **A full here/there/over-there locative set:** `an deni` is directly attested; Volume 2 uses `an denya`. A parallel **an denla** sentence should be established before it is presented as a routine learner transformation.
4. **How far?** **Lo** asks where. The app has numeric distance statements, but no general question frame explicitly meaning “How far is it?”
5. **Fractions and proportional amounts:** No clear everyday half, quarter, twice, ratio, or percentage pattern.
6. **Ordinals:** No general first/second/third numbering pattern. Dual pronouns such as **Inko** (“we two”) count participants; they are not an ordinal system.
7. **Approximate and bounded amounts:** No general “about five,” “at least five,” or “no more than five.” **Felath** (“almost”) and **shenreth** (measurement uncertainty) do different jobs.
8. **Less/fewer/equal:** **Ther** gives “more than”; no matched general “less than,” “fewer than,” or “the same amount.” **Lian'lia** means “Exactly!” as agreement, not “exactly five.”
9. **Capacity and everyday volume:** **Shen** provides a generic measure, but no specific capacity unit or standard “a cup of” construction is given. A bowl or bottle can contain something without automatically becoming a measurement unit.
10. **Price and quantity phrasing:** The words for coin, currency, measure, and price exist. Full scenes should distinguish “five measures of grain,” “five coins,” and “the price is five coins” with verified sentence patterns.
11. **Number agreement after count nouns:** Examples differ between plural-marked nouns and unmarked collective/unit nouns. Define teaching models noun by noun before building an automated count exercise.

These are opportunities for deliberate language development. They should remain visible in the Volume 3 writing process: draft the natural English scene, test whether the current Celan can say each line, and either choose an exact established meaning or develop the needed expression openly.

## 8. A 30-scene Volume 3 shape

These are **situation briefs**, not Celan dialogue or translations. The first four groups can largely draw on established forms. The last two depend more heavily on resolving the gaps above.

| Scenes | Focus | Human reason for the exchange |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–5 | Count and correct | Someone brings the wrong number of tools, people, seeds, chairs, or coins; the correction changes the task. |
| 6–10 | Enough and not enough | Divide water, food, or supplies; distinguish some, few, all, each, full, empty, and enough for a stated need. |
| 11–15 | Measure for work | Cut a rope by handspans, place something a number of strides away, weigh goods, count measures, and check a reference standard. |
| 16–20 | Point to the right thing | Choose the thing by me, by you, or yonder; one person reaches for the wrong one and is redirected. |
| 21–25 | Move through space | Give left/right turns, place an object between two others, locate it in front/behind, and cross an inside/outside boundary. |
| 26–30 | Distance and distinction | Choose a nearer or farther place, ask how far, compare two routes, use an approximate amount, and settle a precise trade or travel plan. Write these after the missing forms are established. |

Volume 3 should stay **standalone and useful** like the revised Volume 2. A scene can teach one distinction without feeling like a spoken worksheet. The learner should be able to hear why the speaker cares about the quantity or location.

## Production sequence

1. Make a compact teaching set from the attested examples above, including each word's exact meaning and complete sentence frame.
2. Settle the high-impact gaps: general near/far, nearer/farther, how far, locative pointing with **denla**, and price versus measure phrasing.
3. Write five English-first scenes across different groups; translate each Celan line back into English without looking at the intended English.
4. Use those five to establish the Volume 3 voice: practical, warm, occasionally mistaken, and responsive to what the other person says.
5. Write the remaining 25 scenes, with a separate pronunciation and mood copy for recording. Keep the written Celan as the learner text.
6. Map selected scenes into the 12-week course: place in Week 6, number in Week 8, comparison during review, and the rest in the conversation library.

This audit is about what the current app can teach. It does not replace a listening pass on the Volume 1 and 2 first-take audio.
