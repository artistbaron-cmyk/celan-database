# Celan everyday conversations — Volume 2 English-first draft

**Earlier writing draft.** The [Celan vernacular pass](everyday_conversations_volume_02_celan_vernacular.md) is the current 30-scene script. Its English lines translate the Celan turns directly; some situations and phrasing changed to fit established Celan speech.

Thirty **standalone** conversations for Nara, Toren, and Varadan. A listener can start with any scene. The names recur, but no scene depends on another. The situations are ordinary Ohnoshan life: home, roads, weather, food, work, trade, rest, and friendship.

This is the **English writing pass**. It is not a Celan translation or an ElevenLabs upload script. The point is to hear whether the people sound like they mean what they say. Each exchange has a practical use for a learner. The [scene-by-scene translation audit](everyday_conversations_volume_02_translation_audit.md) identifies established Celan anchors and the meanings that still need to be settled. Translate each Celan line back into English without using this draft as a crutch. Keep any new word or unresolved construction visible until it is settled.

## 1. Almost left

**Useful for:** explaining a delay without turning it into a long excuse.

*Nara is waiting by the door.*

```text
Nara: I was about to leave.
Toren: I know. I couldn't find my key.
Nara: Where was it?
Toren: In the door.
Nara: Then we're ready.
Toren: Don't tell anyone.
```

## 2. Coming along

**Useful for:** inviting someone and finding out what they actually want from the trip.

```text
Nara: I'm going to the market. Want to come?
Toren: Are you buying bread?
Nara: Thread.
Toren: We can get bread on the way back.
Nara: That's why you want to come?
Toren: It's one reason.
```

## 3. Not today

**Useful for:** declining a request without ending the relationship.

```text
Nara: Can you help me this afternoon?
Toren: Not today. I promised I'd be home.
Nara: I only need a hand for a little while.
Toren: I know. Ask me tomorrow.
Nara: Tomorrow works.
```

## 4. A better time

**Useful for:** replacing a vague plan with a time both people can use.

```text
Toren: Are we still meeting at noon?
Nara: Can we make it later?
Toren: How much later?
Nara: After the rain.
Toren: That's not a time.
Nara: When the market opens again.
Toren: I can find you then.
```

## 5. Which stall?

**Useful for:** asking for directions and correcting an incomplete answer.

```text
Nara: Where did Varadan move his stall?
Toren: Past the well. On the left.
Nara: I went there. That's the cloth seller.
Toren: Then go one stall farther.
Nara: You could have started with that.
```

## 6. The wrong door

**Useful for:** clarifying a location before entering the wrong place.

```text
Toren: This isn't the house.
Nara: You said it had a blue door.
Toren: I said the door beside the blue one.
Nara: So who's inside?
Toren: Let's leave before we find out.
```

## 7. The missing knife

**Useful for:** retracing what someone was doing instead of arguing about a lost item.

```text
Nara: Have you seen my small knife?
Toren: I put it by the sink.
Nara: It's not there.
Toren: You were cutting bread when I came in.
Nara: So?
Toren: Look beside the bread.
```

## 8. Borrowing the awl

**Useful for:** borrowing a tool and agreeing when it must come back.

```text
Toren: Can I borrow your awl?
Nara: For the leather?
Toren: Just one hole. I'll bring it back tonight.
Nara: I need it this afternoon.
Toren: Then I'll use it here and give it straight back.
Nara: That works.
```

## 9. The heavy end

**Useful for:** asking someone to share a physical task and saying when a description misled you.

```text
Nara: Take the other end.
Toren: It's heavier than you said.
Nara: I said it was small.
Toren: Small isn't light.
Nara: Fair. Put it down for a moment.
```

## 10. Bring the bowls here

**Useful for:** offering the part of a task you can do and making another plan for the rest.

```text
Toren: Can you help me wash these bowls?
Nara: I can wash them. I can't carry them all.
Toren: Then I'll bring them to you.
Nara: Put them by the water.
Toren: Here?
Nara: Yes. That's close enough.
```

## 11. Under the shelter

**Useful for:** making room for someone when the first attempt does not quite work.

```text
Nara: Come in. You're soaked.
Toren: There's no room.
Nara: There is if I move.
Toren: Now you're getting wet.
Nara: Then we'll both move.
Toren: Where?
Nara: Closer to the wall.
```

## 12. Just a little water

**Useful for:** accepting care without feeling obliged to take too much.

```text
Toren: Is that your water?
Nara: Yes. Do you need some?
Toren: I can wait.
Nara: You don't have to.
Toren: Then just a little.
Nara: Take it.
```

## 13. No bread yet

**Useful for:** changing a purchase when the thing you came for is unavailable.

```text
Nara: Do you have bread today?
Varadan: Not until evening.
Nara: We need food now.
Varadan: I have fruit.
Nara: Enough for two?
Varadan: If you don't turn it into a feast.
```

## 14. Three coins

**Useful for:** checking whether a price is for one item or a group.

```text
Toren: How much for these?
Varadan: Three coins.
Toren: For all of them?
Varadan: For one.
Toren: Then I'll take one.
Varadan: The small one?
Toren: The biggest one you have.
```

## 15. Hold it for me

**Useful for:** asking a seller to keep an item while you get money.

```text
Nara: I left my coins at home.
Varadan: Do you want me to hold the fruit?
Nara: Can you?
Varadan: Until evening.
Nara: I'll be back before then.
Varadan: I'll keep it here.
```

## 16. What's in it?

**Useful for:** checking food ingredients before eating.

```text
Nara: What's in this stew?
Varadan: Root vegetables and herbs.
Nara: Any nuts?
Varadan: No nuts. I can show you before you eat.
Nara: Please do.
```

## 17. The fruit underneath

**Useful for:** returning spoiled food and choosing a remedy.

```text
Toren: This fruit is spoiled.
Varadan: I gave it to you this morning.
Toren: It was under the good fruit.
Varadan: You're right. Another one or your coins back?
Toren: Another one, please.
Varadan: Here. Check it before you leave.
```

## 18. The end of the line

**Useful for:** finding where to wait without cutting ahead of someone.

```text
Nara: Is this the line for bread?
Toren: Yes. The end is back there.
Nara: I thought you were last.
Toren: I was, five people ago.
Nara: Right. I'll go back.
```

## 19. Is this seat free?

**Useful for:** asking before sitting and respecting a place kept for someone else.

```text
Toren: Is anyone sitting here?
Nara: No. Go ahead.
Toren: Are you saving the next seat?
Nara: For my brother.
Toren: I'll leave that one clear.
Nara: Thank you.
```

## 20. The last piece

**Useful for:** offering food and noticing what someone actually wants.

```text
Nara: Do you want the last piece?
Toren: You take it.
Nara: You haven't eaten.
Toren: I ate on the way here.
Nara: Then why are you looking at it?
Toren: Because it smells good.
```

## 21. Road or bridge?

**Useful for:** repeating the part of a message that matters in a noisy place.

```text
Nara: What did she say?
Toren: The bridge is closed.
Nara: The road or the bridge?
Toren: The bridge. We can use the road.
Nara: Say that again. I can't hear you here.
Toren: The road is open.
```

## 22. Near the fire

**Useful for:** checking an instruction before doing something unsafe.

```text
Nara: Put it near the fire.
Toren: On the fire?
Nara: Near it. Not in it.
Toren: Here?
Nara: Yes. That's far enough.
```

## 23. At the threshold

**Useful for:** asking to enter and giving a reason to wait.

```text
Toren: Can I come in?
Nara: Give me a moment.
Toren: Should I wait outside?
Nara: Yes. The floor is wet.
Toren: I'll stay here.
Nara: All right. Come in now.
```

## 24. Don't sit on it yet

**Useful for:** stopping someone from testing a repair before it is finished.

```text
Nara: The chair moves when I sit.
Toren: Don't sit on it again.
Nara: I need to know if it's fixed.
Toren: First let me fix it.
Nara: Fair. I'll stand.
```

## 25. Once isn't enough

**Useful for:** deciding whether to continue a repair or stop for the day.

```text
Toren: Did the door open?
Nara: Once.
Toren: And now?
Nara: It doesn't.
Toren: Then let's stop for today.
Nara: Or we find what's wrong.
Toren: Tomorrow. We've tried enough.
```

## 26. Stay a minute

**Useful for:** asking for company rather than accepting the help someone first offers.

```text
Nara: You haven't touched your food.
Toren: My stomach hurts.
Nara: Do you want water?
Toren: No. Can you stay a minute?
Nara: Of course.
```

## 27. That's not what I asked

**Useful for:** making a need for rest clear when someone focuses on finishing.

```text
Toren: Can we stop?
Nara: We're almost there.
Toren: That's not what I asked.
Nara: You're right. Let's sit.
Toren: Thank you.
```

## 28. I couldn't hear you

**Useful for:** repairing a misunderstanding before it becomes an argument.

```text
Nara: I thought you were ignoring me.
Toren: I couldn't hear you.
Nara: I should have asked. I'm sorry.
Toren: I know you were worried.
Nara: Are we all right?
Toren: We are.
```

## 29. Speak outside

**Useful for:** asking for a change of place without making it sound alarming.

```text
Toren: Can we speak outside?
Nara: Is something wrong?
Toren: No. The child is asleep.
Nara: Oh. Lead the way.
Toren: Thank you.
```

## 30. The walk home

**Useful for:** accepting company while giving the other person room to choose.

```text
Nara: You don't have to walk me home.
Toren: I know.
Nara: It's late.
Toren: That's why I'm coming.
Nara: All right. Tell me about the market on the way.
Toren: Only if you tell me why you were there so long.
```

## What to listen for in this draft

Each scene turns on a specific need: a missing key, an unclear direction, a price, a boundary, a mistaken instruction, an offer of care. The practical point should be audible without the “Useful for” line. The lines are allowed to be short, but they should not exist only to display a word. The next pass is a Celan language audit and faithful translation, followed by separate recording copies with pronunciation and delivery choices.
