# Volume 2 — translation audit for the 30 English-first conversations

**Historical planning audit.** The later [Celan vernacular pass](everyday_conversations_volume_02_celan_vernacular.md) adapts all 30 situations to the established language and pairs each Celan turn with its actual English meaning. The recording input is in [the performance copy](new_volume_02_recording_copy.md). The gaps below describe the earlier English draft, not unresolved requirements for the vernacular pass.

This is the bridge between the [30 English scripts](everyday_conversations_volume_02_english_first_30.md) and a faithful Celan script. The English scenes are the writing target. A Celan line must say what its English line says; a related sentiment is not enough. A recording copy comes after the written Celan and a fresh English back-translation agree turn by turn.

The approved Dictionary App supplies the words below. A word's presence does not prove that a whole English sentence already has an established Celan construction. In particular, a question with **can**, a promise, an offer, and a request for permission do different things. The grammar guide gives useful patterns, but those conversational uses still need to be tested in complete lines.

## What the language already gives us

- **Actions and time:** `var` (go), `tal` (give/take in context), `dor` (put), `dar` (wait), `aen` (speak/tell), `aenaen` (hear), `nemaen` (understand), `mbaen` (help), `drenselaen` (wash), `vekaen` (carry), `pelaen` (cut), `zhaelpralaen` (repair), `kaxaraen` (open), `xaraen` (close), `nethaen` (sit), `aivaen` (ache), `nolaen` (smell), `tha-` (past), and `nor-` (future).
- **Questions and relation:** `ra` opens a question; `va` opens a command; `ver` negates an action; `mu`, `nu`, `zhe`, `sha`, `lo`, and `ri` can specify what/when/why/how/where/who; `an` marks place, `nor` time, `ser` with/and, `shalnor` before, `vethnor` after, `varnor` until, `kaan` outside, `deni` this near the speaker, and `denya` that near the listener.
- **Places, objects, and states:** `teremil` (home), `evan` (market), `eshvan` (stall/shop), `vorkor` (door), `dren` (water), `morlak` (bread), `pelkorin` (awl), `peleth` (knife), `mekrask` (prepared leather), `drenvor` (bowl), `ndara` (wall), `krez` (fire), `nethor` (seat/chair), `njor` (bridge), `mekshara` (road), `morlpral` (floor), `emiltash` (stomach), `dreneth` (wet), `ruvaneth` (spoiled), `maganeth` (heavy), `feleth` (small/light), `worlian` (enough), `felin` (a little/few), `kinnlin` (noon), `kilvthal` (afternoon), and `kilveth` (evening).
- **Food and trade:** `velrin` (food/meal), `pralfel` (ingredient), `teravin` (root vegetable), `fennil` (herb/leaf), `brenvor` (harvested nuts), `Vilor` (small sweet fruit), `anvanesh` (buy), `belshen` (price), and `shena` (coin).

These are anchors, not permission to stretch a word. `Vilor` is a **small sweet fruit**, so it cannot silently stand for all fruit. `Korinaen` means **hold in one's grip**, so it does not automatically mean **reserve for a buyer**. `Velzhiraen` means **remember**, not **find**. `Felath` means **almost**, not **late**.

## Scene-by-scene map

| Scene | Ready anchors | Meaning that still needs a settled word or construction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Almost left** | `var`, `teremil`, `vorkor`, `nemaen`, past/future | **Was about to** (an action just short of happening), **couldn't find**, **key**, **ready**, and the social request **don't tell anyone**. A simple past `tha-var` would say “I left,” which is the opposite of the first line. |
| **2. Coming along** | `var`, `evan`, `morlak`, `zhir` (thread/continuity), `anvanesh`, `ser` | Casual **Want to come?**, **on the way back**, **that's why?**, and **one reason**. Confirm the material-thread use of `Zhir` in this shopping context rather than relying on its lineage sense alone. |
| **3. Not today** | `mbaen`, `kilvthal`, `teremil`, `korin`, `aenor` (moment) | **Today/tomorrow**, **promised**, **only need**, **for a little while**, and **tomorrow works**. “I must be home” would lose the promise and the interpersonal reason for declining. |
| **4. A better time** | `kinnlin`, `vethnor`, `evan`, `aenaen`/`aen` as relevant | **Still meeting**, **make it later**, **how much later**, **rain**, **opens again**, **find you then**. The joke “That's not a time” needs a natural correction, not a literal word list. |
| **5. Which stall?** | `eshvan`, `aen`, `var`, `thal`, directional vocabulary | **Moved his stall**, **past the well**, **one stall farther**, and **you could have started with that**. The last line is a mild complaint, not a request to start walking. |
| **6. The wrong door** | `teremil`, `vorkor`, `Lunor` (blue), `serdel` (between) | **This isn't the house**, **beside the blue one**, **who's inside**, and **before we find out**. Preserve the correction: the *door beside* the blue door is meant. |
| **7. The missing knife** | `peleth`, `feleth`, `pelaen`, `morlak`, `shalaen` (see), `dor` | **Have you seen**, **sink**, **it's not there**, **when I came in**, and **look beside**. “I see” and “I know where it is” are not the same response. |
| **8. Borrowing the awl** | `pelkorin`, `mekrask`, `an`, `korinaen`, `kilvthal`, `kilveth` | **Borrow**, **one hole**, **bring/give it back**, **tonight**, **use it here**, and **straight back**. This scene depends on temporary possession; “give” alone loses the agreement. |
| **9. The heavy end** | `maganeth`, `feleth`, `dor`, `aenor` | **Other end**, **heavier than you said**, **small isn't light**, **fair**, **put it down for a moment**. The contrast between size and weight is the scene's point; `Feleth` can carry both and must be disambiguated carefully. |
| **10. Bring the bowls here** | `mbaen`, `drenselaen`, `drenvor`, `vekaen`, `dor`, `dren`, `worlian` | **These bowls**, **can't carry them all**, **bring them to you**, **here?**, and **close enough**. Keep Nara's distinction between being able to wash and unable to carry. |
| **11. Under the shelter** | `xarphelor`, `dreneth`, `ndara`, `var`, `ser` | **Soaked**, **no room**, **there is if I move**, **now you're getting wet**, **both move**, **closer to the wall**. `Dreneth` means wet; it may not convey soaked. |
| **12. Just a little water** | `dren`, `dar`, `drorav` (thirst), `felin`, `tal` | **That your water?**, **do you need some?**, **you don't have to**, **then just a little**. Replacing “need some” with “are you thirsty?” changes the question; keep the offer and the polite refusal distinct. |
| **13. No bread yet** | `morlak`, `kilveth`, `velrin`, `Vilor`, `del` (two) | **Today**, **not until evening**, **now**, **fruit** as a general category, **enough for two**, and the teasing **turn it into a feast**. `Vilor` works only if the seller specifically has small sweet fruit. |
| **14. Three coins** | `belshen`, `tren` (three), `shena`, `unar` (one), `tal`, `feleth` | **How much for these?**, **all of them**, **for one**, **small one**, **biggest one you have**. Demonstratives and the scope of the price matter more than simply knowing “three coins.” |
| **15. Hold it for me** | `shena`, `teremil`, `Vilor`, `kilveth`, `shalnor`, `varnor` | **Left my coins at home**, **hold/reserve the fruit**, **can you?**, **I'll be back**, **keep it here**. `Korinaen` is physical gripping and cannot carry the seller's promise. |
| **16. What's in it?** | `teravin`, `fennil`, `brenvor`, `pralfel`, `velaen` (eat) | **This stew** (the type of stew needs choosing), **any nuts?**, **show you before you eat**, and **please do**. The nut question is a safety check, not casual ingredient trivia; preserve the clear “no nuts.” |
| **17. The fruit underneath** | `ruvaneth`, `Vilor`, `shena`, `kinnmor`, `vethvar` (behind) | **Under the good fruit**, **you're right**, **another one or your coins back?**, **check it before you leave**. A refund and a replacement are distinct remedies. |
| **18. The end of the line** | `morlak`, `dar`, `peth` (five), `vethvar` (behind) | **Queue/line**, **end of the line**, **I thought you were last**, **five people ago**, **I'll go back**. `Zhir` as thread/lineage is not automatically a waiting queue. |
| **19. Is this seat free?** | `nethor`, `nethaen`, `var`, `tal` | **Anyone sitting here?**, **go ahead** as permission, **saving the next seat**, **brother**, **leave it clear**, and **thank you**. A “free” seat means unoccupied, not freedom or price. |
| **20. The last piece** | `velaen`, `morlak` if the food is bread, `nolaen` (smell) | **Last piece**, **you haven't eaten**, **on the way here**, **why are you looking at it**, **smells good**. This scene works because Toren already ate but still wants the food; preserve that contradiction. |
| **21. Road or bridge?** | `njor`, `mekshara`, `xaraen` (close), `kaxaraen` (open), `aenaen` (hear), `wek'aen` (again) | **She said**, **bridge is closed** as a travel restriction versus physically shut, **use the road**, **say that again**, **can't hear you here**. A closure of access may need a different expression from physically closing a door. |
| **22. Near the fire** | `dor`, `krez`, `an`, `ka`/`ver` for negation | **Near/on/in** as three contrasting positions and **far enough**. The safety distinction is the entire scene; `an` alone covers at/in/on and cannot distinguish those three placements. |
| **23. At the threshold** | `anshanaen` (enter), `dar`, `morlpral`, `dreneth`, `kaan` (outside), `nor-ka` (now) | **Can I come in?** as permission, **give me a moment**, **should I wait**, **I'll stay here**, **all right**. “I am able to enter” is not the same as asking permission. |
| **24. Don't sit on it yet** | `nethor`, `nethaen`, `zhaelpralaen`, `moraen` (stand), `wek'aen` | **Moves when I sit**, **don't sit again**, **need to know if it's fixed**, **first let me fix it**, **fair**. A moving chair could wobble, tip, or slide; choose the intended fault before translating. |
| **25. Once isn't enough** | `vorkor`, `kaxaraen`, `zhaelpralaen`, `worlian`, `wek'aen` (again) | **Once**, **now it doesn't**, **stop for today**, **find what's wrong**, **tomorrow**, **we've tried enough**. Keep “opened once” separate from “is repaired.” |
| **26. Stay a minute** | `velaen`, `emiltash`, `aivaen`, `dren`, `varan`, `aenor` | **Haven't touched your food**, **my stomach hurts**, **do you want water?**, **stay a minute** as companionship, and **of course**. `Varan` can mean stay at a place, but this line asks for someone's company. |
| **27. That's not what I asked** | `nethaen`, `felath`, `nemaen`, `tal` | **Can we stop?**, **almost there**, **that's not what I asked**, **you're right**, and **thank you**. The core is a request for rest being initially deflected; do not turn Toren's line into a simple question about distance. |
| **28. I couldn't hear you** | `aenaen`, `rethaen`, `nemaen`, `ser` | **Thought you were ignoring me**, **couldn't hear**, **should have asked**, **I'm sorry**, **we all right?**. An apology needs its own force; “I understand” is not a translation of it. |
| **29. Speak outside** | `aen`, `kaan`/`thalor` (outside), `felka` (child), `vethoraen` (sleep), `lorin` (guide) | **Can we speak outside?** as a request, **is something wrong?**, **the child is asleep**, **lead the way**, **thank you**. The reason should reveal ordinary consideration, not imply a crisis. |
| **30. The walk home** | `var`, `ser`, `teremil`, `evan`, `aen`, `kilveth` | **Don't have to walk me home**, **it's late**, **that's why I'm coming**, **tell me about the market on the way**, **only if you tell me why you were there so long**. The exchange is reciprocal and gently teasing; a literal “I go with you” would erase it. |

## Language development that would unlock the most scenes

The first round should solve **reusable conversational moves**, not create a special word for each English sentence. These are concepts to develop, not newly approved Celan forms.

1. **Everyday time and sequence:** today, tomorrow, tonight, once, yet, still, then, while, and “was about to.” These recur across planning, shopping, repairs, and promises.
2. **Requests, permission, and obligation:** “Can I …?” as permission, “Can you …?” as a request, “you don't have to,” “let me,” and “should I.” The grammar guide's `lianeth` covers ability; that alone does not settle all of these uses.
3. **Social repair and reciprocity:** thank you, I'm sorry, you're right, fair, of course, promise, and “we're all right.” These carry the emotional turn in scenes 3, 17, 19, and 26–30.
4. **Temporary possession and trade:** borrow, lend, return an item, reserve it for someone, refund, replacement, and “for one / for all.” These make scenes 8 and 14–17 usable beyond vocabulary drills.
5. **Place and movement:** beside, near, in, on, past, farther, closer, back, and a queue. Scene 22 requires especially exact spatial terms because the difference affects safety.
6. **Common objects and kinship:** key, sink, material thread if `Zhir` is not used that way in ordinary trade, brother/sibling, and a general word for fruit if `Vilor` is too narrow.
7. **Perception and practical states:** find, ready, soaked, occupied/free, wobble, physically blocked/closed to travel, and “wrong” in a repair context.

## Translation and recording gate

For each turn, record the **English intention**, a **Celan line**, then a **fresh literal English back-translation**. If the back-translation changes a request into a statement, a promise into an obligation, a general fruit into a berry, or a safety direction into a vague location, revise the Celan or settle the missing expression. Only after all turns in a scene pass should it receive a separate ElevenLabs paste copy with mood tags and pronunciation choices. This leaves the written dialogue and punctuation readable while giving the actors a natural delivery script.

The existing Volume 2 Celan scripts and mood-matched upload files are a different earlier draft. They have not been replaced by these English-first scenes.
