# Supplement 4B: Celan Capitalization Rules

The Rules of Celan Capitalization

In Celan, capitalization is not used for the beginning of sentences or for all proper nouns as it is in English. Instead, it is used semantically to denote concepts of primacy, sanctity, or archetypal significance. It is a tool for adding emphasis and philosophical weight.

Rule 1: The First Word of a Sentence is NOT Capitalized
This is the most immediate difference from English. Sentences begin with a lowercase letter unless the first word itself meets one of the other criteria for capitalization.

Correct: var I an dren.
Incorrect: Var I an dren.

Rule 2: Capitalize Archetypal and Primordial Concepts
Words are capitalized when they refer to the fundamental, archetypal forces or concepts that govern the world, rather than their mundane, everyday instances.

Terra vs. terra:
Terra: Refers to the "Heart of Terra," the mystical, life-giving source of the world. The planet as a spiritual entity.
terra: Refers to mundane earth, soil, or ground.

Dral vs. dral:
Dral: The Heavens as a cosmic, divine entity or plane.
dral: The physical sky you look up at.

Rathor vs. rathor:
Rathor: History as a great, flowing force. The concept of The Past.
rathor: A specific past event or memory.

Rule 3: Capitalize Names of Nations, Specific Titles, and Proper Names
This rule is the most similar to English, but with a Celan twist.

Nations: Names of the eight nations are always capitalized. (Mechuma, Valkeldor, Pelagae, etc.)
Proper Names: Personal names are capitalized. (Kaikoa, Rorin, etc.)
Titles of Reverence: Formal, specific titles are capitalized when used as a name. This is a sign of immense respect.

var an Velmarin an verdor. (An Honorary Elder walks in the forest.)
Aen Velmarin. (The Honorary Elder speaks.) - Here, "Velmarin" is used as a direct title of address.

The Four Pillars: The names of the Four Pillars (Eh’lara, Ka’tharyn, etc.) are always capitalized as they are foundational, sacred concepts.

Rule 4: Capitalize the Pronoun "I" (I)
The pronoun for the self, I, is always capitalized. This is a philosophical choice, reflecting the sanctity and importance of the individual's consciousness and place within the greater balance. It is the one universal constant.

Rule 5: Poetic and Emotional Emphasis
In poetry, scripture, or highly emotional speech, a speaker may capitalize a word to give it immense personal or spiritual weight, even if it doesn't normally meet the criteria.

Standard: Ohmaen I an thar-ka. (I love my desire.)
Poetic/Emotional: Rinaen I an Thar-ka. (I am Desire itself.) - Here, the speaker is elevating their personal desire to an archetypal force.
