# Requests Retrieval Pack

## Phrase family

Requests, asks, polite instructions, commercial inquiries, and directed social speech.

## What to notice first

A request is not always a command.

The builder should notice whether the English phrase is:

- polite asking
- formal inquiry
- direct instruction
- commercial or practical request
- a softened command

## Strong phrasebank examples

- `ra aen ya belshen velrin?`
  - Do you speak the price of the food?
  - Notes: commercial inquiry; formal and transactional

- `li-ya, nor aen lian?`
  - May we now speak truth?
  - Notes: respectful request to enter a different conversational register

- `va var dren!`
  - Go to the water!
  - Notes: command form, useful as the firmer edge of the request family

## Grammar habits involved

- `ra` fronts a question
- `va` fronts a command
- politeness can be carried by honorifics and respectful address, not only by separate “please” wording
- requests may sound more literal, more formal, or more relational depending on the setting

## Cultural voice notes

- Mechuman requests may sound exact and procedural
- Nivveilian requests may sound restrained and respectful
- Zarithan requests may be much more direct
- Marakorian requests may sound formal and controlled

## Useful builder reminder

Do not flatten every request into a command. First decide whether the English line is asking, directing, negotiating, or inviting.
