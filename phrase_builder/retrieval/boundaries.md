# Boundaries Retrieval Pack

## Phrase family

Commands, refusals, distancing language, personal limits, and pushing something away from the self or from shared space.

## What to notice first

Boundary phrases are not all the same.

The builder should notice whether the English phrase is:

- a direct command
- a refusal
- a warning
- a request for distance
- a statement of scarcity or limit

English idioms in this family often need reshaping.

## Strong phrasebank examples

- `va var dren!`
  - Go to the water!
  - Notes: clean command form

- `ver var I ka trakor.`
  - I do not go without shoes.
  - Notes: personal limit through negation

- `ka var dren, ka emil.`
  - No water, no food.
  - Notes: blunt scarcity / limit statement

- `ver var I dren, li-ser.`
  - I cannot go, respected friend.
  - Notes: refusal phrased with restraint and respect

## Grammar habits involved

- commands front `va`
- negation fronts `ver` before the verb
- refusal often still respects overall Celan sentence shape
- English body-space idioms such as "get out of my face" may need to become presence, distance, or removal language in Celan

## Cultural voice notes

- Zarithan and Valkeldorian versions may feel harsher or more direct
- Nivveilian refusals may stay calm and spare
- Marakorian boundary language may sound more formal and controlled

## Useful builder reminder

Do not translate English boundary idioms word-for-word if Celan would more naturally express distance, refusal, or removal in a different way.
