# Time Questions Retrieval Pack

## Phrase family

Questions about time, timing, sequence, the present moment, and temporal setting.

## What to notice first

Time phrases are not only about the word `time`.

The builder should notice whether the English phrase is asking about:

- current time
- when something happens
- whether something has happened yet
- the flow or setting of an event in time

This pack exists because time questions broke early and clearly in the builder.

## Closest supporting material

- `Nor`
  - time; the flow of events; temporal setting

- `Nor-ka`
  - the wake or afterflow of time

- `Weknor`
  - cyclical or recurring time

- `nor-var`
  - future shaping through time prefix on the verb

- `Kinnlin`
  - noon / daylight culmination

## Phrasebank examples with time structure

- `vaar I thal nor.`
  - I seek peace in the balance of time.
  - Notes: philosophical rather than question-form, but useful for how time can appear as a real conceptual force

- `nor-var I nor Varthas.`
  - I will go to the city / I go in time-setting toward the city.
  - Notes: useful for temporal prefix and time-setting behavior

- `Tal I dren an Kinnlin.`
  - I drink the water at noon.
  - Notes: strong example of temporal placement with `an Kinnlin`

## Concept fields involved

- `Time, Memory, and History`
  - `Nor`

- `Action and Movement`
  - `Var`

## Grammar habits involved

- questions still need the fronted `ra`
- time does not automatically behave like place
- `nor` marks temporal setting; `an` is for location, though fixed time expressions may still appear with `an`
- the builder should not grab random time-adjacent roots and pretend that is a full time question

## Current limitation

The project does not yet have a fully settled phrasebank answer for "What time is it?"

That does not mean time is absent from the canon. It means the exact question family still needs clearer support.

## Useful builder reminder

Treat time questions as a real phrase family. Do not collapse them into loose keyword matching around `Nor`.
