# Affection Retrieval Pack

## Phrase family

Affection, care, love, emotional closeness, and warmly directed feeling.

## What to notice first

Affection in Celan may come through:

- direct love language
- heart-centered language
- connection language
- valuing language

The builder should notice whether the English phrase is:

- openly affectionate
- intimate
- gentle but not romantic
- value-affirming
- more devotional than personal

## Strong phrasebank examples

- `ohmaen I sharaka.`
  - I love freedom.
  - Notes: love phrased as connection and value, not just appetite

- `aen thar lian.`
  - The heart speaks truth.
  - Notes: useful for heart-centered sincerity and emotional truth

- `dral shal thaar.`
  - The heavens light the heart.
  - Notes: not romance, but strong emotional and spiritual warmth

## Concept fields involved

- `Heart, Desire, and Longing`

- `Relation, Connection, and Belonging`
  - `Ohm`

- `Speech, Breath, and Expression`
  - `Aen`

## Grammar habits involved

- affection may be stated directly, but Celan often prefers meaningful relation over flat sentiment labels
- intimate English phrasing may need reshaping if it relies on idioms rather than core feeling
- VSO still anchors the sentence even when the tone is soft

## Cultural voice notes

- Verdalrisian and Pelagaean affection may feel more lyrical and image-rich
- Nivveilian affection may be spare but deep
- Marakorian affection may sound more formal or elevated than casual

## Useful builder reminder

Do not assume affection always needs a modern, blunt “I love you” shape. In Celan, care may move through heart, truth, connection, or presence.
