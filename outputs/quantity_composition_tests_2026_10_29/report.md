# Celan composition tests: sharing, counting and ordering

Manual compositional and ambiguity review against current app meanings and grammar. Arithmetic checks verify intended boundaries only. No native-speaker or independent comprehension test was performed.

These are experimental compositions, not new approved dictionary examples. Existing app and grammar files were not changed. No -eth forms were introduced.

## Findings

- Fractions: the proposed numerator + thal + denominator pattern is reusable if equal shares and a shared whole are explicit in the rule. It is not already established by the ordinary meaning of thal.
- Positions: numbered labels work contextually. Labels alone do not establish an ordinal sequence or its starting point.
- Minimum amounts: enough/not enough works in practical scenes. Exact five-or-more needs a numerical use of ther, extending its currently taught adjective comparison.
- Equality: repeat a known count or apply the same unit to both recipients. A general unknown-amount equality statement remains unresolved; shenlian alone does not solve it.
- Prices: quantity phrase + -ka + belshen is a strong compositional candidate. Distinguish whole-lot price from price per measure.

## All 22 tests

### F1 — Fractions

**Celan:** va karethaen unar thal del dren.

**Intention:** Use half the water.

**Situation:** One identified supply of water.

**Result:** Proposed reusable construction

Thal supplies out of; treating the two as equal shares of the whole is the explicit new convention needed.

### F2 — Fractions

**Celan:** va karethaen unar thal quen dren.

**Intention:** Use a quarter of the water.

**Situation:** Same whole, four equal shares.

**Result:** Proposed reusable construction

Same structure scales without adding a word for quarter.

### F3 — Fractions

**Celan:** va karethaen tren thal quen dren.

**Intention:** Use three quarters of the water.

**Situation:** Same whole, four equal shares.

**Result:** Proposed reusable construction

The numerator selects three shares; the denominator fixes four equal shares.

### F4 — Fractions

**Celan:** va karethaen unar thal del dren.

**Intention:** Use half the combined water.

**Situation:** Two containers hold unequal quantities.

**Result:** Fails if interpreted as choose one container

One of two containers need not contain half the total. The fraction must describe equal abstract shares, not count existing containers.

### F5 — Fractions

**Celan:** va karethaen unar thal verin dren.

**Intention:** Use one zeroth of the water.

**Situation:** Denominator zero.

**Result:** Reject under proposed fraction rule

No division into zero equal shares is defined.

### F6 — Fractions

**Celan:** va karethaen peth thal quen dren.

**Intention:** Use five quarters of the water.

**Situation:** Only one bounded supply is available.

**Result:** Needs a reference-unit explanation

Five quarters is mathematically meaningful but cannot be taken from one whole supply. Distinguish a reference unit from the entire available stock.

### O1 — Positions

**Celan:** va var shan vorkor «tren».

**Intention:** Go through door labelled three.

**Situation:** Doors have visible number labels.

**Result:** Strong contextual composition

The quoted number identifies the label; no ordinal suffix or new meaning is needed.

### O2 — Positions

**Celan:** va var shan vorkor «tren».

**Intention:** Go through the third door encountered.

**Situation:** Unlabelled doors; walking direction not stated.

**Result:** Does not establish a general ordinal

The label construction alone supplies neither counting direction nor a starting point.

### O3 — Positions

**Celan:** va var shan vorkor «tren».

**Intention:** Go through the third door counted from the agreed start.

**Situation:** Speaker and listener first number the doors together.

**Result:** Works with shared numbering

The earlier counting supplies the order. This is a contextual solution, not a universal ordinal rule.

### O4 — Positions

**Celan:** va tal emilpral «del» var I.

**Intention:** Give me plate labelled two.

**Situation:** Plates are numbered for a task.

**Result:** Strong contextual composition

Repeats the label strategy with another noun; does not mean give me two plates.

### B1 — Minimum amount

**Celan:** rinaen peth shen morlak worlian.

**Intention:** Five measures of grain are enough.

**Situation:** Preparing a meal with a shared measure.

**Result:** Supported compositional paraphrase

Worlian already appears as a predicate. This states sufficiency, not an exact minimum.

### B2 — Minimum amount

**Celan:** rinaen peth shen morlak worlian. ver rinaen quen shen morlak worlian.

**Intention:** Five measures suffice; four do not.

**Situation:** Only whole measures are offered; larger supplies are useful.

**Result:** Useful contextual workaround

Gives a practical threshold in this scenario. It does not resolve every quantity between four and five or independently promise that excess is acceptable.

### B3 — Minimum amount

**Celan:** peth reth ther peth

**Intention:** Five or more.

**Situation:** An amount expression.

**Result:** Promising but new numeric construction

Reth and Ther have the right semantic ingredients, but current Ther syntax compares a stated quality: X adjective Ther Y. A bare-number comparison must be explicitly established.

### B4 — Minimum amount

**Celan:** rinaen peth shen morlak worlian. ver rinaen quen shen morlak worlian.

**Intention:** The minimum is exactly five.

**Situation:** 4.5 measures are possible.

**Result:** Insufficient for an exact lower bound

The clauses leave 4.5 unsettled. Do not label this wording an exact at-least construction.

### E1 — Equal amount

**Celan:** va tal peth shen morlak var I.

**Intention:** Give me the same amount as the other recipient.

**Situation:** The other recipient received five of the same measure of grain.

**Result:** Works by repeating the known quantity

Numerical equality is communicated without a separate same word, provided the measure is shared.

### E2 — Equal amount

**Celan:** va tal unar shen morlak var Ya. va tal unar shen morlak var I.

**Intention:** Give us equal amounts.

**Situation:** One calibrated measure is used for both recipients.

**Result:** Works through matched instructions

Two equal unit amounts establish equality operationally, without Thaleth.

### E3 — Equal amount

**Celan:** va tal shen denya var I.

**Intention:** Give me the same amount as that.

**Situation:** The listener indicates a reference measure.

**Result:** Understandable but potentially ambiguous

May request the measure itself or a measured quantity matching it. Use a named material and explicit count when precision matters.

### E4 — Equal amount

**Celan:** va tal shenlian var I.

**Intention:** Give me the same amount.

**Situation:** No shared reference amount.

**Result:** Does not preserve the instruction reliably

Shenlian names a standard/reference measure, not equality to an unnamed earlier quantity.

### P1 — Price

**Celan:** rinaen peth shen morlak-ka belshen peth shena.

**Intention:** The total price of five measures of grain is five coins.

**Situation:** One agreed measure and one understood currency.

**Result:** Strong candidate composition

Read the possessor as the full phrase five measures of grain. No new price word is required; the phrase boundary should be taught explicitly.

### P2 — Price

**Celan:** rinaen unar shen morlak-ka belshen del shena.

**Intention:** One measure of grain costs two coins.

**Situation:** Same market convention.

**Result:** Strong candidate composition

Changes quantity and price independently, so the example does not depend on both numbers being five.

### P3 — Price

**Celan:** rinaen peth shen morlak-ka belshen peth shena.

**Intention:** Each of five measures costs five coins.

**Situation:** Seller intends a total of twenty-five coins.

**Result:** Wrong for this intention

The proposed wording states the price for the five-measure lot, not five coins per individual measure.

### P4 — Price

**Celan:** rinaen peth shen morlak-ka belshen peth shena.

**Intention:** A fully specified price independent of market context.

**Situation:** Different measuring vessels or currencies are possible.

**Result:** Needs the unit and currency clarified

Coin type and measure must be shared or specified. This is a missing reference, not necessarily missing grammar. Counted Shena versus Shenan remains a separate teaching decision.

## Recommended next decisions

1. Test the fraction construction with the explicit equal-share interpretation; start with zero through one whole and explain improper fractions separately.
2. Keep numbered-label expressions available without claiming they establish a full ordinal system.
3. Develop and test bare-number ther comparisons and coordinated equal-or-greater amounts.
4. Use matching counts for equality now; investigate a general equality construction only where the counts are unknown.
5. Review the price examples and whole-phrase possession as sentence constructions.

These tests do not authorize adding words, changing definitions, or publishing new learner rules.
