# Grammar Guide: complete question map of current app content

This maps the **current app Grammar Guide**, whose source is `dictionary/app_data/grammar_guide.csv`. It is a map for reviewing a possible redesign, not a change to the app or a claim that all Celan grammar has been documented. The source has seven main lessons, one Cultural Voice companion, and 40 distinct supporting grammar records. Some source rows repeat a lesson or companion page for display; they do not add another topic.

Each question below is a possible learner-facing lesson. The eight current areas can remain as navigation groups, but they are not enough as eight individual lesson cards. Sentence Shape in particular needs several separate questions. The bracketed IDs show which existing app records each question must carry. A record may appear under two questions when both are genuinely useful, but it should have one primary home in the eventual app.

## Coverage and design implications

- The map covers every one of the 40 distinct supporting grammar records in the current app source. The seven lesson rows and the Cultural Voice page are covered by the eight sections above. Their internal section headings are accounted for in the “Current material to carry” column; each lesson's “What to notice” material belongs in its lesson recaps, not a separate question.
- The current **Sentence Shape** section alone needs 12 learner questions to keep its existing content visible. An eight-card design would hide too much if each card were treated as a complete lesson.
- Pronunciation, number, and writing each currently include several related demonstrations within one source record. Those demonstrations should be divided across the questions above when the visual lessons are built; the record IDs are source links, not a requirement to display one giant block.
- This is a coverage map of **what the app currently teaches**, not proof that the Grammar Guide covers every rule in the Celan language or that every existing explanation is correct. Content review is a separate pass.

## Cross-check: grammar used elsewhere but not taught by these 40 supporting records

This second check compares the current guide with `data/grammar_rules_working.csv` and the app's current dictionary meanings and examples. These are **gaps in the current guide**, not extra material already covered by the 41 questions above.

| Area to review for the guide | Evidence elsewhere in the project | What is missing from the guide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pointing words and viewpoint | The working grammar has an approved `ED-0030` unit for `deni`, `denya`, and `denla`; all three are visible dictionary entries with examples. | They follow the noun and distinguish the speaker's sphere, the listener's sphere, and a referent away from both. None appears in `grammar_guide.csv`. |
| Left, right, center, and boundaries | `ED-0030` also gives rules for `naku`, `sora`, `kor`, `kaan`, entering, and exiting. The app dictionary contains these headwords and examples. | The guide teaches basic place prepositions but does not teach the viewpoint that makes left/right meaningful, the center/midpoint distinction, or the boundary-crossing family as a system. |
| More relation and time words | The dictionary has 18 visible preposition meanings; the guide explicitly introduces eight basic forms (`var`, `ser`, `thal`, `ka`, `esh`, `morl`, `an`, `nor`). | Ten additional listed prepositions, including `shan`, `serdel`, `shalvar`, `vethvar`, `shalnor`, `vethnor`, `varnor`, `naku`, `sora`, and `kaan`, lack a place in the current teaching map. This is an inventory gap; each need not become its own lesson. |
| Joining clauses | The current dictionary lists four conjunctions: `ser` (and), `reth` (or), `aen` (that), and `sernor` (while). The guide's conjunction record explains `ser` and `reth` only. | `Aen` introducing a subordinate clause and `sernor` connecting simultaneous events are not taught. The older `GR-V3-0002` source rule for subordinate `aen` is marked **Needs Human Review**, so its lesson wording should be checked before publication. |
| Hyphens and apostrophes | Approved editorial decision `ED-0001` distinguishes fused root compounds, grammatical hyphens, and preserved apostrophes. The current Writing Conventions lesson teaches capitals but has no apostrophe explanation. | How the written form shows tense, possession, honorifics, numbers, modal forms, and certain expressive or sacred terms needs a learner-facing explanation. |
| Short connectors and discourse markers | The dictionary includes forms such as `Vara` (then), `Thalka` (but), `Sera` (also), and `Tek` (only), plus temporal markers such as `Athnor` and `Nethnor`. The guide's conjunction lesson covers only `ser` and `reth`. | Learners lack a map for joining events, contrasting them, limiting a statement, and setting their timing. These may be grouped as a practical sentence-tools lesson rather than one card per word. |
| Colloquial omission and whole utterances | Approved `ED-0029` allows `Ilin-ka` and `Inko-ka` with an understood noun omitted, and allows some established particles/forms to act as whole utterances in context. | The guide's possession and sentence lessons do not explain this conversational use. It may belong in an advanced spoken-use lesson or in Expressions rather than the core beginner path. |
| Playful speech with grammatical consequences | The working grammar has four `ED-0031` units on laughter, joking, teasing, wordplay, and irony. The dictionary uses these words and sentences. | In particular, `dovkelaen` without a personal object means “joke,” while with a person as its direct object it means “tease that person”; `ser` can mark mutual joking. The rest is largely vocabulary and social usage, which may belong in Expressions rather than a core grammar lesson. |

The eight current areas therefore cover the **current Grammar Guide source**, but they do **not** cover every grammatical pattern the app already uses. A comprehensive redesign should resolve these gaps before the question list is treated as final.
