# Additional euphonic variant candidates — 29 October 2026

Status: **reviewed 30 October 2026**. The five strongest and eight additional forms below were approved and added to the Dictionary App as optional variants under ED-0051. The Wek and lower-priority groups were not adopted. Dedicated audio for the 13 new forms is in `audio/headwords/variants/volume_002/`; the user listened to and approved all 13 recordings on 2026-10-30.

## Basis

The current app lists 197 alternate forms under 193 headwords. I compared the remaining headwords' recorded root breakdowns against the app's Rule of Euphony and excluded forms already present as headwords or listed variants. A mechanical pass found 378 parseable consonant-joining compounds without a listed variant; that is a search pool, not a recommendation count. I then selected compounds where a vowel would separate a difficult root boundary, with strongest weight given to an already approved variant in the same family. Every proposed spelling below was checked against current headwords and listed variants; none collides exactly.

Current editorial policy makes smoothing optional, keeps the unsmoothed headword, and stores any approved smoother form as a searchable variant of the same entry. This audit does not infer new meanings, new headwords, or an automatic variant for every compound. Spoken review remains necessary, especially where the written cluster may sound better than it looks.

## Wek needs its own vowel choice

**Ravwek** and **Ndemwek** also have no listed variants. Their **v-w** and **m-w** joins may benefit from smoothing. Because **Wek** is the second root, the approved choices are **Ravewek / Ravowek** and **Ndemewek / Ndemowek**. The form with **-awek** is excluded by the existing rule. A listening comparison should decide whether either word needs one of these alternatives and, if so, which vowel works.

## Lower priority

**Fennilphel → Fennilaphel**, **Welkrez → Welakrez**, and **Zordren → Zoradren** are pronounceable enough in their current written forms that I would test them only after the stronger group. **Eshkrezaen → Eshakrezaen** is mechanically possible, but the existing editorial decision favored a compact cooking word; I would not add a longer form without a specific sound reason.
