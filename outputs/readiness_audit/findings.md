# Celan readiness audit: open findings

This is a read-only findings list. The current app and approved decisions control. No dictionary source row or app file has been changed in this audit. A candidate generated by a script is not a linguistic finding until its full sentence and approved senses are read.

## Confirmed inconsistencies

### R-01 — Gorm example says dust

Current: `zeraen I thal gorm.` → “I sneeze from dust.” (`PE-BIAOBF1-0065`)

`Gorm` is dirt or grime; the dictionary has `Felmor` for dust. This example appears as direct usage under Gorm and teaches the wrong English association. Recommended: keep the Gorm example and revise the English to a contextually accurate dirt/grime reading, or replace the full pair with an ordinary Gorm sentence. Decision needed: which situation was intended?

### R-02 — Tavan example reverses the participants and omits the stated action

Current: `Felka ser dren an Tavan.` → “The child is with the parent by the water.” (`PE-V3-0031`)

The Celan lacks a being verb and places water after `ser` and the parent after `an`; it does not express the stated relationships. Recommended: `rinaen felka ser tavan an dren.` with the same English. This uses the pattern already shown in the neighboring Tavan example. Decision needed: accept that rewording or supply an attested alternative.

### R-04 — Editorial text is visible as learner guidance

The Felka, Tavan, Velar, and Zhiren pages display “Retain as-is.” as a usage note. A fresh field scan found [26 app usage notes](presentation_notes.csv) with explicit editorial language, including “Preserved as standalone root/morpheme,” “source gloss,” and “current naming applied.” The Gorm page separately displays “Latent root promoted…” in its word-family area. Of the distinct examples placed as direct usage, 1,441 carry the raw label “Expansion example”; example cards also expose source IDs and internal review commentary. These are records of editorial work, not instructions for using Celan. Recommended: keep them in the underlying data and review export, and remove them from the primary learner view. Decision needed: whether the learner app should offer a separate, collapsed source-record view. The visual redesign itself remains for discussion with the user. The 26-note list is a targeted scan, not proof that every editorial phrase has been found.

### R-10 — Three recreation examples say “they” where Celan names a friend

| Example | Current wording |
|---|---|
| `PE-RC1-0030` | `shalnakaen ser jornak.` → “They win the race.” |
| `PE-RC1-0034` | `vethnakaen ser jornak.` → “They lose the race.” |
| `PE-RC1-0043` | `mahrshalilaen ser an mahr.` → “They surf on the sea.” |

All three appear as direct usage. `Ser` is a friend/ally as a noun, or “with/and” in other roles; it is not the third-person plural pronoun. The app has `Lalin` for plural “they.” The sentences therefore change who acts. Recommended: if plural actors were intended, change the subjects to `Lalin` and retain the English; if one friend was intended, correct the English instead. Decision needed: which participant was intended in these three examples?

### R-11 — Ecology examples add actions that the Celan does not state

| Example | Current pair | Missing or changed action |
|---|---|---|
| `PE-EGE1-0033` | `rinaen verdin morl verdmarin.` → “The plant grows beneath the tree.” | `Rinaen` says the plant is beneath the tree; it does not say it grows. |
| `PE-EGE1-0036` | `rinaen zhirverd esh verdxar.` → “The vine climbs across the tree bark.” | The Celan places the vine over the bark but gives no climbing action. |
| `PE-EGE1-0050` | `kes lorin dumanali an rathor ser rathnali an nor.` → “The scholar records past extinction and a presently endangered species.” | `Kes` means notices or registers in awareness, not writes a record. |

All three are direct app examples. A student following them could use a location sentence to report growth or climbing, or `Kes` to mean formal recordkeeping. Recommended: keep the intended English only where an existing action verb can express it; otherwise make the English accurately describe what the Celan says. Decision needed: whether the English action in each row is essential to the scene.

### R-12 — One ecology example uses “without/no” to negate a full clause

Current: `rinaen mbu shalrin; ka rinaen la verdin.` → “The fungus is an organism; it is not a plant.” (`PE-EGE1-0041`)

The app gives `Ka` the meanings “without” and the response “no,” while the current grammar puts `ver` immediately before the verb for sentence negation. The second Celan clause therefore does not teach the approved way to say “it is not a plant.” It appears as direct usage under six headwords, including Ka and Verdin. Recommended: `rinaen mbu shalrin; ver rinaen la verdin.` with the same English, after checking the surrounding ecological examples for similar negation. Decision needed: accept this grammar correction or identify a specific approved clausal `ka` rule.

### R-13 — An ecology example uses a noun as the cutting verb

Current: `pel pelnor nalior; jor xilnalior.` → “Erosion cuts the habitat; fragmentation surges.” (`PE-EGE1-0063`)

The app defines `Pel` as the noun sharpness or clarity, while `Pelaen` is the cutting verb. The second clause has the valid verb `Jor`, but the first has no approved cutting verb. It appears as direct usage under the linked words. Recommended: use an established cutting verb in the first clause while keeping the English, after checking whether that verb takes erosion as an agent. Decision needed: approve the repaired wording once its full construction is reviewed; no new `Pel` verb meaning is implied.

## Questions requiring clarification

### R-05 — Time markers need a connected rule

The working rule and earlier decision describe `an` as spatial and `nor` as temporal. Yet the user's approved `shalilaen Eshlorien an dren an Zhaelor.` means “The wave naming flows by the water in spring.” Older examples use `an` with dawn, noon, nightfall, and morning; another uses `thal` for “in the afternoon.” (`PE-V2-0001`, `0003`, `0004`, `0006`; `PE-TVO1-0045`; and related rows.) The screenshot's Weksharaen morning sentence may be an error or an intended time-span use of `an`. Recommended: determine whether seasons and other broad time spans permit `an`, and then review each time expression. Decision needed: the scope of temporal `an`, if any.

### R-06 — Rail means versus rail location

Current: `weksharaen mekral an zhirshara.` → “The mechanic commutes by rail.” (`PE-TVO1-0046`)

`An` locates something at or in a place, while the English states the means of travel. Prior decisions say rail travel can be expressed with the existing rail words but do not specify this exact construction. Recommended: review transport examples before changing the sentence. Decision needed: whether this wording is an attested route construction.

### R-09 — Multi-meaning words still need example-to-meaning review

The current app has 89 headwords with more than one displayed sense. It intentionally labels examples on many such pages “Sense assignment pending review,” and the current CSV exports leave examples blank on non-first sense rows until they are assigned. For example, `Lianeth` has distinct ability and reliability uses; a sentence showing “I can speak” cannot automatically prove “reliable.” This is an acknowledged open placement task, not an error in the approved meanings. Recommended: judge each multi-sense example against the particular sense it teaches and retain the pending label wherever evidence remains insufficient. Decision needed: none yet; present specific ambiguous assignments only after that review.
